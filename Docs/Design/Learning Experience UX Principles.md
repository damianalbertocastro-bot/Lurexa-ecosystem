# Learning Experience UX Principles

- Status: **Normative design baseline**
- Depends on: [Lurexa Learning Methodology](../Curriculum/00-LUREXA-LEARNING-METHODOLOGY.md)

## Design for a learning loop

Every lesson experience should make five things clear:

1. what the learner will be able to do;
2. what to do now;
3. why the feedback matters;
4. what evidence has been created;
5. what meaningful action follows.

A learner should never need to guess whether an activity is instruction, practice, assessment, optional challenge, or a technical failure.

## Interaction rules

- Show one primary learning action per screen or section.
- Chunk instructions and model an expected response before demanding production.
- Use retrieval and variation rather than superficial repetition.
- Give feedback close to the action and keep it specific to the objective.
- Preserve first attempts; do not make retries visually feel like cheating.
- Treat progress indicators as orientation tools, not pressure devices.
- Let learners pause, resume and recover from weak connectivity.
- Make audio/speech controls explicit: record, stop, play back, retry, submit and privacy state.
- Use corrections that protect confidence: acknowledge communicative success before prioritizing improvement.
- Ensure failure states name the problem and provide a next action.

## Assessment and mastery

Completion, correctness, confidence and mastery are different signals. The UI must not label a learner “mastered” from a single completion or hide hint/retry use. When recommendations are personalized, provide a short understandable rationale appropriate to the learner’s level.

## Cultural and linguistic relevance

Initial English content should be useful to Dominican and Spanish-speaking learners without reducing their lives to stereotypes. Contexts should expand from familiar local situations toward Caribbean, Latin American and global communication. Pronunciation UX focuses on intelligibility, not imitation of a single prestige accent.

## Accessibility and bandwidth

Do not make a rich learning experience depend on autoplay video, large images, hover-only interaction, a mouse, or uninterrupted connectivity. Offer transcripts/captions where relevant, compact assets, saved progress feedback and safe recovery after interruption.
