# Cultural Adaptation Framework

Status: Curriculum production specification
Owner: Lurexa Learning Technologies

## Principle
Lurexa teaches globally useful English through contexts that are familiar enough to reduce cognitive distance and broad enough to prepare learners for international communication.

The initial implementation is Dominican-first and Spanish-speaker-aware, not Dominican-only.

## Context progression
Personal -> Local -> Dominican -> Caribbean -> Latin American -> International -> Global/Abstract

## Separation rule
Every activity must separate:
- competency objective;
- linguistic target;
- cultural/context wrapper.

This allows the same competency to be taught through different contexts without fragmenting the Learner Model.

## Adaptation layers
### Layer 1 — Universal
Human experiences that translate broadly: identity, family, routines, work, food, travel, health, technology, culture, community, goals.

### Layer 2 — Dominican relevance
Use authentic but non-tokenized contexts such as:
- Santo Domingo, Santiago, Boca Chica and other recognizable locations;
- guagua/transportation contexts when explained appropriately;
- local food and service encounters;
- tourism and hospitality;
- family/community interaction;
- baseball, music, festivals and everyday urban life;
- Dominican professional and academic contexts.

### Layer 3 — Caribbean/Latin American comparison
Invite comparison rather than presenting Dominican culture as isolated.

### Layer 4 — International transfer
Use airports, hotels, remote work, international study, migration, global media, business, science and cross-cultural interaction.

## Topic title rule
Titles must connect hierarchically.
Example:
C1 — Language, Identity & Global Perspective
Module — Caribbean Heritage
Unit — Dominican Music and Cultural Fusion
Lesson — Dembow: Rhythm, Language and Identity
Activity — Hear the Beat: Stress and Meaning

## Representation rules
- Avoid caricatures, forced slang and stereotypical assumptions.
- Do not assume all Dominicans share identical social class, region, interests or speech habits.
- Use culturally authentic names and situations while preserving diversity.
- Distinguish Dominican Spanish transfer tendencies from individual learner evidence.
- When a local term is used, provide enough context for broader Spanish-speaking learners.

## Linguistic transfer rule
Culture and L1 adaptation may influence examples, explanation and remediation, but individualized evidence overrides general population assumptions.

## Content balance
Across a level, target an approximate progression:
- A1: 60% personal/local/Dominican, 25% Caribbean/Latin American, 15% international.
- A2: 45% local/Dominican, 30% Caribbean/Latin American, 25% international.
- B1: 30% local/Dominican, 30% regional, 40% international/global.
- B2: 20% local/Dominican, 25% regional, 55% international/global.
- C1-C2: culturally flexible; local identity remains available while global/abstract contexts dominate.
These are curriculum planning targets, not per-lesson quotas.

## Localization contract
Each lesson should be able to declare:
- `contextRegion`;
- `culturalAnchors`;
- `adaptableContext: true|false`;
- `alternateContexts` when useful;
- `l1SupportProfile` when language-transfer support exists.

## Teacher adaptation
Teachers may adapt a scenario to learner context if they preserve:
1. competency target;
2. language target;
3. task difficulty;
4. assessment criteria.

## AI adaptation
AI may personalize examples using authorized learner context, but must not change the competency or silently make tasks easier/harder beyond allowed adaptation rules.

## Success criterion
A Dominican learner should recognize themselves in the course without feeling confined to the Dominican Republic, and a non-Dominican Spanish speaker should still be able to learn successfully from the same curriculum.
