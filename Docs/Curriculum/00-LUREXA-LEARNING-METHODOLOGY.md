# Lurexa Learning Methodology

Status: Authoritative pedagogical source of truth for MVP  
Owner: Lurexa Learning Technologies  
Initial implementation: English language learning  
Designed for future extension: additional languages and subjects

## 1. Purpose

This document defines how Lurexa teaches. It governs lesson design, learning progression, AI tutoring, human-teacher use, feedback, assessment, adaptive practice, creation tasks, and the educational use of persistent learner memory.

Lurexa is not a digital textbook. It is a learning system that transforms supported exposure into independent performance.

## 2. Universal methodology

The domain-neutral Lurexa learning cycle is:

**Encounter → Understand → Practice → Retrieve → Apply → Create → Receive Feedback → Improve → Transfer → Retain**

This cycle must remain reusable for future languages, mathematics, science, teacher development, technical learning, and other domains.

Technology serves this cycle. Technology does not define it.

## 3. Governing principles

### Communication and performance over passive completion

Completion is not mastery. Watching, reading, clicking, or finishing an activity does not by itself prove learning.

### Lurexa remembers learning

Relevant authorized learning evidence should influence future learning. Lurexa should not repeatedly rediscover what a learner already knows, struggles with, or has recently improved.

> **One learner. One evolving model. Every Lurexa experience adapts around it.**

### Prior knowledge is respected

Learners can place into an appropriate level, challenge material, and skip competencies they can reliably demonstrate.

### Personalization preserves curriculum integrity

AI may adapt examples, support, review, scaffolding, difficulty, and practice frequency. It must not destroy a coherent progression or silently invent new proficiency standards.

### Human and AI roles are complementary

AI provides high-frequency practice, immediate feedback, repetition, adaptive support, scenario rehearsal, and pattern detection. Human teachers provide judgment, motivation, nuanced evaluation, intervention, cultural discussion, coaching, and high-value assessment.

### Cultural relevance without cultural confinement

The initial English program is optimized for Spanish speakers, especially Dominican learners, while preparing them for international English use. Context should expand from personal/local to Dominican, Caribbean, Latin American, international, and global/abstract topics.

### Accent erasure is not a goal

Phonetics and pronunciation prioritize intelligibility, communicative confidence, naturalness, flexibility, listening perception, rhythm, stress, and appropriate delivery.

## 4. Universal curriculum hierarchy

Domain → Framework/Standard → Level → Course → Module → Unit → Lesson → Activity → Learning Evidence → Mastery

For English, CEFR is the proficiency framework. CEFR must not be hard-coded as the platform-wide learning architecture.

## 5. English skill system

English learning explicitly develops seven skills:

1. Listening
2. Speaking
3. Reading
4. Writing
5. Vocabulary
6. Grammar
7. Phonetics

Two integrated performance modes combine these skills:

- **Conversation**: speaking + listening + vocabulary retrieval + grammar retrieval + phonetics + pragmatics + interaction.
- **Create & Apply**: meaningful products or performances such as recordings, presentations, interviews, podcasts, reports, roleplays, projects, debates, maps, itineraries, cultural commentaries, and practical simulations.

## 6. Skill priority by CEFR level

Skill emphasis changes with proficiency. Lurexa must not reuse one lesson formula with only harder vocabulary.

### A1 — Foundations for Real Communication

Primary: listening, vocabulary, phonetics, controlled/guided speaking.  
Secondary: foundational grammar and reading.  
Supporting: short guided writing.

Learners need high scaffolding, short chunks, repeated retrieval, clear audio, visual context, and survival communication strategies.

### A2 — Everyday Independence

Primary: listening, speaking, vocabulary retrieval, everyday interaction.  
Growing: reading, functional grammar, short connected writing, connected-speech awareness.

### B1 — Independent Communication

Primary: interaction, speaking, listening, fluency, functional vocabulary.  
Growing: connected reading/writing, discourse, pragmatic control, self-correction.

### B2 — Flexible Communication

Primary: spontaneous speaking, discourse, authentic/semi-authentic listening, reading, argumentation, professional/academic communication.

### C1 — Precision and Sophistication

Primary: extended speaking, nuanced listening, sophisticated reading/writing, register, lexical precision, rhetorical control, advanced interaction.

### C2 — Adaptive Mastery

Primary: nuance, synthesis, register shifting, domain-sensitive communication, spontaneous extended production, interpretation of varied accents and discourse styles.

## 7. Standard lesson cycle

A standard Lurexa lesson should normally contain:

1. Hook
2. Lesson Mission
3. Vocabulary Builder
4. Contextual Input
5. Comprehension
6. Language Noticing
7. Grammar and/or Phonetics Focus
8. Guided Practice
9. Conversation
10. Create & Apply
11. Review
12. Quiz
13. Reflection
14. Learning Evidence

Not every lesson requires identical timing or screens. The UX should vary while preserving the pedagogical logic.

## 8. Lesson mission

Every lesson must define a learner-readable communicative or performance outcome.

Prefer: “By the end of this lesson, you can ask and answer basic questions about where someone is from.”

Avoid objectives that only state a grammar label.

## 9. Vocabulary methodology

Every English lesson must contain vocabulary development relevant to the lesson objective.

Vocabulary learning should progress through meaningful stages such as:

**See → Hear → Recognize → Pronounce → Retrieve → Use → Recall Later**

Items may include word/phrase, contextual meaning, audio, stress, collocations, example, image, and productive use.

Static front/back flashcards are not the default experience. Use interactive vocabulary cards whose behavior changes based on mastery evidence.

## 10. Listening methodology

Listening should train listening processes, not only post-audio multiple-choice performance.

Recommended sequence:

1. Prepare/predict
2. First listen for global meaning
3. Second listen for specific information
4. Micro-listening for difficult sound/connected-speech features
5. Listen again
6. Listening-to-speaking transfer

At higher levels, increase natural speed, speaker variety, inference, accent exposure, density, abstraction, and discourse complexity.

## 11. Speaking methodology

Speaking develops through:

**Controlled → Guided → Interactive → Independent**

Speaking feedback prioritizes:

1. Task achievement
2. Comprehensibility
3. Meaning-blocking errors
4. Target-language control
5. Pronunciation/intelligibility
6. Highest-value next improvement

Do not correct every error immediately.

## 12. Conversation methodology

Conversation is required across units because students must use what they learn.

Conversation may occur as:

- learner ↔ AI
- learner ↔ teacher
- learner ↔ learner
- learner ↔ group

A unit should normally include:

**Guided Conversation → Scenario Conversation → Increasingly Independent Conversation**

AI conversations should be scenario-driven, level-controlled, goal-aware, and connected to current learning rather than generic “chat in English” sessions.

## 13. Phonetics methodology

Phonetics is the seventh explicit skill and should be shared conceptually with Lurexa Coach.

Progression:

- A1: sound awareness, high-value contrasts, final consonants, basic clusters, word stress, basic sentence stress, intelligibility.
- A2: clusters, linking, sentence stress, reductions, perception-production connection.
- B1: rhythm, weak forms, connected speech, intelligibility during spontaneous speech.
- B2: discourse chunking, contrastive stress, pacing, natural interaction.
- C1: pragmatic/rhetorical intonation, nuance, professional delivery.
- C2: register-sensitive delivery, accent variation comprehension, advanced pacing, emphasis and intonation control.

Learner-specific evidence overrides generalized L1 assumptions.

## 14. Grammar methodology

Use:

**Context → Notice → Meaning → Form → Controlled Practice → Guided Communication → Free Communication → Retrieval**

Grammar supports communication. It is not the final learning objective.

## 15. Reading methodology

Reading develops prediction, skimming, scanning, main-idea identification, evidence location, inference, reference, attitude, argument analysis, synthesis, and source evaluation as level permits.

## 16. Writing methodology

Writing should increasingly follow:

**Analyze → Plan → Draft → Feedback → Revise → Submit → Reflect**

AI may support ideation, organization, feedback, and explanation. It should not automatically replace the learner’s productive work.

## 17. Create & Apply

Students should regularly make, explain, perform, design, solve, present, investigate, or produce something with what they learned.

Examples:

- A1: self-introduction, family profile, neighborhood map, routine recording.
- A2: itinerary, restaurant simulation, short video/message.
- B1: interview, podcast, social campaign, opinion presentation.
- B2: proposal, negotiation, structured debate, professional presentation.
- C1/C2: research brief, cultural analysis, expert panel, capstone, advanced professional artifact.

AI may suggest or generate tasks. Teachers may accept, modify, regenerate, assign, and assess them.

## 18. Human-teacher methodology

Live teacher time should not simply reteach asynchronous content.

Async learning handles exposure, explanation, interactive practice, AI tutoring, preliminary conversation, and formative feedback.

Human sessions emphasize:

**Retrieve → Interact → Apply → Create → Receive Human Feedback**

Teachers should receive useful learner context before sessions where authorized, including current target, strengths, recurring difficulties, suggested focus, and relevant evidence.

## 19. Learning delivery options

The same curriculum may support:

- **Flex**: primarily asynchronous; limited or optional human interaction; compatible with free/low-cost access and slower completion.
- **Guided**: asynchronous core + approximately one teacher meeting weekly; recommended default where available.
- **Intensive**: higher async load + approximately two teacher meetings weekly; greater speaking/feedback frequency.

A 54-week learning cycle may be offered as a program structure, not a promise that a learner moves from A1 to C2 in one year.

Recommended annual structure:

- 48 instructional weeks
- 4 consolidation/assessment weeks
- 2 flexible/recovery weeks

Progression follows demonstrated competence, not calendar completion alone.

## 20. Free-tier principle

The free tier must preserve sound pedagogy. Paid tiers may change access, pace, AI limits, teacher access, reporting, personalization depth, and convenience; they must not deliberately provide inferior instructional methodology.

Free users should receive placement, valid learning experiences, interactive vocabulary, meaningful review, and an appropriate starting path. A learner who places at B1 must not be forced into A1 because A1 is the most available free content.

## 21. Mastery and memory

Recommended learning-state progression:

**Encountered → Practiced → Demonstrated → Mastered → Retained**

A previously mastered competency may later become **Needs Revalidation** if evidence suggests decay.

Stored learner evidence is useful only if it changes future instruction. Lurexa should use relevant evidence to decide what to review, skip, reinforce, scaffold, challenge, recommend, or surface to a teacher.

## 22. Evidence versus interpretation

Follow `Docs/Architecture/Learner Model Architecture.md`.

Evidence includes observable events such as assessment responses, speaking performances, teacher observations, pronunciation attempts, retries, successful retrieval, and writing artifacts.

Interpretation includes likely mastery, recurring error patterns, recommended targets, inferred proficiency, and suggested interventions.

Do not silently overwrite evidence with AI conclusions.

## 23. Cultural and L1 adaptation

The current English implementation should use familiar Dominican and Latin contexts while progressively broadening learner exposure.

Separate stable learning objectives from adaptable contexts. For example, “express and support an opinion” can be practiced through Dominican music, Caribbean tourism, workplace technology, environmental policy, or international media.

English-specific linguistic transfer knowledge must live in extensible language/L1 profiles rather than be hard-coded into the universal methodology.

## 24. Activity UX principle

Asynchronous learning should feel like doing, not scrolling through a PDF.

Favor short interaction cycles:

**Input → Interaction → Feedback → Production**

Learners should rarely receive long passive sequences without meaningful interaction.

## 25. Productive struggle

Do not reveal answers immediately when a learner hesitates. Use graduated support where appropriate:

Retry → small hint → larger hint → example → explanation → answer.

## 26. Error-treatment rule

Classify errors by pedagogical priority:

- communication-critical: address promptly;
- target-language: normally address;
- developmental: address selectively;
- non-priority: often defer.

## 27. Domain extensibility

Future domains must define their own Domain Learning Profile containing:

- proficiency/standard framework;
- competencies;
- skill taxonomy;
- teaching strategies;
- activity types;
- assessment methods;
- progression rules;
- mastery criteria;
- AI tutor behavior;
- teacher role.

Do not force language-specific skills onto non-language subjects.

## 28. Non-negotiable Lurexa learning principles

1. Lurexa remembers.
2. Lurexa adapts.
3. Lurexa verifies mastery.
4. Lurexa respects prior knowledge.
5. Lurexa connects AI, teachers, curriculum and evidence.
6. Lurexa develops usable performance.
7. Lurexa remains culturally adaptable.
8. Lurexa uses AI to strengthen pedagogy, not replace it.
9. Lurexa grows with the learner.
10. The longer a learner uses Lurexa, the less the ecosystem should need to rediscover them.

## 29. Definition of success

A successful Lurexa course does not merely produce completed lessons. It produces learners who can independently perform meaningful tasks they previously could not perform.

> **Lurexa teaches by progressively transforming supported exposure into independent performance, remembering the evidence, and adapting what comes next.**
