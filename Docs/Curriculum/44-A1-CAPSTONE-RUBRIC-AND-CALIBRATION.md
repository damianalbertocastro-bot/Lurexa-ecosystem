# A1 Capstone Rubric and Calibration Protocol

Status: Authoritative pilot rubric for `My Life, My English`  
Applies to: A1 capstone teacher review, future calibrated AI assistance, representative A1 pilot

## 1. Purpose

This rubric makes A1 capstone judgments consistent enough to support pilot calibration. It does not turn A1 into a single numerical test.

Reviewers judge whether the learner demonstrates **functional A1 performance with the expected level of support**. The question is not whether the learner is error-free. The question is whether the learner can communicate the required meaning reliably enough for the A1 task.

Allowed judgments:

- `meets` — the artifact provides defensible evidence for this requirement;
- `not_yet` — the artifact shows relevant learning but not sufficient independent A1 performance;
- `inconclusive` — the artifact does not provide enough information to decide.

A reviewer must provide a rationale and confidence value. Confidence is about the evidence/judgment, not the learner's worth or motivation.

## 2. General A1 performance standard

At A1, a learner may:

- use short/simple sentences and memorized chunks;
- pause and self-repair;
- make grammar/pronunciation errors that do not prevent the target meaning;
- ask for repetition or clarification;
- rely on familiar vocabulary;
- need contextual support.

A1 `meets` does **not** require:

- native-like pronunciation;
- complex grammar;
- fast speech;
- spontaneous extended discourse;
- perfect spelling;
- zero teacher/AI support across the course.

For capstone evidence, however, the specific performance being judged must be sufficiently independent. A model copied line-for-line or an answer supplied by AI is not independent evidence.

## 3. Requirement rubrics

### A. Listening — understand key details in familiar spoken language

Requirement: `a1-capstone-listening`

Target competencies include:

- `EN.A1.LISTEN.KEY_DETAILS`
- `EN.A1.LISTEN.PREDICTABLE_EXCHANGES`

`meets` when the learner can recover the essential practical meaning and required key detail(s) from a short, clear, familiar A1 input without being shown the answer first.

Evidence indicators:

- identifies the correct time/place/name/price or equivalent requested detail;
- understands enough of the exchange to choose or state the appropriate next action;
- a replay may be allowed when the task design permits it, but repeated answer-revealing scaffolds reduce independence.

`not_yet` when the learner consistently misses communication-critical details, relies on visible transcript/answer cues, or responds to a substantially different meaning.

`inconclusive` when the artifact proves only that audio was played, not what the learner understood.

### B. Speaking and interaction — sustain a short supported exchange

Requirement: `a1-capstone-speaking-interaction`

Target competencies include:

- `EN.A1.SPEAK.INTRODUCE_SELF`
- `EN.A1.CONV.SHORT_SUPPORTED_CONVERSATION`
- `EN.A1.CONV.ASK_ANSWER_BASIC_QUESTIONS`

`meets` when the learner can contribute enough meaningful language to sustain the A1 scenario rather than only repeat the tutor's words.

Evidence indicators:

- provides personally/relevantly appropriate answers;
- asks or initiates at least one simple question/turn when required;
- responds to a follow-up;
- maintains the communicative goal for the minimum scenario length;
- errors may occur, but meaning remains recoverable.

`not_yet` when responses are mostly copied, unrelated, one-word where the task requires more, or the learner cannot maintain the exchange even with normal A1 interaction support.

`inconclusive` when the transcript is too short, interrupted, or dominated by system/tutor language.

### C. Reading and writing — use functional written English

Requirement: `a1-capstone-reading-writing`

Target competencies include:

- `EN.A1.READ.FUNCTIONAL_INFORMATION`
- `EN.A1.WRITE.BASIC_MESSAGE`
- `EN.A1.WRITE.PERSONAL_SENTENCES`

`meets` when the learner identifies the essential information in a short familiar text and produces a short message that communicates the required personal/practical information.

Evidence indicators:

- required facts/actions from the source are correctly understood;
- written message includes the key requested information;
- basic sentence/chunk meaning is clear despite expected A1 form errors;
- wording is learner-produced rather than copied from a model.

`not_yet` when critical information is missing/wrong, the response cannot be understood, or the output is predominantly copied.

`inconclusive` when only a submission/completion event exists without reviewable text or task outcome.

### D. Pronunciation — produce core phrases intelligibly

Requirement: `a1-capstone-pronunciation`

Target competencies include:

- `EN.A1.PHON.INTELLIGIBLE_CORE_PHRASES`
- `EN.A1.PHON.WORD_STRESS`
- `EN.A1.PHON.BASIC_INTONATION`

The standard is **intelligibility**, not accent reduction.

`meets` when the listener can understand the intended core phrase/message without repeated reconstruction caused by pronunciation.

Evidence indicators:

- high-value words are recognizable;
- word stress/rhythm/intonation supports rather than routinely blocks meaning;
- familiar transfer features may remain when they do not undermine intelligibility;
- learner may self-correct.

`not_yet` when recurring pronunciation features repeatedly obscure the communication-critical words or require the listener to guess the intended phrase.

`inconclusive` when audio quality is poor, recording is too short, or the target phrase cannot be identified.

Never use this criterion to reward imitation of a prestige/native accent.

### E. Mediation and online interaction — relay basic information

Requirement: `a1-capstone-mediation-online`

Target competencies include:

- `EN.A1.MED.RELAY_PERSONAL_DETAIL`
- `EN.A1.ONLINE.SHARE_BASIC_INFORMATION`

`meets` when the learner successfully relays the essential requested detail to another person or short digital exchange.

Evidence indicators:

- preserves the key name/time/place/price/request accurately;
- message is understandable for the intended recipient;
- can use simple phrases and contextual support.

`not_yet` when the relayed detail changes in a communication-critical way or the recipient cannot determine the required information.

`inconclusive` when there is no evidence that the information was actually relayed.

### F. Transfer — use A1 language in a changed practical situation

Requirement: `a1-capstone-transfer`

Target competencies include:

- `EN.A1.SPEAK.BASIC_TRANSACTION`
- `EN.A1.CONV.REQUEST_CLARIFICATION`
- `EN.A1.STRAT.ASK_REPEAT`

`meets` when the learner can use familiar A1 resources in a scenario that is not a verbatim repetition of the original lesson.

Evidence indicators:

- completes the practical goal;
- adapts a known chunk to the new detail/situation;
- uses clarification/repetition when needed rather than abandoning the interaction;
- can respond to one simple unexpected but level-appropriate change.

`not_yet` when success depends on repeating the original model exactly or the learner cannot respond when one detail changes.

`inconclusive` when the task is too similar to the training example to demonstrate transfer.

### G. Reflection — identify demonstrated capability and a next target

Requirement: `a1-capstone-reflection`

Target competency includes:

- `EN.A1.STRAT.USE_CHUNKS`

`meets` when the learner can identify at least one thing they can now do in English and one realistic next target using supported/simple reflection language.

This requirement is supportive rather than a critical exit gate; weak metalinguistic reflection should not erase otherwise strong A1 communicative evidence.

## 4. Independence and support coding

For calibration, reviewers distinguish:

- `independent` — learner produces/chooses meaning without answer-revealing support;
- `light_support` — repetition, neutral clarification, task reminder, or non-answering prompt;
- `scaffolded` — sentence frame, constrained options or corrective cue materially supports the response;
- `modeled` — target answer/phrase is provided before reproduction.

Strong level-exit evidence normally needs `independent` or appropriately light-supported performance. `scaffolded` and `modeled` attempts remain valuable learning evidence but should not be silently reclassified as independent.

## 5. Confidence anchors

Suggested reviewer confidence:

- `0.90–1.00` — clear representative artifact; decision is highly stable;
- `0.75–0.89` — good evidence with minor ambiguity;
- `0.60–0.74` — usable but limited evidence; consider another artifact;
- `0.50–0.59` — weak/incomplete basis; `inconclusive` is usually safer.

The UI should not accept less than `0.50` for a human capstone validation because very low-confidence evidence should be treated as insufficient rather than converted into a strong judgment.

## 6. Calibration protocol

Before commercial A1 exit decisions:

1. select at least 12–20 anonymized/reviewable A1 artifacts across speaking, writing, interaction and pronunciation;
2. have at least two qualified reviewers independently judge a useful subset;
3. record judgment, rationale and confidence;
4. compare agreement by requirement;
5. discuss disagreements using the rubric rather than learner identity;
6. refine examples/wording where disagreement reveals ambiguity;
7. create anchor examples for `meets`, `not_yet` and `inconclusive`;
8. repeat after material curriculum changes.

For an early small pilot, the sample is for **calibration**, not a population-valid psychometric claim.

## 7. AI-assistance calibration rule

A future Mind evaluator may propose a capstone judgment, but during calibration:

- AI and human judgments remain distinguishable;
- AI confidence is recorded;
- disagreements are reviewed;
- AI must not independently award CEFR advancement merely because output resembles a model;
- pronunciation scoring requires validated speech analysis, not transcript-only inference;
- AI should be permitted to return `inconclusive`.

Human-reviewed pilot evidence becomes a calibration set for later bounded automation.

## 8. A1 exit interpretation rule

`READY` requires the critical integrated requirements to have sufficient qualified independent evidence. One strong artifact may contribute to more than one requirement only when the artifact genuinely demonstrates each competency family; the review is recorded separately per requirement.

`READY_WITH_TARGETS` means the critical A1 profile is supported while non-critical targets remain for continued retrieval/growth.

`TARGETED_REVALIDATION` means most of the A1 profile is supported but one or more critical requirements need fresh independent evidence.

`NOT_YET_READY` means the current qualified evidence is too incomplete across critical requirements for an A1 exit decision.

`MORE_EVIDENCE_NEEDED` is used when there is not yet enough evidence to interpret the profile responsibly.

## 9. Anti-patterns

Do not:

- average all skills into one percentage and hide a critical missing skill;
- fail a learner for a non-native accent;
- pass pronunciation because a recording exists;
- pass speaking because a transcript is grammatically good if the interaction was heavily modeled;
- pass writing because a response was submitted;
- count AI-produced wording as learner evidence;
- let one weak artifact erase stronger later independent evidence;
- let one strong memorized artifact establish the whole level;
- require full A1 repetition when targeted revalidation is sufficient.
