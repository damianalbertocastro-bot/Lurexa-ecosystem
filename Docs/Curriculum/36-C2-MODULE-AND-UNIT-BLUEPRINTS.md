# English C2 Module and Unit Blueprints — Adaptive Mastery

Status: Production curriculum-development authority
Depends on: `11-ENGLISH-PROGRAM-MAP-A1-C2.md`, `24-CURRICULUM-COVERAGE-AND-QA-GATES.md`, `27-C2-COMPETENCY-MATRIX.md`

## 1. Level design principle

C2 is not a collection of rare vocabulary or grammar points. It is adaptive performance across demanding, unfamiliar and shifting communicative conditions.

C2 should require learners to:

- interpret subtle implication, irony, ambiguity and rhetorical positioning;
- synthesize specialized and competing sources;
- enter unfamiliar domains strategically;
- shift register, style, role and rhetorical distance;
- mediate expert ideas for different audiences;
- facilitate high-complexity discussion;
- manage incomplete evidence and uncertainty;
- improvise extended production;
- demonstrate transfer across domains rather than rehearsed topic expertise.

Support should normally be strategic and optional. Assessed work should contain substantial unscaffolded performance.

# Module 1 — Language, Meaning and Subtlety

## Module mission

Interpret and produce subtle meaning involving implication, ambiguity, connotation, humor, irony, stylistic effect and pragmatic subtext.

Core competencies:

- `EN.C2.LISTEN.SUBTLE_IMPLIED_MEANING`
- `EN.C2.LISTEN.HUMOR_IRONY_AMBIGUITY`
- `EN.C2.READ.INTERPRET_SUBTLE_STYLE_EFFECT`
- `EN.C2.READ.INTERPRET_AMBIGUITY_IRONY`
- `EN.C2.VOCAB.CONNOTATION_NUANCE`
- `EN.C2.VOCAB.METAPHOR_FIGURATIVE_INTERPRETATION`
- `EN.C2.PRAG.MANAGE_SUBTEXT`
- `EN.C2.PRAG.DEPLOY_HUMOR_IRONY_APPROPRIATELY`

## Unit 1.1 — What Is Said, What Is Meant

Performance: reconstruct implied meaning and justify interpretation from linguistic/contextual evidence.

Evidence:

- subtle listening interpretation;
- competing inference analysis;
- confidence/uncertainty statement;
- reformulation of implied meaning for a different audience.

## Unit 1.2 — Ambiguity, Humor and Irony

Performance: identify plausible interpretations and explain how context, relationship, tone and shared knowledge affect meaning.

Coach hook: high-context conversation where Coach changes tone/subtext and learner must recognize or repair interpretation.

## Unit 1.3 — Style as Meaning

Performance: explain and deliberately alter stylistic effect without changing core propositional content.

Create & Apply: multi-version text/audio demonstrating controlled shifts in tone, distance or rhetorical effect.

Teacher extension: close analysis and performance workshop emphasizing interpretive evidence rather than intuition alone.

# Module 2 — Advanced Rhetoric and Argument

## Module mission

Construct, challenge and reshape sophisticated arguments for different audiences while controlling rhetoric, evidence, stance and style.

Core competencies:

- `EN.C2.SPEAK.ARGUE_WITH_RHETORICAL_CONTROL`
- `EN.C2.SPEAK.REFRAME_ARGUMENT_STRATEGICALLY`
- `EN.C2.READ.EVALUATE_COMPETING_ARGUMENTS`
- `EN.C2.READ.ANALYZE_RHETORICAL_POSITIONING`
- `EN.C2.WRITE.SOPHISTICATED_ARGUMENT`
- `EN.C2.WRITE.REVISE_FOR_RHETORICAL_EFFECT`
- `EN.C2.GRAMMAR.RHETORICAL_GRAMMAR_CHOICE`
- `EN.C2.PHON.RHETORICAL_INTONATION_CONTROL`

## Unit 2.1 — Architecture of Sophisticated Arguments

Performance: trace claim structure, assumptions, evidence, concessions, counterargument and rhetorical strategy.

Evidence:

- argument reconstruction;
- rhetorical critique;
- identification of hidden premises;
- alternative framing.

## Unit 2.2 — Rhetorical Transformation

Performance: reshape the same argument for different audiences/purposes without distorting evidence.

Evidence:

- public vs expert transformation;
- spoken vs written transformation;
- register/rhetorical rationale;
- response to critique.

## Unit 2.3 — Advanced Debate and Editorial Performance

Performance: argue spontaneously, answer sophisticated counterarguments and revise a position when warranted.

Create & Apply: keynote/editorial/advanced debate.

Coach hook: adversarial-but-constructive interlocutor that tests logic, evidence and rhetorical flexibility.

# Module 3 — Expert Mediation and Facilitation

## Module mission

Facilitate understanding among people, perspectives and domains by compressing, expanding, reframing and negotiating complex meaning.

Core competencies:

- `EN.C2.SPEAK.FACILITATE_HIGH_COMPLEXITY_DISCUSSION`
- `EN.C2.CONV.MODERATE_EXPERT_DISCUSSION`
- `EN.C2.CONV.RECONCILE_COMPETING_PERSPECTIVES`
- `EN.C2.MED.MEDIATE_EXPERT_IDEAS`
- `EN.C2.MED.FACILITATE_CONFLICTING_EXPERT_PERSPECTIVES`
- `EN.C2.MED.COMPRESS_EXPAND_INFORMATION`
- `EN.C2.MED.BUILD_SHARED_CONCEPTUAL_GROUND`

## Unit 3.1 — Expert to Non-Specialist

Performance: explain specialized information accurately to a different audience while preserving essential nuance.

Evidence:

- expert source comprehension;
- audience transformation;
- clarification exchange;
- explanation of simplification choices/limits.

## Unit 3.2 — Facilitate Competing Expertise

Performance: identify conceptual differences, reframe them and build enough common ground for productive discussion.

Evidence:

- multiparty facilitation;
- neutral synthesis;
- conflict-point reframing;
- shared-ground statement.

## Unit 3.3 — Mediate Ambiguity and Uncertainty

Performance: help participants reason when evidence, definitions or outcomes remain uncertain.

Create & Apply: expert mediation artifact + facilitated exchange.

Coach hook: cross-domain expert simulation where learner must ask domain-entry questions rather than pretend knowledge.

Teacher extension: observed mediation with explicit scoring for accuracy, neutrality, audience adaptation and facilitation.

Shift effectively among technical, academic, public, interpersonal and creative registers while handling unfamiliar-domain demands strategically.

## Unit 4.1 — Entering an Unfamiliar Domain

Performance: establish enough conceptual/terminological footing to communicate responsibly without faking expertise.

Evidence:

- strategic questioning;
- terminology inference;
- clarification/paraphrase;
- explanation of knowledge limits.

## Unit 4.2 — Register Transformation

Performance: transform content across technical, executive, public and interpersonal registers.

Evidence:

- multi-audience written transformation;
- oral delivery variation;
- rhetorical/style justification;
- feedback-led revision.

## Unit 4.3 — High-Stakes Professional Communication

Performance: communicate under pressure where precision, diplomacy and strategic framing matter.

Scenarios:

- executive recommendation;
- crisis/update communication;
- difficult negotiation;
- expert briefing;
- public response.

Create & Apply: high-stakes communication portfolio.

Coach hook: role/register shifts during one continuous scenario.

## Module mission

Core competencies:

- `EN.C2.LISTEN.RECONSTRUCT_ARGUMENT_UNDER_UNCERTAINTY`
- `EN.C2.SPEAK.MANAGE_AMBIGUITY_AND_UNCERTAINTY`

- `EN.C2.MED.MEDIATE_AMBIGUITY_UNCERTAINTY`
- `EN.C2.ONLINE.MANAGE_CONFLICT_AND_ALIGNMENT`

## Unit 6.1 — Complex Systems

Performance: explain relationships, feedback loops, emergent effects and trade-offs without oversimplification.

## Unit 6.2 — Incomplete Evidence

Performance: distinguish what is known, plausible, disputed and unknown and adjust conclusions appropriately.

Evidence:

- uncertainty reconstruction;
- decision under incomplete evidence;
- confidence calibration;
- argument revision when new evidence appears.

## Unit 6.3 — Competing Values and Decisions

Performance: facilitate/argue through decisions where no single criterion resolves the issue.

Create & Apply: systems analysis + high-complexity decision forum.

Coach hook: new evidence arrives mid-conversation and learner must revise position transparently.

Teacher extension: deliberative forum emphasizing reasoning quality and uncertainty management.

# Module 7 — Independent Mastery Project

## Module mission

Demonstrate self-directed language performance in a learner-selected domain with an authentic/realistic audience and purpose.

Core competencies:

- `EN.C2.CREATE.INDEPENDENT_MASTERY_PROJECT`
- `EN.C2.READ.TRANSFER_ACROSS_DOMAINS`
- `EN.C2.SPEAK.RESPOND_TO_UNFAMILIAR_DOMAIN`
- `EN.C2.WRITE.COMPLEX_SYNTHESIS`
- `EN.C2.MED.CROSS_DOMAIN_REFRAME`
- `EN.C2.STRAT.REFRAME_FOR_MULTIPLE_AUDIENCES`

## Unit 7.1 — Define the Domain and Purpose

Learner defines:

- question/problem;
- audience;
- purpose;
- relevant sources;
- expected genres/registers;
- evidence of successful communication.

AI may challenge scope and assumptions but must not define the final project for the learner.

## Unit 7.2 — Research, Create and Iterate

Evidence:

- source synthesis;
- domain-language strategy;
- substantial draft/artifact;
- expert/public audience transformation;
- revision trail.

## Unit 7.3 — Present, Defend and Transfer

Learner presents/defends work, handles unfamiliar questions and reframes key ideas for another audience/domain.

Teacher/expert extension: external reviewer or expert audience where feasible; review evaluates communication performance, not domain credentials.

# Module 8 — Adaptive Mastery Portfolio

## Module mission

Demonstrate broad transfer and flexibility across deliberately different contexts rather than one polished specialty.

## Unit 8.1 — Cross-Context Challenge

Learner completes short demanding tasks across contrasting domains/registers.

Examples:

- interpret subtle interpersonal subtext;
- summarize specialist input;
- make an executive recommendation;
- mediate conflicting viewpoints;
- produce public-facing explanation;
- respond to an unfamiliar question.

## Unit 8.2 — Register and Audience Transformation

Learner transforms one complex idea across at least three audiences/modes while retaining accuracy.

Evidence:

- expert version;
- non-specialist version;
- interpersonal or public version;
- rationale;
- spontaneous follow-up.

## Unit 8.3 — English Without a Script

Unrehearsed/semi-structured challenge requiring:

- extended spontaneous interaction;
- ambiguity management;
- repair;
- register shift;
- mediation;
- rhetorical adaptation;
- strategic handling of unknown content.

## C2 Capstone — English Without a Script

Required portfolio:

1. subtle listening/pragmatic interpretation;
2. broad accent/register comprehension;
3. specialized-source synthesis;
4. sophisticated writing;
5. spontaneous unfamiliar-domain interaction;
6. expert-to-public mediation;
7. register transformation;
8. high-complexity digital collaboration;
9. independent mastery project;
10. cross-domain adaptive challenge;
11. delayed retrieval/revalidation;
12. reflection on limits, strategies and future domain growth.

## C2 Coach progression

Coach should increasingly behave as a sophisticated interlocutor rather than a tutor.

It should:

- introduce ambiguity/subtext;
- change roles/registers;
- challenge rhetorical choices;
- introduce unfamiliar concepts with enough context to reason;
- require audience transformation;
- simulate high-stakes negotiation/facilitation;
- test strategic repair;
- avoid unnecessary correction of non-consequential variation.

## C2 teacher/expert extension

Human support adds most value through:

- calibration of nuanced performance;
- high-complexity facilitation;
- rhetorical/pragmatic feedback;
- independent project review;
- professional/domain audience simulation;
- interpretation of ambiguous evidence.

Expert/domain contributors may provide authentic audience feedback, but Lurexa language judgments must remain transparent about what is language evidence vs domain expertise.

## C2 retrieval architecture

C2 must retrieve from earlier levels by increasing:

- unpredictability;
- domain distance;
- audience variation;
- time pressure where pedagogically appropriate;
- rhetorical/pragmatic complexity;
- reduction in scaffolding.

Retrieval should not be trivial repetition of advanced vocabulary.

## C2 publish gate

C2 is curriculum-complete only when:

- evidence spans multiple domains/registers;
- unfamiliar-context transfer is demonstrated;
- subtle interpretation is repeatedly evidenced;
- expert mediation is authentic/simulated at high fidelity;
- spontaneous interaction is substantial;
- independent mastery project is calibrated;
- cross-domain portfolio prevents one-specialty overclaiming;
- C2 is represented as exceptional flexibility, not native-like perfection or accent conformity.
