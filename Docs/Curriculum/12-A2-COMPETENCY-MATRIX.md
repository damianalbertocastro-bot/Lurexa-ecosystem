# English A2 Competency Matrix — Everyday Independence

Status: Authoritative A2 curriculum-development matrix  
Framework: CEFR A2 with CEFR Companion Volume integration  
Depends on: `02-ENGLISH-COMPETENCY-MODEL.md`, `11-ENGLISH-PROGRAM-MAP-A1-C2.md`

## 1. Level mission

A2 moves learners from highly supported survival communication toward routine independent communication. The learner should increasingly combine familiar language rather than depend on memorized scripts.

A2 development should show:

- longer listening/speaking turns;
- greater independence in routine situations;
- simple connected discourse;
- more reliable past/present/future reference;
- growing control of question formation;
- practical digital communication;
- straightforward mediation;
- improved connected-speech perception and production;
- transfer of A1 competencies into less predictable contexts.

## 2. Evidence expectations

A2 mastery cannot come from recognition items alone. Each major productive competency should receive evidence through at least two contexts before strong mastery is inferred.

Preferred evidence mix:

- recognition/comprehension;
- guided production;
- independent production;
- conversation/interaction;
- transfer task;
- later retrieval;
- teacher or AI-supported performance evidence where appropriate.

# Listening — LISTEN

- `EN.A2.LISTEN.ROUTINE_CONVERSATIONS` — understand the main point and key details in short routine conversations.
- `EN.A2.LISTEN.PAST_EVENTS` — follow simple accounts of past events when delivered clearly.
- `EN.A2.LISTEN.PLANS_ARRANGEMENTS` — understand basic future plans, schedules and arrangements.
- `EN.A2.LISTEN.SERVICE_INTERACTIONS` — understand common exchanges in shops, restaurants, transport, appointments and services.
- `EN.A2.LISTEN.SHORT_ANNOUNCEMENTS` — identify essential information in simple announcements or instructions.
- `EN.A2.LISTEN.DESCRIPTIONS` — understand straightforward descriptions of people, places, routines and experiences.
- `EN.A2.LISTEN.KEY_REASON` — identify a simple reason, preference or problem.
- `EN.A2.LISTEN.CONNECTED_SPEECH_FAMILIAR` — recognize frequent reductions/linking in familiar phrases with contextual support.

# Speaking — SPEAK

- `EN.A2.SPEAK.DESCRIBE_PAST_EVENT` — give a simple connected account of a past event.
- `EN.A2.SPEAK.DESCRIBE_EXPERIENCE` — describe familiar experiences with basic detail.
- `EN.A2.SPEAK.EXPLAIN_ROUTINE_PROBLEM` — explain a simple practical problem and what is needed.
- `EN.A2.SPEAK.MAKE_PLAN` — describe and negotiate a simple plan or arrangement.
- `EN.A2.SPEAK.COMPARE_SIMPLE_OPTIONS` — compare familiar people, places, objects or choices.
- `EN.A2.SPEAK.GIVE_ADVICE_BASIC` — give simple advice using familiar language.
- `EN.A2.SPEAK.DESCRIBE_GOAL` — describe a short-term personal, study or work goal.
- `EN.A2.SPEAK.RECOMMEND_FAMILIAR` — recommend a familiar place, activity, food, media item or experience and give a reason.
- `EN.A2.SPEAK.HANDLE_SERVICE_SITUATION` — manage a routine service transaction with basic follow-up.

# Reading — READ

- `EN.A2.READ.EVERYDAY_MESSAGES` — understand routine personal and practical messages.
- `EN.A2.READ.SCHEDULES_NOTICES` — locate key information in schedules, notices, menus, listings and instructions.

- `EN.A2.READ.INFER_BASIC_FROM_CONTEXT` — infer likely meaning of a familiar or transparent item using context.

# Cross-module mapping

## Module 1 — Stories From Everyday Life

Priority: past-event listening/speaking/writing, past grammar, `-ed`, narrative sequencing.

## Module 2 — Around Town and On the Move

Priority: travel/service listening, planning, transport vocabulary, arrangements, digital coordination.

## Module 3 — Home, Community and Services

Priority: description, comparisons, problem explanation, requests and community mediation.

## Module 4 — Health and Well-Being

Priority: symptoms/needs, advice, obligation, health listening, practical interaction.

## Module 5 — Work, Study and Responsibilities

Priority: goals, duties, professional messages, basic register and classroom/workplace interaction.

## Module 6 — Travel and Experiences

Priority: experience language, present perfect introduction, itineraries, relaying practical travel information.

## Module 7 — Entertainment, Culture and Social Life

Priority: recommendations, opinions/reasons, simple cultural comparison, social interaction.

## Module 8 — Everyday Independence Challenge

Priority: transfer, spontaneous combination, integrated performance, retention and level-exit evidence.

# A2 exit evidence portfolio

A2 completion should include evidence such as:

1. spoken account of a past experience;
2. routine service/problem conversation;
3. plan/arrangement interaction;
4. short connected written message or paragraph;
5. listening task with practical details and main point;
6. short reading/digital interaction task;
7. pronunciation sample showing intelligible past endings and connected phrases;
8. mediation task relaying practical information;
9. Create & Apply artifact;
10. delayed retrieval of selected A1 and early-A2 targets.

# Mastery rule

A2 level completion is a holistic decision based on a body of evidence. It must not require every competency to be flawless, and it must not be granted because a learner completed every screen. The decision should reflect whether the learner can function with A2-appropriate independence across the program's major communicative demands.
