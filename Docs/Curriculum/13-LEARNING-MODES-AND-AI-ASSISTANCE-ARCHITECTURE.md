# Lurexa Learning Modes and AI Assistance Architecture

Status: Authoritative curriculum-delivery model  
Owner: Lurexa Learning Technologies  
Applies to: Lurexa Learn and teacher-supported delivery

## 1. Core decision

Lurexa uses **one competency-based curriculum with multiple support intensities**.

The asynchronous curriculum is the pedagogical core. AI assistance is embedded throughout that core. Human teachers extend, enrich, validate and intervene where their judgment adds value.

Lurexa must not maintain separate academic standards for self-paced, AI-assisted and teacher-supported learners.

## 2. Non-negotiable principles

1. **Asynchronous-first completeness** — a learner must be able to make meaningful progress without waiting for a teacher meeting.
2. **Self-paced progression** — learners progress according to available time and demonstrated competence, not a mandatory calendar pace.
3. **AI-assisted learning** — AI provides timely scaffolding, practice, feedback, rehearsal and adaptive support throughout the learning cycle.
4. **Teacher-supported acceleration and depth** — teachers add human interaction, judgment, motivation, coaching, observation and high-value assessment.
5. **One standard, different support** — support level may change; competency expectations do not silently change.
6. **Evidence drives adaptation** — learner history changes what is reviewed, skipped, scaffolded, challenged and recommended next.
7. **Human help is targeted** — teacher time should be concentrated where human expertise has greater marginal value than another automated activity.

## 3. Shared curriculum model

All delivery modes use the same:

- competency graph;
- module/unit sequence;
- level outcomes;
- mastery definitions;
- evidence model;
- assessment standards;
- retrieval architecture;
- cultural/linguistic adaptation rules.

Delivery mode changes:

- pacing;
- frequency of AI interactions;
- human contact;
- feedback source;
- amount of independent practice;
- intervention intensity;
- accountability structure.

## 4. Learning modes

### Flex — Self-Paced AI-Assisted

Primary learner: independent learner, free/low-cost learner, or learner who cannot maintain fixed meeting times.

Core experience:

- asynchronous lessons;
- AI tutor support;
- interactive vocabulary;
- listening and pronunciation practice;
- guided and independent speaking;
- AI conversation;
- adaptive review;
- automated formative feedback;
- Create & Apply tasks that can be self- or AI-reviewed where appropriate;
- optional human escalation when available.

The Flex path must remain academically valid. It may have slower progression, fewer premium AI interactions or less human feedback, but it must not intentionally teach a weaker curriculum.

### Guided — Self-Paced Core + Teacher Support

Recommended default for learners who want structured human support.

Core experience:

- complete asynchronous AI-assisted curriculum;
- approximately one teacher touchpoint per week when scheduled;
- teacher review of learner evidence;
- targeted live conversation and production;
- teacher feedback on selected high-value artifacts;
- intervention and goal adjustment;
- accountability without turning the course into a fixed synchronous class.

Teacher sessions use async evidence and should not simply reteach the digital lesson.

### Intensive — Self-Paced Core + High-Frequency Human Coaching

For learners who want greater speaking frequency, faster feedback loops, professional/academic goals, or more intensive accountability.

Core experience:

- same async curriculum and standards;
- higher practice load;
- more frequent teacher/coach contact;
- more human-reviewed productive tasks;
- more live interaction and simulation;
- closer monitoring of high-priority gaps.

## 5. Self-paced does not mean unstructured

Self-paced learners still receive:

- recommended sequence;
- next-best activity;
- estimated workload;
- progress milestones;
- retrieval prompts;
- revalidation checks;
- optional study-plan recommendations;
- alerts when important competencies are becoming weak;
- challenge/test-out opportunities.

A learner may move faster or slower, but Lurexa remains responsible for coherent sequencing.

## 6. AI within the learning cycle

AI should support the universal Lurexa cycle as follows:

- **Encounter:** contextualize and personalize examples.
- **Understand:** explain at the learner's level and offer alternative explanations.
- **Practice:** generate controlled and guided practice constrained by approved competencies.
- **Retrieve:** vary prompts and target weak recall.
- **Apply:** simulate situations and roleplay.
- **Create:** coach planning and revision without replacing learner production.
- **Feedback:** identify highest-value corrections and explain them.
- **Improve:** provide focused retry and practice.
- **Transfer:** vary context and audience.
- **Retain:** trigger spaced retrieval and revalidation.

AI is a pedagogical assistant, not an unrestricted content generator.

## 7. AI escalation ladder

Support should normally progress from least to most assistance:

1. learner attempts independently;
2. AI asks a guiding question;
3. small hint;
4. larger hint;
5. example or contrast;
6. concise explanation;
7. model answer where pedagogically justified;
8. targeted follow-up practice.

The system should preserve first-attempt evidence before support is given.

## 8. Human escalation ladder

A learner should be surfaced for teacher attention when evidence indicates one or more of the following:

- repeated failure after appropriate AI scaffolding;
- persistent communication-critical pattern;
- stalled progress across several sessions;
- significant productive/receptive imbalance;
- repeated pronunciation/intelligibility barrier;
- weak confidence or avoidance behavior where human coaching may help;
- high-value writing/speaking task requiring nuanced judgment;
- placement or level-exit uncertainty;
- learner explicitly requests human help.

Teacher support should therefore be **evidence-triggered as well as scheduled**.

## 9. Teacher-session contract

Before a supported session, the teacher should receive an authorized learning brief containing only useful pedagogical information, such as:

- current unit and mission;
- competencies recently practiced;
- strengths;
- recurring difficulties;
- unresolved high-priority errors;
- recent speaking/writing evidence;
- suggested live task;
- suggested feedback priority;
- learner goals.

The teacher can override AI suggestions.

A live session should normally emphasize:

**Retrieve → Interact → Apply → Create → Human Feedback → Next Action**

## 10. Offline and interruption resilience

Because the curriculum is asynchronous, lessons should tolerate interrupted study.

Design expectations:

- meaningful short stages;
- resumable progress;
- clear checkpointing;
- downloadable/offline-compatible content where product capability allows;
- no assumption that a learner can complete a long session in one sitting;
- retrieval on return rather than blindly resuming after long gaps.

## 11. Progression rules

Calendar time does not determine level progression.

Progress should combine:

- competency evidence;
- integrated performance;
- retention evidence;
- level-exit tasks;
- teacher validation where required or available.

Recommended weekly schedules are planning tools, not mastery gates.

## 12. Mode switching

Learners should be able to move among Flex, Guided and Intensive without losing curriculum history.

A switch changes support configuration, not academic identity.

Examples:

- Flex learner adds a teacher subscription → same competency history continues.
- Guided learner pauses teacher sessions → async pathway remains available.
- Intensive learner reduces pace → no need to restart the course.

## 13. Commercial access principle

Business tiers may vary:

- AI usage limits;
- teacher meeting frequency;
- feedback turnaround;
- analytics depth;
- premium simulations;
- convenience and support.

They must not distort the learner's measured CEFR level or deliberately provide pedagogically invalid instruction.

## 14. Definition of success

The delivery architecture succeeds when a learner can progress independently with strong AI support, receive human help exactly where it adds value, change support modes without restarting, and build one continuous evidence-backed learning history.

> **Lurexa is self-paced by default, AI-assisted throughout, and teacher-enhanced when human support adds value.**
