# English C1 Module and Unit Blueprints — Precision, Identity and Global Perspective

Status: Production curriculum-development authority  
Depends on: `11-ENGLISH-PROGRAM-MAP-A1-C2.md`, `24-CURRICULUM-COVERAGE-AND-QA-GATES.md`, `26-C1-COMPETENCY-MATRIX.md`

## 1. Level design principle

C1 should require reliable control of complex meaning, strategic register choice, synthesis, facilitation, mediation and professional/academic performance.

Compared with B2, C1 should introduce:

- denser and less explicitly signposted input;
- more implicit stance and rhetorical meaning;
- longer autonomous production;
- strategic register adaptation;
- multiparty facilitation;
- real-time synthesis;
- nuanced disagreement/reframing;
- responsible expression of uncertainty;
- greater accent/register variation;
- culturally and professionally complex perspectives.

Support should increasingly take the form of post-performance feedback, strategic prompts and optional tools rather than sentence-level scaffolding.

# Module 1 — Caribbean Heritage, Language and Identity

## Module mission

Analyze relationships among language, culture, history, identity, variety and power while communicating nuanced interpretations and avoiding essentialist claims.

Core competencies:

- `EN.C1.READ.INTERPRET_STYLE_REGISTER`
- `EN.C1.LISTEN.IMPLICIT_STANCE`
- `EN.C1.SPEAK.REFRAME_PERSPECTIVE`
- `EN.C1.WRITE.NUANCED_EVALUATION`
- `EN.C1.VOCAB.LEXICAL_PRECISION`
- `EN.C1.PRAG.SIGNAL_STANCE_PRECISELY`
- `EN.C1.PLURI.INTERPRET_IDENTITY_LANGUAGE_RELATION`
- `EN.C1.PLURI.RECOGNIZE_VARIETY_STATUS_POWER`

## Unit 1.1 — Language, History and Identity

Performance: explain how language practices can reflect history, community and identity while distinguishing evidence from assumption.

Evidence:

- synthesis of spoken/written sources;
- extended interpretation;
- response to a competing perspective;
- reflective written analysis.

## Unit 1.2 — Caribbean Englishes and Language Variation

Performance: interpret variation without treating one variety as inherently deficient.

Focus:

- accent/variety comprehension;
- register;
- sociolinguistic status/power;
- intelligibility vs prestige;
- identity-sensitive language.

Evidence:

- varied-accent listening;
- source-based explanation;
- discussion of language attitudes;
- audience-sensitive reframing.

## Unit 1.3 — Cultural Expression and Voice

Performance: analyze how language choices contribute to identity and cultural expression.

Create & Apply: public commentary, audio essay or panel contribution.

Coach hook: defend a nuanced position while Coach challenges overgeneralization and asks for evidence/examples.

Teacher extension: seminar comparing interpretations from multiple Caribbean/global perspectives.

# Module 2 — Power, Language and Public Discourse

## Module mission

Analyze framing, rhetoric, implication, politeness and power in public/professional discourse and respond with deliberate rhetorical choices.

Core competencies:

- `EN.C1.LISTEN.RHETORICAL_PURPOSE`
- `EN.C1.READ.RHETORICAL_STRUCTURE`
- `EN.C1.SPEAK.PRESENT_COMPLEX_ARGUMENT`
- `EN.C1.WRITE.ARGUMENTATIVE_ANALYSIS`
- `EN.C1.GRAMMAR.HEDGING_PRECISION`
- `EN.C1.PHON.RHETORICAL_EMPHASIS`
- `EN.C1.PRAG.NAVIGATE_POWER_RELATIONS`
- `EN.C1.PRAG.INTERPRET_IMPLICATION_NUANCE`

## Unit 2.1 — Framing and Rhetorical Purpose

Performance: identify how structure, word choice, omission and emphasis shape audience interpretation.

Evidence:

- rhetorical annotation;
- implicit-stance listening;
- oral explanation;
- comparative analysis.

## Unit 2.2 — Politeness, Face and Power

Performance: interpret and produce language that manages hierarchy, disagreement and interpersonal risk.

Focus:

- mitigation;
- indirectness/directness;
- pragmatic force;
- face management;
- professional/social power dynamics.

Coach hook: difficult request or disagreement with changing role/status relationship.

## Unit 2.3 — Public Commentary

Performance: construct a rhetorically deliberate public response for a defined audience.

Create & Apply: commentary/editorial + oral defense.

Teacher extension: rhetoric workshop where students transform the same position for two audiences and justify the changes.

# Module 3 — Leadership, Collaboration and Difficult Conversations

## Module mission

Facilitate discussion, reframe disagreement, negotiate complexity and build shared understanding in demanding interpersonal/professional contexts.

Core competencies:

- `EN.C1.SPEAK.FACILITATE_DISCUSSION`
- `EN.C1.SPEAK.NEGOTIATE_COMPLEXITY`
- `EN.C1.CONV.FACILITATE_COMPLEX_DISCUSSION`

- `EN.C1.MED.CO_CONSTRUCT_MEANING`
- `EN.C1.PHON.PRAGMATIC_INTONATION`

## Unit 3.1 — Lead the Conversation

Performance: open, structure, redirect and close a multiparty discussion while protecting participation.

Evidence:

- facilitation simulation;
- turn-management analysis;
- summary of shared/remaining issues;
- self-review of interventions.

## Unit 3.2 — Difficult Conversations

Performance: address disagreement/problem behavior while preserving clarity, dignity and progress.

Scenarios:

- missed commitment;
- conflicting priorities;
- feedback disagreement;
- misunderstanding involving tone/culture;
- sensitive correction.

Evidence:

- live/AI roleplay;
- pragmatic decision log;
- retry with changed constraints;
- reflective analysis.

## Unit 3.3 — Build Shared Understanding

Performance: mediate conflicting views and propose a shared formulation or decision path.

Create & Apply: facilitated meeting + written outcome summary.

Coach hook: multiparty simulation with one participant misunderstanding or resisting.

Teacher extension: observed facilitation with feedback on pragmatic/prosodic choices.

# Module 4 — Knowledge, Research and Evidence

## Module mission

Synthesize demanding sources, evaluate evidence quality, communicate uncertainty and produce disciplined research-informed analysis.

Core competencies:

- `EN.C1.READ.SYNTHESIZE_MULTIPLE_SOURCES`
- `EN.C1.READ.EVALUATE_EVIDENCE_QUALITY`
- `EN.C1.LISTEN.COMPLEX_EXTENDED_DISCOURSE`
- `EN.C1.WRITE.RESEARCH_BRIEF`
- `EN.C1.WRITE.SYNTHESIS_MULTISOURCE`
- `EN.C1.SPEAK.EXPRESS_UNCERTAINTY_PRECISELY`
- `EN.C1.MED.SYNTHESIZE_DEMANDING_SOURCES`
- `EN.C1.STRAT.PRIORITIZE_INFORMATION`

## Unit 4.1 — What Counts as Evidence?

Performance: distinguish claim, evidence, method/limitation and inference across accessible disciplinary sources.

Evidence:

- source evaluation;
- uncertainty language;
- comparison of evidence strength;
- short analytic briefing.

## Unit 4.2 — Synthesize, Don't Stack Summaries

Performance: integrate sources around ideas, agreements, tensions and evidence quality.

AI rule: AI may generate competing synthesis examples for critique but not produce the assessed final synthesis.

Evidence:

- synthesis matrix;
- oral real-time synthesis;
- written multisource synthesis;
- revision for cohesion/precision.

## Unit 4.3 — Research Brief

Performance: communicate a research-informed answer/recommendation to a defined professional audience.

Create & Apply: research brief + executive oral briefing/Q&A.

Coach hook: explain evidence/limitations to a skeptical stakeholder.

Teacher extension: source-evidence conference focused on reasoning and uncertainty, not grammar correction alone.

# Module 8 — Precision and Perspective Capstone

## Module mission

Integrate C1 research, argument, facilitation, mediation, register, cultural perspective and professional communication under high autonomy.

## Unit 8.1 — Define and Investigate

Learner selects or receives a complex question with:

- multiple perspectives;
- demanding sources;
- uncertainty;
- defined real/realistic audience.

Evidence:

- research question;
- source/evidence evaluation;
- synthesis map;
- audience/purpose strategy.

## Unit 8.2 — Create and Communicate

Learner produces a substantial artifact such as:

- research brief;
- strategic recommendation;
- public commentary;
- multimodal analysis;
- professional proposal.

Required: clear provenance and responsible uncertainty.

## Unit 8.3 — Facilitate, Defend and Mediate

Learner:

- presents core position;
- answers challenging questions;
- facilitates discussion;
- reframes disagreement;
- mediates one complex point for a different audience;
- synthesizes the discussion outcome.

## C1 Capstone — Language, Identity and Global Perspective

Required portfolio:

1. demanding listening interpretation;
2. multisource reading/evidence evaluation;
3. research-informed writing;
4. extended oral argument;
5. facilitated/multiparty interaction;
6. mediation/reframing performance;
7. strategic professional/digital communication;
8. rhetorical/pragmatic pronunciation sample;
9. delayed retrieval from B2/C1;
10. reflection on precision, perspective and future goals.

## C1 Coach progression

Coach should:

- challenge assumptions;
- request evidence/qualification;
- create power/register shifts;
- simulate difficult conversations;
- require real-time reformulation;
- introduce unfamiliar but accessible domains;
- test pragmatic/prosodic choices.

## C1 teacher extension

Teacher-supported work should prioritize:

- seminars;
- facilitation;
- complex feedback conversations;
- research/evidence defense;
- rhetorical delivery;
- mediation of conflicting perspectives;
- calibration of nuanced language/pragmatics.

## C1 retrieval architecture

Retrieval should include:

- B2 argument/synthesis in more demanding contexts;
- earlier C1 register/pragmatic targets under changed audiences;
- delayed reformulation and facilitation;
- cross-module reuse of research/evidence language.

## C1 publish gate

C1 is curriculum-complete only when:

- synthesis and mediation occur across demanding sources/perspectives;
- multiparty facilitation is evidenced;
- register adaptation is repeated across modalities;
- complex uncertainty/qualification is demonstrated;
- varied accents/registers are represented without stereotyping;
- capstone performance is calibrated;
- no advanced competency is inferred from isolated grammar or vocabulary tests.
