# A1 Modules 2–8 Production Specification

Status: Curriculum production architecture
Level: A1 — Foundations for Real Communication

## Purpose
This document completes the A1 production architecture after Module 1. It gives Codex, curriculum authors, Lurexa Studio and teachers a coherent sequence of units, lessons, language targets, phonetic priorities, conversation tasks and Create & Apply outcomes.

The lesson titles and cultural contexts may evolve. Competency progression and pedagogical sequencing must remain stable unless the source-of-truth methodology is revised.

# Module 2 — My World in Numbers

## Module mission
Understand and communicate the numbers, dates and times needed for everyday life.

## Core communication outcomes
Learner can:
- understand and say common numbers;
- give age and simple contact information;
- understand and say dates;
- identify days/months;
- tell basic time;
- understand a simple schedule;
- arrange a simple meeting time.

## Unit 1 — Numbers Everywhere

### Lesson 1 — Numbers I Use
Targets: 0–100, age, basic quantities.
Listening: ages and common numbers.
Phonetics: teen/ty contrast; stress in thirteen/thirty, fourteen/forty, etc.
Speaking: say age and selected numbers.
Create & Apply: `My Number World` — learner records five personally relevant but privacy-safe numbers.

### Lesson 2 — Prices and Money
Targets: common prices, pesos/dollars as contexts, `How much is it?`
Listening: short shop/café prices.
Reading: basic price labels/menu snippets.
Conversation: ask and answer simple price questions.
Create & Apply: choose items within a fictional budget.

### Lesson 3 — Phone Numbers and Codes
Targets: phone numbers, room numbers, simple codes; zero/oh awareness where useful.
Listening: number strings with chunking.
Phonetics: pacing and grouping numbers clearly.
Create & Apply: fictional contact card.

### Lesson 4 — Number Challenge
Integrated retrieval, listening and speaking mission.
Conversation: exchange age, a fictional phone number and one price.

## Unit 2 — Days, Dates and Celebrations

### Lesson 1 — My Week
Targets: days of the week, today/tomorrow, weekday/weekend.
Grammar/function: basic `on Monday`, `today is...`.
Listening: simple weekly schedule.
Phonetics: word stress in weekdays.
Create & Apply: build a weekly mini-calendar.

### Lesson 2 — Dates and Birthdays
Targets: ordinal/date chunks, birthday, date of birth.
Listening: dates.
Speaking: state birthday/date.
Phonetics: ordinal endings and date rhythm; intelligibility over perfect form.

### Lesson 3 — Months and Celebrations
Targets: months, common celebrations and local/international dates.
Cultural adaptation: Dominican Independence Day, birthdays, Christmas/New Year and international examples without requiring cultural trivia.
Reading: simple event calendar.
Create & Apply: `Three Important Dates`.

### Lesson 4 — Plan a Birthday
Conversation: ask date/time and make a simple plan.
Create & Apply: invitation card/message.

## Unit 3 — What Time Is It?

### Lesson 1 — Telling Time
Targets: hour/half hour/quarter where appropriate; digital time as primary accessible format.
Listening: times.
Phonetics: clear number production.

### Lesson 2 — Daily Schedules
Targets: at + time, morning/afternoon/evening; start/finish.
Reading: work/school schedules.
Grammar: prepositions of time in high-frequency chunks.

### Lesson 3 — Meeting Times
Targets: `What time?`, `At...`, `Is ___ okay?`
Conversation: negotiate one simple time.

### Lesson 4 — Let’s Meet
Unit mission: interpret a simple schedule and arrange a meeting.
Create & Apply: create and explain a one-day schedule.

## Module 2 phonetics priorities
- number stress;
- teen/ty distinctions;
- ordinal intelligibility;
- rhythm in date/time chunks.

## Module 2 assessment
Listen to a schedule, read a calendar, state date/time information and arrange a meeting in a short conversation.

# Module 3 — The People Around Me

## Module mission
Talk about family, friends and community members using basic relationships and descriptions.

## Unit 1 — Family and Relationships

### Lesson 1 — Who Is in Your Family?
Targets: immediate/extended family and inclusive relationship vocabulary.
Listening: short family introductions.
Grammar: my/your/his/her.
Create & Apply: family or important-people map with fictional option.

### Lesson 2 — Family Connections
Targets: `This is my...`, `His/Her name is...`, basic have/has chunks where useful.
Reading: simple family tree/profile.
Conversation: ask about one important person.

### Lesson 3 — My Family Story
Writing: 4–6 simple sentences.
Speaking: 30–45 second family/important-person description.
Phonetics: final consonants and phrase chunking.

## Unit 2 — Describing People

### Lesson 1 — What Do They Look Like?
Targets: tall, short, young, older, hair/eyes/basic appearance descriptors.
Grammar: be + adjective; have/has in fixed communicative patterns.
Cultural rule: avoid value judgments linking appearance to worth.

### Lesson 2 — What Are They Like?
Targets: kind, friendly, funny, quiet, active, helpful, serious.
Listening: identify a person from description.
Phonetics: adjective stress.

### Lesson 3 — Who Am I Describing?
Information-gap style guessing task.
Conversation: describe a person; AI/peer asks one follow-up.
Create & Apply: profile card.

## Unit 3 — People in My Community

### Lesson 1 — Friends and Neighbors
Targets: friend, neighbor, coworker/classmate; basic relation statements.
Cultural context: local neighborhood and online/international connections.

### Lesson 2 — People at Work and School
Targets: teacher, manager, coworker, classmate and selected high-frequency roles.
Listening: workplace/school mini-dialogues.

### Lesson 3 — Someone Important to Me
Integrated reading/writing/speaking.
Create & Apply: 45–60 second audio portrait.

### Lesson 4 — Community Profile Mission
Module assessment: introduce/describe two people and understand equivalent descriptions.

## Module 3 phonetics priorities
- final plural /s z ɪz/ awareness;
- final consonants;
- stress in descriptive words;
- chunking short descriptions.

# Module 4 — My Everyday Life

## Module mission
Describe everyday routines, frequency and what is happening now in familiar contexts.

## Unit 1 — Morning to Night

### Lesson 1 — Wake Up and Go
Vocabulary: wake up, get up, shower, eat breakfast, leave, go to work/school.
Grammar: present simple first-person.
Listening: morning sequence.
Create & Apply: arrange routine cards and narrate.

### Lesson 2 — Work and Study Day
Targets: work, study, start, finish, have lunch, go home.
Reading: `A Day in My Life`.
Speaking: 4–6 sentence routine.

### Lesson 3 — Evening Routine
Targets: cook/eat dinner, relax, watch, study, sleep/go to bed.
Phonetics: third-person -s awareness may begin through input, but productive focus remains first-person routine.

### Lesson 4 — My Day in 60 Seconds
Create & Apply: one-minute routine recording.
Conversation: compare simple routines with AI/teacher/peer.

## Unit 2 — How Often?

### Lesson 1 — Always, Usually, Sometimes
Grammar: adverbs of frequency with common present-simple frames.
Listening: identify frequency.

### Lesson 2 — Weekly Habits
Targets: every day, on weekends, once/twice where useful.
Reading: simple habit profiles.

### Lesson 3 — Compare Our Routines
Conversation: ask `How often...?`; answer and react.
Create & Apply: simple habit survey with teacher/peer option.

## Unit 3 — Today Is Different

### Lesson 1 — What Are You Doing?
Introduce present continuous for immediate action through meaningful images/scenes.
Targets: `I'm working`, `She's studying` in limited controlled range.

### Lesson 2 — Usually vs Today
Contrast routine vs now without overloading tense terminology.
Listening: identify usual action vs current action.

### Lesson 3 — A Different Day
Speaking/writing: `I usually..., but today...`
Conversation: exchange two current activities.

### Lesson 4 — My Real Week
Integrated module mission: routine + frequency + one current change.

## Module 4 phonetics priorities
- third-person final sounds in listening/early production;
- sentence stress in routine phrases;
- reductions/chunking in common expressions;
- pacing connected 5–7 sentence speech.

## Module 4 assessment
Listen to a routine, read a weekly schedule, describe own routine and distinguish habit from current action.

# Module 5 — Food, Flavor and Everyday Choices

## Module mission
Talk about food preferences, understand simple menus and complete basic food-shopping/restaurant interactions.

## Unit 1 — What Do We Eat?

### Lesson 1 — Breakfast Around Us
Vocabulary: eggs, bread, fruit, coffee, milk, water and culturally relevant examples such as mangú where useful.
Listening: breakfast orders/descriptions.
Create & Apply: build a breakfast plate and describe it.

### Lesson 2 — Dominican, Caribbean and Global Foods
Vocabulary: selected foods and categories, not encyclopedic lists.
Reading: simple food cards.
Cultural comparison: familiar foods alongside regional/international options.
Conversation: `Do you like...?`

### Lesson 3 — Food I Like
Grammar/function: like/don't like, favorite, want.
Speaking: preference statements with basic reasons using chunks like `because it's...` only if manageable.

## Unit 2 — At a Café or Restaurant

### Lesson 1 — Read the Menu
Reading: simple menu sections/prices.
Vocabulary: drink, meal, breakfast/lunch/dinner, menu, bill/check where region/register context is explained.

### Lesson 2 — Order, Please
Functions: `I'd like...`, `Can I have...?`, `Please`, `Thank you` as functional chunks.
Listening: short café exchange.
Phonetics: polite intonation and chunk stress.

### Lesson 3 — Questions and Requests
Targets: water, more, without, with, bill/check; simple clarification.
Conversation: order and respond to one service question.

### Lesson 4 — Restaurant Roleplay
Scenario conversation with AI/human.
Create & Apply: complete order from a menu under a fictional budget.

## Unit 3 — Shopping for Food

### Lesson 1 — Count or Measure?
Grammar foundation: countable/uncountable through concrete shopping language.
Vocabulary: bottle, bag, kilo/pound where contextualized, some/any.

### Lesson 2 — At the Supermarket
Listening: quantities/prices.
Reading: shopping list.

### Lesson 3 — What Do We Need?
Function: `We need...`, `Do we have any...?`, `some/any` in limited contexts.
Conversation: plan a small shopping list.

### Lesson 4 — Plan a Simple Meal
Create & Apply: select ingredients, make a list and explain basic choices.

## Module 5 phonetics priorities
- polite request intonation;
- stress in food vocabulary;
- linking in `Can I have...`, `I'd like...`;
- final consonants in common food/order words.

## Module 5 assessment
Read a menu, understand a simple order, make a purchase/order and describe basic food preferences.

# Module 6 — My Neighborhood and Getting Around

## Module mission
Describe familiar places, locate them and ask for/give simple directions.

## Unit 1 — Places Near Me

### Lesson 1 — What’s in My Neighborhood?
Vocabulary: school, supermarket, pharmacy, bank, park, restaurant, hospital/clinic, bus stop, beach where relevant.
Grammar: there is/there are.
Create & Apply: neighborhood inventory.

### Lesson 2 — Places I Use
Reading: simple local map/ directory.
Speaking: `There is a... near...`
Conversation: ask about one local place.

### Lesson 3 — Describe Your Area
Writing: 4–6 sentences about a real or fictional area.
Cultural adaptation: urban, coastal, suburban and small-town examples.

## Unit 2 — Where Is It?

### Lesson 1 — In, On, Next To
Prepositions: in, on, next to, near, between, across from as appropriate.
Visual map interaction.

### Lesson 2 — Maps and Landmarks
Vocabulary: corner, street, avenue, block, landmark.
Reading/listening: locate destinations.

### Lesson 3 — Find the Place
Information gap: learner follows or gives location clues.
Phonetics: contrastive stress for location words.

## Unit 3 — Getting There

### Lesson 1 — Turn Left, Turn Right
Functions: imperatives for directions, go straight, turn, stop.
Listening: simple route.

### Lesson 2 — Transportation Basics
Vocabulary: bus, car, taxi, metro, walk, bike, airport/station as needed.
Dominican relevance: guagua can appear as cultural vocabulary with English equivalent/context, but the English target remains transport language.

### Lesson 3 — Ask for Directions
Conversation: `Excuse me, where is...?` + route response.
Repair language reinforced.

### Lesson 4 — My Neighborhood Map Mission
Create & Apply: draw/select a simple map and record directions to two places.

## Module 6 phonetics priorities
- direction chunk stress;
- clear final consonants;
- `left/right/straight` intelligibility;
- polite request intonation.

## Module 6 assessment
Follow a route, locate places, ask for directions and give a short route explanation.

# Module 7 — What I Like and What I Do for Fun

## Module mission
Talk about preferences, hobbies, abilities and simple social plans.

## Unit 1 — Likes and Dislikes

### Lesson 1 — I Like It
Vocabulary: like, love, don't like, favorite; common activities/music/food categories.
Grammar/function: like + noun / simple -ing chunks where natural.
Conversation: exchange preferences.

### Lesson 2 — My Favorites
Targets: favorite music, sport, food, place, activity.
Cultural context: merengue, bachata, dembow may appear alongside broader music categories without assuming learner preference.
Create & Apply: favorites card/audio.

### Lesson 3 — What Do You Like?
Question forms: `Do you like...?`, `What's your favorite...?`
Conversation: 6–8 turn preference exchange.

## Unit 2 — Hobbies, Sports and Entertainment

### Lesson 1 — Free-Time Activities
Vocabulary: play, watch, listen, read, dance, exercise, go out, stay home.
Listening: hobby profiles.

### Lesson 2 — What Can You Do?
Grammar/function: can/can't for basic ability.
Phonetics: can/can't intelligibility; stress difference in connected speech.

### Lesson 3 — Music, Sports and Me
Reading/listening: short preference profiles.
Speaking: explain 2–3 activities learner does/likes.
Create & Apply: 45-second `My Free Time` recording.

## Unit 3 — Making Simple Plans

### Lesson 1 — Want to Go?
Functions: `Do you want to...?`, `Let's...`, invitation chunks.

### Lesson 2 — Yes, No, Maybe
Functions: accept/decline politely: `Sure`, `Sorry, I can't`, `Maybe another day` as chunks.
Phonetics: expressive/pragmatic intonation.

### Lesson 3 — Weekend Plan Conversation
Scenario: make a plan for Saturday/Sunday, agree on activity/time/place.

### Lesson 4 — Create a Mini Event Plan
Create & Apply: event card/message with place, date/time and activity.

## Module 7 phonetics priorities
- can/can't;
- invitation/acceptance intonation;
- chunk stress;
- natural rhythm in high-frequency social phrases.

## Module 7 assessment
Discuss preferences/abilities and arrange a simple social plan.

# Module 8 — Life in Action

## Module mission
Integrate A1 skills in realistic missions and gather readiness evidence for A2 progression.

This module introduces little essential new grammar. Its main purpose is retrieval, transfer, independent use and revalidation.

## Unit 1 — Real-Life Missions

### Lesson 1 — Meet Someone
Scenario: first meeting in class, work, travel or online setting.
Must combine greeting, name, origin, role and clarification.

### Lesson 2 — Order Something
Scenario: café/shop transaction involving price, quantity and polite request.

### Lesson 3 — Find a Place
Scenario: locate destination and ask/give directions.

### Lesson 4 — Make a Plan
Scenario: preference + date/time + invitation + response.

## Unit 2 — My Life, My English

### Lesson 1 — My Profile
Retrieve and refine self-introduction.

### Lesson 2 — My Routine
Retrieve and refine routine/frequency language.

### Lesson 3 — My Community
Combine people + neighborhood + preferences.

### Lesson 4 — Portfolio Build
Learner chooses/revises evidence artifacts from previous modules.
Portfolio may include:
- self-introduction;
- important person description;
- routine;
- food/service task;
- neighborhood directions;
- preferences/social plan.

Teacher can review selected artifacts where available.

## Unit 3 — A1 Challenge and Next Step

### Lesson 1 — Listen and Read
Integrated A1 receptive challenge with familiar and slightly novel contexts.

### Lesson 2 — Speak and Converse
2–3 minute supported conversation sampling several functions.

### Lesson 3 — Write and Create
Short connected text and one Create & Apply task.

### Lesson 4 — A1 Readiness Review
Evidence synthesis across seven skills.
Includes:
- vocabulary retrieval;
- grammar-in-context;
- listening;
- reading;
- writing;
- speaking/conversation;
- phonetic intelligibility sample.

## A1 capstone — My Life, My English
Goal: demonstrate that the learner can use beginner English for real personal/everyday communication.

Suggested capstone bundle:
1. 60-second self-profile;
2. 30–45 second important-person description;
3. simple routine text/audio;
4. service or directions scenario;
5. 2–3 minute conversation;
6. short written personal message/profile.

## A1 readiness logic
Possible outcomes:
- Ready for A2
- Ready for A2 with targeted A1 review
- A1 competencies need focused revalidation

Do not force full-level repetition when weaknesses are narrow.

# A1 Cross-Level Retrieval Rules

## Vocabulary
High-value vocabulary must recur across later modules rather than disappear after first instruction.
Examples:
- identity vocabulary reappears in travel/social contexts;
- numbers reappear in prices, times and directions;
- family/person adjectives reappear in social plans;
- routine verbs reappear in preferences and capstone tasks.

## Grammar
Earlier structures are reused in increasingly independent contexts.
Do not present every recurrence as a new grammar lesson.

## Phonetics
Targets progress from awareness to intelligible use:
- sound/spelling awareness;
- final consonants;
- number/word stress;
- sentence stress;
- linking/chunking;
- pragmatic intonation in invitations/requests.

## Conversation
Every module includes at least:
- guided exchange;
- scenario conversation;
- more independent module performance.

## Create & Apply
Every unit includes at least one productive artifact/performance. Human teachers may deepen these without changing the underlying competency targets.

# A1 Production Priority
Engineering should not attempt to implement every A1 lesson simultaneously.

Recommended content rollout:
1. Module 1 Unit 1 vertical slice;
2. validate v2 lesson player/evidence/memory;
3. finish Module 1;
4. Module 2;
5. Modules 3–4;
6. Modules 5–7;
7. Module 8 capstone/readiness.

This keeps curriculum scale behind product validation rather than ahead of it.
