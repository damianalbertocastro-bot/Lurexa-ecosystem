# A2 Module 1 Production Lesson Blueprint — Stories From Everyday Life

Status: Implementation-ready production specification  
Depends on: `12-A2-COMPETENCY-MATRIX.md`, `17-A2-MODULE-AND-UNIT-BLUEPRINTS.md`, `20-A1-MODULE-1-PRODUCTION-BLUEPRINT.md`, `21-A1-LEARNING-LOOP-VALIDATION-AND-PILOT-SPEC.md`

## 1. Module mission

Learners move from isolated past forms toward connected accounts of recent events, everyday problems, and experiences. The module must require listening, speaking, writing, conversation, pronunciation, retrieval, and transfer—not merely past-simple exercises.

## 2. Production rule

A2 uses the validated A1 learning loop but with:

- longer input;
- more learner-generated language;
- fewer sentence frames;
- more follow-up questions;
- stronger past-time control;
- more connected speech;
- greater responsibility for repair;
- short independent narration.

# 4. Unit 1.1 — Yesterday and Last Weekend

## Lesson 1.1.1 — What Happened?

Mission: **I can understand the main sequence of a short past-event story.**

Estimated async time: 20–25 minutes.

### Async sequence

1. visual prediction;
2. first listen for global meaning;
3. second listen for sequence/details;
4. timeline reconstruction;
5. notice past-time markers;
6. retrieve selected irregular verbs;
7. micro-listen to difficult endings/chunks;
8. spoken 3-event retell;
9. AI feedback on sequence + highest-value language issue;
10. delayed retrieval prompt.

### AI role

- ask learner for sequence before correcting forms;
- distinguish message success from grammatical accuracy;
- do not rewrite full retell;
- prompt one repair at a time;
- use learner evidence to decide whether to support irregular form, sequence marker, or pronunciation.

### Evidence

- main idea;
- sequence reconstruction;
- key detail comprehension;
- independent retell length;
- past-form accuracy;
- scaffolding level;
- intelligibility.

## Lesson 1.1.2 — Regular and Irregular Past for Use

Mission: **I can talk about common completed actions in the past.**

Method: contextual input → noticing → meaning/form → controlled retrieval → guided production → free short response.

Do not present long decontextualized verb lists.

### Pronunciation

Teach `-ed` as meaning-bearing speech, not a spelling quiz. Use small high-frequency sets and emphasize final consonant intelligibility.

### Spanish-speaker risk

Watch for insertion of extra syllable in `-ed` forms when not required. Correct selectively and through listen/compare/whole-word retry.

## Lesson 1.1.3 — Tell Me About Your Weekend

Mission: **I can give a short connected account of my weekend or another recent day.**

### Async sequence

idea planner → vocabulary retrieval → 30-second first attempt → AI asks 2–3 follow-ups → focused feedback → second attempt → compare first/second performance.

### AI rule

The first attempt must occur before the AI supplies a model answer.

### Teacher extension

Live story exchange emphasizing follow-up questions and spontaneous clarification.

# 5. Unit 1.2 — When Things Went Wrong

## Lesson 1.2.1 — A Small Problem

Mission: **I can understand and explain a simple problem that happened.**

Scenario examples: missed bus, lost item, late appointment, wrong order, phone battery died, forgot something.

### Async sequence

branching story → identify problem → order events → problem vocabulary → guided explanation → AI asks what happened next.

### Evidence

problem identification, sequencing, explanation completeness, target vocabulary retrieval.

## Lesson 1.2.2 — Questions About the Past

Mission: **I can ask simple questions about what happened.**

Targets:

- What happened?
- Where did you go?
- What did you do?
- Did you call ___?
- Why did ___?

### Error priority

For Spanish speakers, auxiliary omission or verb duplication after `did` is a high-value target when observed.

### AI role

Use information-gap conversation so question formation is necessary to complete the task.

## Lesson 1.2.3 — Solve the Problem

Mission: **I can explain a past problem and complete a simple problem-resolution conversation.**

AI roleplay counterpart: friend, transport worker, receptionist, service worker.

Required learner actions:

- explain what happened;
- answer at least two follow-ups;
- ask one relevant question;
- clarify if misunderstood;
- state simple outcome/next action.

Create & Apply: 60–90 second problem-and-solution account.

Teacher extension: diagnose whether breakdown comes from tense, question formation, vocabulary retrieval, or pronunciation rather than treating all errors alike.

# 6. Unit 1.3 — Experiences Worth Sharing

## Lesson 1.3.1 — A Memorable Experience

Mission: **I can understand and describe a simple familiar experience.**

Input should include short personal stories from varied speakers/contexts.

### Async sequence

predict → listen/read → key detail → reaction language → vocabulary → guided retell → personal connection.

## Lesson 1.3.2 — Have You Ever...?

Mission: **I can ask about a familiar life experience and give a simple answer.**

Present perfect is introduced narrowly for experience; past simple follows for details.

Example:

- Have you ever traveled alone?
- Yes, I have.
- Where did you go?

Do not attempt exhaustive present-perfect instruction in this module.

### AI role

Keep conversation at A2; prompt simple past detail after experience response.

## Lesson 1.3.3 — Story Exchange

Mission: **I can exchange experiences in a 6–10 turn conversation.**

Success requires:

- answer beyond one word;
- ask at least two follow-up questions;
- give one simple reaction/reason;
- maintain understandable sequence;
- use repair if needed.

Create & Apply: mini personal-story audio + short response/reaction to another supplied story.

# 7. Module-level AI adaptation

### Strong learner

- shorten controlled past-form practice;
- increase narration length;
- introduce less predictable follow-up;
- preserve delayed retrieval.

### Weak form / strong communication

- do not interrupt story flow excessively;
- provide post-task correction on one or two recurring forms;
- retrieve the form later.

### Strong grammar / weak fluency

- reduce written form drills;
- add timed planning + speaking;
- AI asks natural follow-up rather than grammar questions.

### Pronunciation barrier

- isolate high-value ending/cluster only after communication attempt;
- use listen → discriminate → produce → reinsert into story.

# 8. Teacher support

Teacher brief should identify:

- past-event comprehension;
- narrative sequence control;
- past question accuracy;
- regular `-ed` intelligibility;
- irregular-form patterns;
- speaking length;
- follow-up-question behavior;
- scaffolding dependence.

Teacher session should use new stories/problems rather than replay app examples.

# 9. Expert educator/mentor support

Escalation is appropriate when teachers need support with:

- deciding which past-tense errors are developmentally acceptable vs intervention priorities;
- persistent Dominican-Spanish pronunciation transfer;
- balancing fluency and correction;
- interpreting disagreement between AI evidence and teacher observation;
- calibrating A2 storytelling/performance rubrics.

# 10. Module exit evidence

Minimum bundle:

1. listening comprehension of a past event;
2. spoken recent-event account;
3. past-question interaction;
4. simple written past account;
5. regular `-ed` perception/production evidence;
6. AI story/problem conversation;
7. Create & Apply personal story;
8. delayed retrieval of early module targets;
9. at least one transferred use of A1 repair/question skills.

# 11. Acceptance criteria

A2 Module 1 is production-ready only if learners cannot complete it through recognition alone, AI does not substitute for learner narration, teacher support adds new performance evidence, and the system can distinguish grammar knowledge from communicative past-event performance.
