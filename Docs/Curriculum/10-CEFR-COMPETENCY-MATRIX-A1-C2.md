# CEFR Competency Matrix A1-C2

Status: Curriculum production specification
Owner: Lurexa Learning Technologies

## Purpose
This matrix defines the cross-level progression that every English course, assessment, lesson, teacher session and Coach experience must respect. It is intentionally competency-first: lessons teach competencies; competencies are not owned by one lesson.

## Skill domains
1. Listening
2. Speaking
3. Reading
4. Writing
5. Vocabulary
6. Grammar
7. Phonetics

Integrated modes: Conversation, Create & Apply, Pragmatics/Interaction.

## A1 — Foundations for Real Communication
Learner can manage highly familiar predictable communication with substantial support.

- Listening: recognize familiar words, personal information, numbers, times, short instructions and predictable exchanges.
- Speaking: introduce self/others, ask and answer basic questions, describe routine/family, complete basic transactions and give simple directions.
- Reading: understand signs, labels, forms, menus, schedules, short personal messages and simple descriptions.
- Writing: complete forms and produce short personal messages/descriptions of approximately 3-6 connected sentences.
- Vocabulary: high-frequency personal, family, routine, food, places, time, preferences and survival-language chunks.
- Grammar: be; subject pronouns; possessives; articles; present simple; basic questions; there is/are; basic prepositions; countability foundations; some/any; can for simple ability/request where appropriate.
- Phonetics: spelling/sound awareness, high-impact contrasts, final consonants, simple clusters, word stress, basic sentence stress and intelligible question/statement intonation.
- Conversation: sustain 30-90 seconds of supported exchange and use repair strategies such as repetition/clarification requests.
- Create & Apply: personal profile, self-introduction, routine, family presentation, simple roleplay and neighborhood map/task.

## A2 — Everyday Independence
Learner can manage familiar everyday situations with reduced dependence on scripts.

- Listening: understand short narratives, routine service encounters, travel/health/work exchanges and essential details in clear speech.
- Speaking: narrate simple past events, describe plans and preferences, compare options, give advice and maintain short everyday conversations.
- Reading: understand short articles, travel information, emails, notices, simple workplace/school texts and short narratives.
- Writing: produce connected paragraphs, messages, invitations, short narratives and functional emails.
- Vocabulary: travel, health, work/study, leisure, shopping/services, housing, relationships and common phrasal/collocational chunks.
- Grammar: past simple; present continuous; comparatives/superlatives; can/should/must foundations; future plans; basic present perfect introduction where justified; object pronouns; adverbs; expanded prepositions.
- Phonetics: consonant clusters, common vowel contrasts, sentence stress, basic linking, weak forms and common reductions.
- Conversation: sustain 2-4 minute familiar-topic interaction; ask follow-ups; clarify; repair misunderstandings.
- Create & Apply: itinerary, review, short story, advice recording, service simulation, short video or mini-presentation.

## B1 — Independent Communication
Learner can maintain connected communication across familiar social, study and work contexts.

- Listening: follow main points and relevant detail in clear standard speech, podcasts/interviews and short news or explanatory material.
- Speaking: narrate experiences, explain reasons, express/support opinions, describe goals/problems and participate in discussions.
- Reading: understand factual articles, opinion texts, instructions, blogs and accessible authentic materials; distinguish main ideas/supporting detail.
- Writing: produce organized paragraphs, narratives, opinions, explanations and practical professional messages.
- Vocabulary: topic expansion, common collocations, phrasal verbs, stance language, discourse chunks and useful word families.
- Grammar: relative clauses, first/second conditional foundations, reported speech, present perfect/past contrasts, modals, gerunds/infinitives and increasingly controlled tense/aspect choices.
- Phonetics: connected speech, weak forms, linking, rhythm, chunking, reductions and comprehensibility during spontaneous speech.
- Conversation: sustain 5-8 minute interactions, take turns, respond to others, disagree politely and recover when language fails.
- Create & Apply: interview, podcast, social campaign, problem-solving presentation, cultural report and collaborative task.

## B2 — Flexible Independent Communication
Learner can communicate with fluency and detail across personal, academic and professional contexts.

- Listening: follow extended speech, panel discussion, lectures and varied natural speech when the topic is reasonably accessible.
- Speaking: develop arguments, negotiate, present, speculate, explain complex relationships and respond spontaneously.
- Reading: interpret editorials, reports, research-oriented texts and complex argumentation; identify attitude, implication and organization.
- Writing: produce structured arguments, formal correspondence, reports, summaries and professional texts with appropriate register.
- Vocabulary: abstract topics, professional/academic collocation, nuance between near-synonyms and flexible paraphrase.
- Grammar: complex passive structures, mixed/advanced conditionals, complex connectors, advanced modals, embedded clauses and controlled variation.
- Phonetics: discourse chunking, contrastive stress, pacing, emphasis, natural interaction and tolerance of accent variation.
- Conversation: sustain extended spontaneous discussion, negotiation and debate; manage disagreement and clarification strategically.
- Create & Apply: proposal, negotiation, debate, formal presentation, report and multi-source synthesis task.

## C1 — Precision, Nuance and Sophistication
Learner can use English flexibly, precisely and effectively for demanding social, academic and professional purposes.

- Listening: understand extended complex discourse, implicit stance, varied accents and most natural-speed educated speech.
- Speaking: formulate nuanced arguments, adapt register, persuade, qualify claims and deliver sophisticated presentations.
- Reading: analyze complex essays, literature, research texts and rhetoric; infer stance, assumptions and implications.
- Writing: produce coherent analytical, persuasive, academic and professional texts with precise organization and register.
- Vocabulary: idiomaticity, connotation, register, rhetorical language, specialized terminology and advanced collocation.
- Grammar: advanced modal meaning, inversion, nominalization, information structure, complex clause control and stylistic variation.
- Phonetics: rhetorical intonation, pragmatic meaning, emphasis, pacing, professional delivery and nuanced prosody.
- Conversation: lead extended discussions, mediate viewpoints, adapt language strategically and respond to complex argumentation.
- Create & Apply: cultural commentary, research presentation, persuasive speech, analytical podcast, professional brief and project defense.

## C2 — Adaptive Mastery
Learner can interpret and produce highly nuanced language across unfamiliar, abstract and specialized contexts.

- Listening: understand virtually all spoken discourse, including rapid connected speech, implicit humor, rhetorical nuance and accent variation with occasional domain-specific gaps.
- Speaking: communicate spontaneously with precision, subtle distinction, stylistic flexibility and highly effective discourse management.
- Reading: synthesize dense specialized, literary, academic and abstract texts; interpret stylistic and rhetorical effects.
- Writing: produce sophisticated, audience-sensitive, precise texts across genres and specialized domains.
- Vocabulary: deep lexical networks, idiomatic control, metaphor, specialized language and deliberate register/style choices.
- Grammar: near-complete flexible control; grammar is primarily refined through discourse, style and rhetorical effect rather than isolated rule teaching.
- Phonetics: adaptive prosody, pacing, emphasis, pragmatic intonation, accent comprehension and context-sensitive delivery.
- Conversation: facilitate expert-level interaction, synthesize competing positions and shift register seamlessly.
- Create & Apply: capstone, expert panel, technical/professional report, seminar leadership and integrated research project.

## Progression rule
A higher CEFR label does not erase lower-level competencies. Later levels require increasingly independent, flexible and context-sensitive performance of earlier functions.

## Mastery states
introduced -> practiced -> demonstrated -> mastered -> retained

A competency may become `needs_revalidation` when later evidence shows decay.

## Implementation rule
Course content may use culturally different contexts while referencing stable competency IDs. Context is replaceable; competency history is persistent.
