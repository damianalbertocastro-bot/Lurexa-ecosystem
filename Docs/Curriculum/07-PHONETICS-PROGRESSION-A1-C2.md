# Phonetics Progression A1-C2

Status: MVP phonetics authority  
Shared relevance: Lurexa Learn + Lurexa Coach

## 1. Purpose

Phonetics is the seventh explicit English skill. It develops perception, intelligibility, rhythm, stress, connected speech, intonation and delivery. It does not pursue accent erasure.

## 2. Progression principle

Teach phonetics according to communicative impact, CEFR level and observed learner evidence. Avoid dumping an exhaustive phoneme syllabus into beginner courses.

## 3. A1 — Intelligible Foundations

Learner goals:

- understand that English spelling and sound do not always match;
- perceive/produce selected high-value contrasts;
- produce important final consonants;
- manage basic consonant clusters;
- recognize word stress;
- use basic sentence stress and question/statement intonation;
- produce core phrases intelligibly.

Priority examples for Spanish-speaking learners may include /ɪ/ vs /iː/, /θ/ and /ð/ when useful, final consonants, clusters and stress.

Suggested activity families:

- listen-and-choose;
- minimal/high-value contrast;
- mouth/visual cue where appropriate;
- record-and-compare;
- word stress tap/mark;
- final-sound challenge;
- short shadowing.

## 4. A2 — Connected Foundations

Learner goals:

- improve consonant-cluster control;
- recognize and use basic linking;
- develop sentence stress;
- perceive common reductions;
- connect listening perception to production;
- maintain intelligibility in short unscripted speech.

## 5. B1 — Rhythm and Fluency

Learner goals:

- recognize/produce common weak forms;
- use connected speech without sacrificing clarity;
- improve English rhythm;
- chunk longer utterances;
- use intonation for basic attitude and turn management;
- maintain intelligibility during spontaneous conversation.

## 6. B2 — Discourse Delivery

Learner goals:

- use contrastive stress;
- chunk discourse meaningfully;
- control pacing;
- understand a broader range of natural reductions;
- adapt intonation during discussion, negotiation and presentation;
- understand increasing speaker/accent variation.

## 7. C1 — Rhetorical and Pragmatic Control

Learner goals:

- use intonation to signal stance, implication and nuance;
- control rhetorical stress;
- deliver professional/academic speech effectively;
- vary pace and prominence intentionally;
- interpret subtle pragmatic meaning in spoken discourse.

## 8. C2 — Adaptive Phonetic Mastery

Learner goals:

- understand diverse accent variation with high flexibility;
- adapt register-sensitive delivery;
- control emphasis, pacing and intonation for rhetorical effect;
- interpret highly reduced/connected natural speech;
- use pronunciation flexibly across professional, academic and social contexts.

## 9. Learn vs Coach responsibility

Lurexa Learn teaches phonetics in curriculum context and generates evidence. Lurexa Coach provides deeper speaking/pronunciation practice, corrective cycles and targeted rehearsal.

Both should reference the same stable phonetics competencies and authorized learner context rather than maintain unrelated pronunciation profiles.

## 10. Feedback hierarchy

Prioritize:

1. intelligibility impact;
2. target feature relevance;
3. repeated pattern;
4. actionable correction;
5. immediate retry.

Do not overwhelm learners with every detectable difference from a native accent.

## 11. Evidence

Useful phonetics evidence can include:

- perception accuracy;
- production attempt;
- teacher observation;
- AI observation with confidence;
- intelligibility outcome;
- recurrence across tasks;
- successful correction;
- later retention.

## 12. UX pattern

A strong phonetics interaction often follows:

Hear → Notice → Discriminate → Produce → Receive focused feedback → Retry → Use in phrase/conversation.
