# Lurexa Correction & Feedback Policy

Status: Initial pedagogical policy — v0.2  
Applies to: Lurexa Coach, Lurexa Learn, teacher-facing recommendations  
Dependencies: `00-LINGUISTIC-INTELLIGENCE-OVERVIEW.md`, `01-ERROR-TAXONOMY.md`, `02-DOMINICAN-ENGLISH-ERROR-CORPUS.md`, `03-DOMINICAN-SPANISH-TRANSFER-CATALOG.md`, `../08-CONVERSATION-FRAMEWORK.md`

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines whether, when, how much, and how Lurexa should correct learner language. It does not define the error taxonomy or persistence architecture.

> **Do not correct an error merely because it occurred. Correct it when correction has greater pedagogical value than allowing communication to continue.**

## 2. Decision inputs

Correction decisions should consider:

- skill-specific CEFR level;
- task mode;
- communicative impact and actual breakdown;
- current lesson/session target;
- recurrence for this learner;
- self-correction evidence;
- likely ability to self-repair;
- interruption cost to fluency;
- error domain;
- recent correction history;
- learner correction preference when appropriate.

No single input controls the decision alone.

## 3. Timing

### Immediate
Use when communication breaks down, the exact target is being practiced in controlled work, pronunciation creates likely misunderstanding, a pragmatic problem has meaningful social consequences, the learner requests immediate correction, or repeated target errors risk reinforcing the wrong form.

### After the turn / short segment
Use when the error matters but meaning remains clear, guided conversation is underway, or the learner is likely to self-correct with a prompt.

### End of task
Use for fluency-focused stories, roleplays, explanations, debates, and other sustained production where interruption would reduce output.

### Monitor only
Use for acceptable variation, isolated low-impact errors, features above the learner's current level, recently overcorrected features, or lower-priority issues.

## 4. Default feedback quantity

- **A1:** normally one priority correction per turn/segment.
- **A2:** normally one or two per short segment.
- **B1:** normally one or two after a segment/task.
- **B2:** normally two or three targeted refinements after meaningful production.
- **C1-C2:** selective refinement of patterns, precision, naturalness, discourse, register, and high-value pronunciation.

Controlled accuracy tasks may justify more frequent correction.

## 5. CEFR philosophy

### A1
Protect willingness to speak, intelligibility, survival communication, basic sentence frames, and foundational sound distinctions. Prefer `MODEL_AND_REPEAT`, `RECAST`, brief explicit correction, and short retry. Avoid grammar lectures.

### A2
Add self-correction and stable control of common tense/question patterns. Use short explanations when needed. Prefer elicited repair, recast, model/repeat, and delayed feedback.

### B1
Protect sustained fluency while stabilizing known systems. Distinguish knowledge gaps from retrieval failures. Prioritize recurring features, productive vocabulary, repair, and automaticity. Delay most ordinary correction during fluency work.

### B2
Increase precision, lexical range, collocation, natural phrasing, pronunciation control, and register. Address persistent errors expected to be stabilizing, but do not correct acceptable accent variation.

### C1-C2
Refine expressive range, nuance, rhetorical control, collocation, register shifting, discourse, and communicatively valuable pronunciation. Close receptive-productive gaps rather than simply adding more advanced vocabulary.

## 6. Task-mode policy

- `CONTROLLED_ACCURACY`: correction may be immediate/frequent.
- `GUIDED_PRACTICE`: correct important target errors after item/turn; elicit self-repair where possible.
- `GUIDED_CONVERSATION`: let the thought finish unless communication fails; use recast or short-delay feedback.
- `FLUENCY_CONVERSATION`: collect observations; correct selectively afterward.
- `PRONUNCIATION_FOCUS`: immediate repeated correction is appropriate for the target feature; unrelated grammar is usually lower priority.
- `ASSESSMENT`: do not coach in ways that invalidate scoring.
- `FREE_PRODUCTION`: prioritize communication, recurrence, and learner goals.

## 7. Priority hierarchy

When several issues occur together, normally prioritize:

1. communication breakdown;
2. current target;
3. high-impact pronunciation/pragmatics;
4. stable recurring learner pattern;
5. high-frequency level-relevant grammar;
6. productive vocabulary/retrieval limitation;
7. naturalness/collocation;
8. isolated low-impact form errors.

Task mode may reorder this hierarchy.

## 8. Self-correction

Self-correction is positive evidence. Record the initial error when useful, record the successful repair, reduce correction intensity, and do not treat it as equivalent to an unrecognized repeated error.

## 9. Recurrence

A1-A2 learners may receive repeated brief correction for foundational high-frequency targets. At B1+, repeated recurrence should increasingly trigger targeted micropractice or scheduled review rather than identical correction every time.

## 10. Grammar vs pronunciation ambiguity

A spoken missing marker such as `-ed`, third-person `-s`, plural `-s`, or a final consonant may reflect grammar, pronunciation, or both. Use cross-modal evidence when possible:

- can the learner write/select the form?
- can the learner perceive it?
- can the learner repeat it accurately in isolation?
- does it fail across modalities?

Do not infer the cause from one spoken event.

## 11. Pronunciation correction principle

Pronunciation instruction targets intelligibility, perceptual discrimination, important lexical contrasts, stress, rhythm, connected speech, and professional/social clarity. It does not target accent erasure.

For true beginners, when spelling is likely to trigger Spanish decoding, prefer: `hear → discriminate → repeat → meaning → spelling`.

## 12. L1-aware explanations

Coach may use concise Spanish-aware contrasts when they clarify the target, but must avoid nationality-based assumptions.

Good: `Spanish uses tener for age. English uses be: I'm 25.`

Avoid: `Dominicans always say this wrong.`

## 13. Vocabulary and retrieval

When a learner uses basic vocabulary despite evidence that richer vocabulary is already known receptively, first support retrieval: cue recall, ask for reformulation, reuse the word in a new context, then test later spontaneous production.

Recognition is not productive mastery.

## 14. Advanced grammar

For B1-C2 spontaneous grammar failures, distinguish conceptual misunderstanding, incomplete form knowledge, retrieval failure, automaticity failure, and L1 fallback. If the learner knows the rule but cannot retrieve it, prefer retrieval/reformulation over reteaching the entire rule.

## 15. Feedback format

Default sequence:

1. acknowledge successful communication only when useful;
2. identify the priority feature(s);
3. show or elicit the preferred form;
4. explain briefly only if needed;
5. request retry when productive practice adds value;
6. return to meaningful communication or schedule review.

## 16. Evidence output

A correction event should be able to record:

- observation/pattern ID;
- intervention type;
- learner recognition;
- self-correction outcome;
- prompted retry outcome;
- later spontaneous use;
- recurrence/improvement.

Correction is part of an evidence loop, not a terminal message.

## 17. MVP requirements

Coach must support immediate versus delayed correction, level/task-sensitive dosage, recast, self-correction prompt, explicit correction, retry, fluency protection, recurrence memory, positive self-correction evidence, L1-aware explanations, and evidence output back to Lurexa Mind.
