# Dominican English Error Corpus

Status: Initial teacher-observed corpus — v0.1  
Applies to: Lurexa Mind, Lurexa Coach, Lurexa Learn, Lurexa Teach  
Parent taxonomy: `01-ERROR-TAXONOMY.md`

## 1. Purpose

This corpus normalizes recurring English-learning patterns reported from Dominican classrooms. It is not a list of stereotypes and must not be used to infer that an individual learner has a pattern without learner-specific evidence.

Core rule:

> Population knowledge generates hypotheses. Learner evidence generates personalization.

All entries in this first version are derived from Damian classroom observations unless otherwise stated. Teacher observations and transfer explanations are stored separately: an observed learner form is evidence; an L1 explanation is an interpretation.

## 2. Corpus interpretation rules

- Do not treat every Dominican learner as exhibiting every pattern below.
- Do not treat a population pattern as proof of individual causation.
- Do not correct every occurrence automatically; correction behavior belongs to the correction policy.
- Preserve distinctions among pronunciation, grammar, fluency, vocabulary, naturalness, pragmatics and transfer.
- Recognition of a form is not equivalent to productive control.
- Self-correction is evidence of stronger control than an unrecognized error.

## 3. Initial normalized patterns

### DO-ENG-PRO-001 — Spanish grapheme-to-sound decoding of written English

- **Learner behavior:** true-beginner learners may pronounce unfamiliar written English using Spanish letter-to-sound expectations.
- **Primary domain:** E01 Pronunciation & Phonology
- **Subdomain:** OTHER_PHONOLOGICAL
- **Observation type:** TEACHER_OBSERVATION / PRONUNCIATION_TASK
- **Target relationship:** DEVELOPMENTAL
- **Typical CEFR relevance:** A1, especially true beginners
- **Communicative impact:** CI1-CI2 depending on word
- **Correction priority:** P2_MEDIUM; higher during pronunciation focus or when intelligibility is affected
- **Evidence strength:** EV2_RECURRENTLY_TEACHER_OBSERVED
- **Transfer:** ORTHOGRAPHIC_TRANSFER / PHONOLOGICAL_TRANSFER
- **Transfer status:** TEACHER_SUSPECTED
- **Population class:** GENERAL_SPANISH_TRANSFER with Dominican classroom salience
- **Provenance:** DAMIAN_CLASSROOM_OBSERVATION
- **Pedagogical note:** establish an auditory representation of new words before relying heavily on spelling when feasible.

## 4. Related teacher observations not yet normalized as error patterns

The following are valuable but should remain separate from error records until their role is clearer:

- Low voluntary reading engagement may reduce opportunities for repeated incidental vocabulary exposure.
- Learners often need repeated contextual encounters before new vocabulary becomes productively available.
- Advanced learners may understand more sophisticated vocabulary than they spontaneously use.
- English pronunciation learning requires learners to build a distinct sound/spelling system rather than apply Dominican Spanish expectations directly.
- Cultural and pragmatic adaptation matters, but the goal is communicative flexibility and intelligibility, not accent erasure or imitation of one prestige culture.

## 5. First Coach implications derived from the corpus

These are provisional and will be governed by the dedicated correction policy:

1. Do not infer a grammar problem when a spoken inflection may instead be a pronunciation failure.
2. Do not infer lack of vocabulary knowledge when the problem may be productive retrieval.
3. At true beginner level, consider sound-first presentation for pronunciation-sensitive vocabulary.
4. Use repeated retrieval and contextual reuse for B1+ vocabulary growth rather than recognition-only practice.
5. Distinguish self-corrected slips from unrecognized recurring errors.
6. Use population transfer knowledge to select what to monitor, never to pre-label an individual learner.

## 6. Next corpus expansion priorities

Future teacher observations should especially target areas currently underrepresented:

- articles and determiners;
- prepositions;
- false friends and lexical calques;
- natural Dominican-to-English expressions;
- pragmatic directness, requests and disagreement;
- listening failures caused by reductions and connected speech;
- discourse organization and storytelling;
- word stress, sentence stress and rhythm;
- final consonant weakening beyond regular past `-ed`;
- writing-specific transfer patterns.

The corpus should expand only when new observations add real coverage or stronger evidence; quantity alone is not the goal.
