# Dominican Spanish → English Transfer Catalog

Status: Initial pedagogical transfer catalog — v0.1  
Applies to: Lurexa Mind, Lurexa Coach, Lurexa Learn, Lurexa Teach  
Dependencies: `00-LINGUISTIC-INTELLIGENCE-OVERVIEW.md`, `01-ERROR-TAXONOMY.md`, `02-DOMINICAN-ENGLISH-ERROR-CORPUS.md`

## 1. Purpose

This catalog describes plausible Spanish/Dominican Spanish → English transfer relationships that may help Lurexa notice, explain, and prioritize learner language patterns.

It is a hypothesis layer, not a learner diagnosis.

> Population knowledge generates hypotheses. Learner evidence generates personalization.

## 2. Transfer safety rule

A learner's Dominican or Spanish-speaking background may influence what Lurexa monitors. It must never be used to assert that an individual learner has a feature without learner-specific evidence.

Every transfer claim must carry a status such as `TEACHER_SUSPECTED`, `SUPPORTED_BY_RECURRENT_EVIDENCE`, or `RESEARCH_SUPPORTED_POPULATION_PATTERN`.

## 3. Orthography and phonology

### TR-PHO-001 — Spanish grapheme-to-sound decoding applied to English

- **Observed relationship:** beginners may pronounce unfamiliar written English using Spanish letter-to-sound expectations.
- **Likely mechanism:** Spanish orthography is relatively transparent compared with English; familiar decoding habits are reused.
- **Corpus links:** `DO-ENG-PRO-001`, `DO-ENG-PRO-003`, `DO-ENG-PRO-005`.
- **Typical relevance:** A1-A2.
- **Coach implication:** establish sound before spelling when useful: `hear → discriminate → repeat → meaning → spelling`.

### TR-PHO-002 — Initial vowel insertion before English s + consonant clusters

- **Observed relationship:** production resembling `estudent` for `student`.
- **Likely mechanism:** adaptation of unfamiliar English onset structure to a Spanish-compatible syllable pattern.
- **Corpus link:** `DO-ENG-PRO-002`.
- **Typical relevance:** A1-A2; persistence possible.

### TR-PHO-003 — English vowel-category compression/substitution

- **Observed relationship:** unfamiliar English vowel distinctions may be mapped onto a smaller set of familiar Spanish vowel categories.
- **Corpus links:** `DO-ENG-PRO-003`, `DO-ENG-PRO-004`.
- **Typical relevance:** A1-B1.
- **Coach implication:** perception and production should be treated separately; spelling alone is insufficient evidence.

### TR-PHO-004 — TH perception/production substitution

- **Observed relationship:** difficulty perceiving or producing /θ/ and /ð/ may lead to substitutions with more familiar consonants.
- **Corpus links:** `DO-ENG-PRO-007`, `DO-ENG-PRO-008`.
- **Typical relevance:** A1-B1; may persist later.
- **Coach implication:** diagnose `cannot hear` versus `cannot produce` before selecting practice.

### TR-PHO-005 — Final morphology may be weakened in speech

- **Observed relationship:** past `-ed`, third-person `-s`, plural/final consonants may be difficult to hear or produce reliably.
- **Important caution:** a missing audible marker may reflect grammar, phonology, or both.
- **Corpus links:** `DO-ENG-PRO-006`, `DO-ENG-GRA-003`.
- **Coach implication:** use cross-modal evidence before labeling the cause.

## 4. Grammar and sentence construction

### TR-GRA-001 — Translation-mediated sentence assembly

- **Observed relationship:** learners may formulate a message in Spanish first and translate components into English.
- **Possible outcomes:** Spanish-like word order, missing English auxiliaries, literal lexical choices, slowed production.
- **Corpus link:** `DO-ENG-GRA-001`.
- **Typical relevance:** A1-B1; residual use possible at B2.
- **Coach implication:** teach and recycle English chunks/frames rather than correcting only the final translated sentence.

### TR-GRA-002 — Do/does omission in questions

- **Observed relationship:** learners may rely on declarative order plus intonation rather than English `do/does` support.
- **Corpus link:** `DO-ENG-GRA-002`.
- **Typical relevance:** A1-A2.
- **Coach implication:** prioritize productive question frames and rapid retrieval, not terminology-heavy explanation.

### TR-GRA-003 — Third-person singular morphology instability

- **Observed relationship:** `She work`, `My mother watch TV`.
- **Cause status:** not assumed to be purely transfer; may reflect general L2 developmental morphology and/or weak final consonant production.
- **Corpus link:** `DO-ENG-GRA-003`.
- **Coach implication:** distinguish knowledge, automaticity, and audibility.

### TR-GRA-004 — Advanced-grammar fallback under pressure

- **Observed relationship:** B1-B2 learners may know conditionals, aspect, modifiers, or complex structures in exercises but revert to simpler or Spanish-organized forms in spontaneous speech.
- **Likely mechanism:** retrieval/automaticity limits plus L1 fallback.
- **Typical relevance:** B1-C1.
- **Coach implication:** use reformulation, retrieval prompts, and repeated spontaneous reuse before reteaching the whole rule.

## 5. Lexical and semantic transfer

### TR-LEX-001 — Spanish-equivalent overmapping

- **Observed relationship:** learners may treat two English words as interchangeable because both map to one familiar Spanish translation.
- **Typical relevance:** B1-C2.
- **Coach implication:** teach semantic boundaries, collocations, register, and example contrasts rather than one-word translations.

### TR-LEX-002 — Familiar-looking English words trigger Spanish pronunciation or meaning

- **Observed relationship:** cognate-like or orthographically familiar English forms may activate Spanish pronunciation or an overly broad Spanish meaning.
- **Corpus link:** `DO-ENG-PRO-005`.
- **Coach implication:** distinguish positive cognate transfer from false-friend or pronunciation interference.

## 6. Pragmatic transfer

### TR-PRA-001 — Direct request forms

- **Observed relationship:** early learners may use bare imperatives where English teaching targets a conventional request form.
- **Alternative explanation:** limited grammatical repertoire may be more important than cultural transfer.
- **Corpus link:** `DO-ENG-PRA-001`.
- **Status:** unresolved; do not label as Dominican-specific without stronger evidence.

## 7. Translation as a processing strategy

Translation is not automatically an error. At early levels it may be an ordinary scaffold. It becomes pedagogically relevant when it repeatedly:

- slows comprehension or speech;
- produces literal English structures;
- blocks direct lexical retrieval;
- prevents automatic use of known English chunks.

Coach should progressively reduce dependence on translation rather than prohibit it categorically.

## 8. Culture and pronunciation

Learning English pronunciation involves more than individual consonants and vowels. Learners also build new expectations about:

- rhythm;
- stress;
- reductions;
- intonation;
- turn-taking;
- emphasis;
- politeness and stance.

Lurexa should teach these as communicative resources, not as requirements to erase Dominican identity or imitate one prestige accent.

## 9. Population classification

Each pattern should eventually be labeled as one of:

- `DOMINICAN_SPECIFIC_CANDIDATE`
- `DOMINICAN_SALIENCE_HIGH`
- `GENERAL_SPANISH_TRANSFER`
- `GENERAL_ESL_PATTERN`
- `UNRESOLVED`

A teacher-observed Dominican classroom pattern is not automatically Dominican-specific.

## 10. Research upgrade path

Teacher observations in this catalog are a starting evidence source. Future versions should distinguish:

1. Damian classroom observation;
2. multi-teacher observation;
3. Lurexa learner-corpus evidence;
4. external SLA/linguistic research;
5. convergent evidence from several sources.

Research support may strengthen a population hypothesis but still does not prove individual causation.

## 11. Product behavior

Population transfer knowledge may influence:

- what Coach listens for;
- which contrasts it tests;
- which explanation it selects;
- what pronunciation activities Learn recommends;
- what teacher briefs surface.

It must not directly create a durable learner weakness record without learner evidence.
