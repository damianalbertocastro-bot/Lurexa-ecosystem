# Lesson 1 — First Hello
Estimated core time: 25-30 minutes
Mission: Greet someone, say your name and respond naturally when meeting a person for the first time.

## Stage 1 — Hook: First Day
UX: 10-15 second animated/photo sequence of two young adults meeting in a familiar Dominican/Caribbean study or work context.
Prompt: `You arrive at a new English class. What do you think they say first?`
Interaction: choose between 3 short audio greetings.
Evidence: prediction only; no mastery impact.

## Stage 2 — Vocabulary Builder: Words for a First Meeting
Interactive cards:
1. Hello / Hi
2. Good morning
3. Good afternoon
4. Good evening
5. My name is...
6. I'm...
7. Nice to meet you.
8. Bye / Goodbye

Card progression:
- image/context + audio;
- tap meaning/use;
- repeat into microphone;
- retrieve from context image;
- choose appropriate greeting by time/situation.

Avoid fixed front/back cards.

## Stage 3 — Listen: Meeting at Class
Audio dialogue, natural but A1-controlled:
A: Hi! I'm Ana. What's your name?
B: Hello. I'm Luis.
A: Nice to meet you, Luis.
B: Nice to meet you, too.

First listen: Who is meeting for the first time?
Second listen: What are their names?
Micro-listen: `I'm Ana` / `What's your name?` / `Nice to meet you.`

## Stage 4 — Notice the Language
Highlight:
- I am -> I'm
- What is -> What's
- My name is...
Explain contraction as spoken/written everyday English, not as a long grammar lecture.

## Stage 5 — Phonetics Lab: Clear First Words
Targets:
- /h/ in hello (avoid dropping it when it affects intelligibility);
- stress in HEL-lo and NICE to MEET you;
- final consonant awareness in `meet`.
Interactions:
- listen/choose;
- tap stressed syllable;
- record phrase;
- compare with model;
- retry after one actionable feedback point.

## Stage 6 — Guided Speaking: Build Your Greeting
Prompt builder:
`Hi. I'm ____. Nice to meet you.`
Learner records once with text visible, then again with text partially hidden.

## Stage 7 — Conversation: Meet Sofia
AI role: Sofia, another A1 learner at a new class in Santo Domingo.
Goal: greet, give name, respond to `Nice to meet you`, and close conversation.
Maximum target: 4-6 turns.
AI behavior: do not introduce advanced questions; if learner struggles, offer one phrase option rather than taking over.

## Stage 8 — Create & Apply: My 15-Second Hello
Learner creates a short voice introduction suitable for meeting someone new.
Teacher extension: assign students to exchange greetings in a live meeting and introduce one partner to the group later in Module 1.

## Stage 9 — Review
- audio recognition: choose greeting;
- retrieval: image -> say phrase;
- reorder: `name / My / is / Carla`;
- one spoken response.

## Stage 10 — Quiz
6 items:
1. audio greeting recognition;
2. context/time greeting choice;
3. complete `My ___ is...`;
4. choose natural reply to `Nice to meet you`;
5. identify correct `I'm` meaning/use;
6. oral: greet and say your name.

Quiz is formative. Retry permitted after feedback.

## Lesson evidence
- vocabulary recognition/retrieval;
- listening detail;
- short oral production;
- phonetics attempt metadata;
- conversation task completion.

# Lesson 2 — What’s Your Name?
Estimated time: 25-30 minutes
Mission: Ask for and give a person's name, and ask for repetition when needed.

## Vocabulary Builder
first name, last name, full name, spell, please, again, repeat, sorry, I don't understand.

## Listening
Two short dialogues with names that may be unfamiliar to the learner.
Global: identify relationship/context.
Detail: capture names.
Micro-listen: `Could you say that again?` may be introduced as a useful chunk even if grammar is above A1 analysis; primary survival frame remains `Again, please` / `Can you repeat that?`.

## Grammar/Function
- What's your name?
- My name is...
- I'm...
- What's his/her name? introduced only if needed for recognition, not full mastery yet.

## Phonetics
Name spelling and alphabet preview; final /m/ in name; clear question intonation without exaggeration.

## Conversation
AI deliberately gives one unfamiliar name so learner must use a clarification strategy.
Success requires at least one repair move if triggered.

## Create & Apply
Record a two-role mini-dialogue or complete it live with a teacher/partner.

## Quiz/review
Include one listening item where learner must request or choose a clarification phrase.

# Lesson 3 — Where Are You From?
Estimated time: 30-35 minutes
Mission: Say your city/country and ask another person where they are from.

## Vocabulary Builder
Dominican Republic, Dominican, Santo Domingo, Santiago, Caribbean, country, city, from; plus a small international set such as Colombia/Colombian, Mexico/Mexican, United States/American, Spain/Spanish, Brazil/Brazilian.

Do not overload nationality morphology. Prioritize communicative chunks.

## Listening
Short introductions from 3 speakers with varied but accessible accents.
Tasks:
- country/city match;
- identify one key detail;
- listen again after transcript support.

## Grammar
- I'm from...
- Where are you from?
- I am/I'm + nationality when appropriate.
- be question/statement pattern in context.

## Phonetics
Word stress in selected countries/nationalities; syllable awareness; contrast spelling vs sound.

## Conversation
Scenario: meet someone at a Caribbean youth/professional event or online class.
Goal: exchange name + city/country + one closing phrase.

## Create & Apply
Create a 20-30 second `Where I'm From` audio card with optional image/map context.

# Lesson 4 — Meet Someone New
Estimated time: 30-40 minutes
Purpose: unit integration and performance.

## Warm retrieval
No notes initially. Retrieve 8-10 Unit 1 expressions through image/audio prompts.

## Listening challenge
A short first-meeting dialogue with name, city/country and one repair request.

## Reading micro-task
Read a simple profile/chat introduction and identify key personal information.

## Conversation mission
AI or human partner conversation, target 60-90 seconds:
- greet;
- exchange names;
- exchange origin;
- respond naturally;
- request clarification if needed;
- close.

Scaffolding levels:
1. full phrase support;
2. keyword prompts;
3. independent attempt.

## Unit phonetics check
Sample 3-4 high-value phrases for intelligibility. Do not issue an accent score. Flag patterns as observations with confidence.

## Create & Apply
`My First English Connection`: learner records or performs a complete mini-conversation. Teacher can assess with a short rubric.

## Unit rubric
- Task achievement: completes required exchange
- Comprehensibility: message understandable
- Interaction: responds/asks appropriately
- Target language: key Unit 1 frames
- Phonetics: target phrases intelligible

Use level-appropriate performance descriptors rather than demanding native-like accuracy.

## Unit vocabulary wrap
Round 1 recognition
Round 2 recall
Round 3 audio recognition
Round 4 pronunciation
Round 5 contextual use
Round 6 conversation use

## Unit evidence outcome
Lurexa may update evidence for each competency. Unit completion does not automatically mark competencies mastered.

## Recommended next-step logic
- Strong performance -> Unit 2
- Weak listening but strong speaking -> Unit 2 + listening retrieval recommendation
- Repeated pronunciation issue -> Unit 2 + Coach/phonetics micro-practice if accessible
- Significant breakdown across core functions -> brief Unit 1 targeted review, not full forced restart.
