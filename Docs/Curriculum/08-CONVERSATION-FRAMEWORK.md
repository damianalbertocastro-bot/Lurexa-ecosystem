# Conversation Framework

Status: MVP conversation authority  
Applies to: Lurexa Learn, Lurexa Coach, teacher-led practice

## 1. Purpose

Conversation is an integrated learning mode that combines speaking, listening, vocabulary retrieval, grammar retrieval, phonetics, pragmatics, fluency, turn-taking and repair strategies.

Conversation is not an eighth language skill. It is where multiple skills become usable communication.

## 2. Required progression

Conversation should normally progress through:

**Guided Conversation → Scenario Conversation → Independent Conversation**

Scaffolding decreases as proficiency and evidence increase.

## 3. Conversation modes

- Learner ↔ AI
- Learner ↔ Teacher
- Learner ↔ Learner
- Learner ↔ Group

Each mode serves different purposes. AI offers repetition and immediacy; human interaction adds unpredictability, judgment, social meaning and coaching.

## 4. AI conversation rule

Do not use generic prompts such as “Practice English with the AI” as the default.

Use scenario-driven tasks with:

- learner role;
- counterpart role;
- communicative goal;
- level-controlled language;
- target competencies;
- completion criteria;
- appropriate scaffold level;
- feedback timing.

Example A2:

> You are checking into a hotel. Ask about your reservation, breakfast, Wi-Fi and checkout time.

## 5. AI behavior

The AI should:

- stay in role unless safety or pedagogical needs require otherwise;
- adapt to the learner’s level using authorized context;
- avoid unnecessary advanced vocabulary;
- allow productive struggle;
- avoid interrupting every error;
- recognize successful communication;
- prioritize high-value errors;
- provide concise post-segment feedback;
- allow retries;
- reuse current lesson language naturally.

## 6. Feedback timing

Default correction modes:

- beginner controlled practice: after turn when needed;
- guided conversation: after short segment;
- fluency practice: after task;
- communication-critical misunderstanding: immediate clarification.

## 7. CEFR conversation progression

### A1

- greetings;
- introductions;
- personal questions;
- basic clarification;
- ordering/requesting;
- simple directions;
- 30-90 second supported interaction.

### A2

- routine social exchanges;
- travel/service situations;
- simple past experiences;
- invitations/plans;
- advice and everyday problem solving.

### B1

- sustained familiar-topic conversation;
- narration;
- opinions/reasons;
- clarification and repair;
- problem solving;
- simple disagreement.

### B2

- spontaneous extended interaction;
- negotiation;
- structured debate;
- professional/academic discussion;
- nuanced agreement/disagreement.

### C1

- complex argumentation;
- facilitation;
- persuasion;
- culturally nuanced discussion;
- professional and academic interaction;
- rhetorical stance.

### C2

- expert discussion;
- subtle interpretation;
- register shifting;
- moderation;
- abstract and specialized discourse;
- high-flexibility interaction across accents and contexts.

## 8. Cultural adaptation

Use culturally relevant scenarios without limiting the learner to local contexts. Progress from familiar Dominican/Caribbean settings toward Latin American and international communication.

Example progression for travel:

local route/planning → Caribbean trip → airport/hotel → international problem-solving.

## 9. Conversation evidence

Useful evidence may include:

- task completion;
- number/quality of turns;
- response length;
- comprehension success;
- clarification strategy use;
- fluency;
- vocabulary retrieval;
- grammar target use;
- pronunciation/intelligibility;
- interaction management;
- later transfer.

Do not reduce conversation to one opaque score.

## 10. Human teacher use

Live sessions should use async evidence to select conversation priorities. Teachers may receive AI-generated briefs and suggested scenarios, but they may modify the task and remain responsible for high-value human judgment.

## 11. Create & Apply integration

Conversation can culminate in production such as:

- roleplay recording;
- interview;
- debate;
- presentation + Q&A;
- panel;
- collaborative problem-solving output.

## 12. Conversation and Lurexa remembers

If a learner repeatedly avoids extended answers, struggles with a repair phrase, or demonstrates improved fluency, relevant evidence should inform later Learn, Coach and teacher experiences through approved Learner Model contracts.

## 13. MVP requirement

The first A1 production slice should include at least one guided AI/simulated conversation scenario with a clear goal, level control, structured feedback, retry, and evidence output.
