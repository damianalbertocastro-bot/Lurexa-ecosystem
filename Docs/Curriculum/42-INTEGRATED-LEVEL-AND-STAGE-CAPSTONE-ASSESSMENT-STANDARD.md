# Integrated Level and Stage Capstone Assessment Standard

Status: Authoritative curriculum assessment requirement
Applies to: Lurexa Learn A1-C2 and Lurexa Teach T1-T5
Purpose: verify that completion of a level or professional stage is supported by integrated performance evidence rather than course completion alone

## 1. Governing rule

Every learner language level and every teacher-development stage must end with a **general integrated capstone project**.

The capstone is not a ceremonial final assignment. It is a level/stage-exit evidence bundle designed to answer:

> Can this person independently use the major competencies expected at this level/stage in a coherent authentic or realistic performance?

A learner or teacher may complete all modules without yet passing the capstone. Module completion and level/stage competence are separate decisions.

## 2. Assessment architecture

A capstone must:

1. integrate competencies from across the whole level/stage rather than repeat the final module only;
2. require productive performance, not selected response alone;
3. include at least one unfamiliar or meaningfully varied transfer context;
4. preserve first-attempt evidence separately from scaffolded revision;
5. include opportunities for feedback and improvement without erasing independent-performance evidence;
6. include delayed or spaced retrieval from earlier modules;
7. use more than one evidence source where the competence is multidimensional;
8. record competency IDs, evidence provenance, curriculum version and assessment conditions;
9. distinguish AI assistance, teacher/mentor assistance and independent work;
10. generate a level/stage decision with confidence and remaining development priorities.

No single composite score should hide a serious competence gap. Critical productive competencies may function as minimum gates.

## 3. Language-level capstone dimensions

English A1-C2 capstones should sample the seven explicit skills:

- Listening
- Speaking
- Reading
- Writing
- Vocabulary
- Grammar
- Phonetics

They must also integrate, at level-appropriate depth:

- Conversation / interpersonal interaction
- Create & Apply / presentational performance
- Mediation
- Online interaction
- Pragmatic competence
- Communication strategies
- Plurilingual/pluricultural competence
- Delayed retrieval and transfer

The capstone does not require equal weighting of every dimension. Weighting follows the level mission and competency model.

## 4. English A1-C2 capstone sequence

### A1 — My Life, My English

Mission: demonstrate basic functional communication across highly familiar personal and everyday situations.

Integrated project scenario: create a simple personal profile/portfolio and use it in a short real-life communication sequence.

Required evidence should include:

- understand a short introduction or practical message;
- read simple personal/functional information;
- introduce oneself and answer/ask basic questions;
- complete a short supported conversation with clarification/repair;
- produce an intelligible recorded introduction;
- write a short personal profile/message;
- relay one simple practical detail to another person;
- complete one simple online/digital exchange;
- retrieve earlier A1 targets without seeing the original model.

Decision focus: functional independence with predictable language, not grammatical perfection.

### A2 — Living More Independently in English

Mission: manage routine everyday, travel, service, social and work/study-adjacent situations with growing independence.

Integrated project scenario: plan and complete an everyday-life challenge such as a short trip/day plan involving scheduling, service interaction, a problem, a social exchange and a follow-up message.

Required evidence includes past/present/future reference, routine problem solving, practical listening/reading, connected speaking/writing, mediation of simple information and spontaneous follow-up.

### B1 — My Voice in English

Mission: demonstrate independent connected communication across familiar personal, work/study, media, cultural and problem-solving contexts.

Integrated project scenario: investigate or reflect on a meaningful familiar issue/experience, produce a short multimedia explanation or presentation, participate in discussion, respond to questions and complete an associated written/digital artifact.

Required evidence includes connected narration/explanation, supported opinion, sustained conversation, practical mediation, reading/listening synthesis, connected writing, pronunciation/fluency in spontaneous speech and transfer from earlier levels.

### B2 — From Opinion to Impact

Mission: demonstrate flexible independent communication, supported argument, synthesis, negotiation and audience-aware register.

Integrated project scenario: develop and defend a proposal or recommendation for a real or realistic problem.

Required evidence includes multisource comprehension, structured argument in speaking/writing, negotiation or constructive disagreement, source synthesis, audience adaptation, mediation, professional/online interaction and spontaneous defense.

### C1 — Language, Identity and Global Perspective

Mission: demonstrate precise, nuanced, strategically adaptable communication across demanding professional, academic, cultural or public contexts.

Integrated project scenario: produce a research-informed analysis/recommendation for a defined audience and facilitate a discussion around it.

Required evidence includes demanding-source synthesis, nuanced written analysis, professional presentation, facilitated discussion, perspective mediation, register/pragmatic control, rhetorical delivery and uncertainty management.

### C2 — English Without a Script

Mission: demonstrate adaptive mastery across unfamiliar, high-complexity, cross-domain and multi-audience communication.

Integrated project scenario: complete an independent mastery challenge in which the learner must research, interpret, create, defend, mediate and transform a substantial idea/artifact for more than one audience or register.

Required evidence includes subtle listening/reading interpretation, spontaneous expert-level interaction, sophisticated writing, complex synthesis, expert-to-nonspecialist mediation, register transformation, broad variety comprehension and performance in at least one unfamiliar context.

C2 is not native-speaker imitation and does not require accent erasure.

## 5. Teacher-development T1-T5 capstones

Teacher stage capstones use the same principle: professional competence must be demonstrated through authentic or high-fidelity simulated performance.

### T1 — The First Coherent Lesson

Teacher designs, teaches/simulates, assesses and reflects on one complete learner-centered lesson.

Must demonstrate foundational planning, sequencing, learner activity, basic language/skill teaching, interaction, evidence collection, feedback and reflection.

### T2 — Improve a Learning Cycle

Teacher identifies a real or simulated learner-performance weakness, redesigns the learning cycle, implements/rehearses the intervention and evaluates resulting evidence.

Must integrate differentiation, pronunciation/speaking support, formative assessment, communicative task design, async/blended learning and reflective adjustment where relevant.

### T3 — Evidence-Driven Unit

Teacher designs and implements/simulates a multi-lesson competency-based unit, collects and interprets evidence, facilitates sustained learning and revises the design.

Must demonstrate competency mapping, integrated assessment, diagnostic reasoning, advanced interaction, curriculum/material evaluation and responsible AI use.

### T4 — Program Improvement Project

Teacher leads an evidence-based improvement initiative affecting a wider learning program, assessment system, curriculum area or teacher group.

Must demonstrate curriculum architecture, calibration, coaching/observation, learning analytics, intervention design, inquiry and program-level judgment.

### T5 — Professional Learning System

Teacher leader designs, facilitates and evaluates a professional-development system or initiative for educators.

Must demonstrate adult-learning design, teacher-development competency mapping, facilitation/training, mentoring, quality leadership, evidence interpretation and sustainable professional knowledge creation.

## 6. AI, teacher and mentor roles

AI may support planning questions, rehearsal, simulated interlocutors/learners, feedback and evidence organization. It must not create the final evidence on behalf of the learner/teacher or independently award level/stage competence.

Teachers/mentors may provide authorized feedback and validation. The record must preserve what was independently demonstrated before human support.

For high-stakes or ambiguous professional evidence, Expert Educator/Mentor review may be required.

## 7. Capstone decision model

Recommended result states:

- `READY` — evidence supports the level/stage exit claim;
- `READY_WITH_TARGETS` — exit claim is supported but specific competencies remain active development targets;
- `MORE_EVIDENCE_NEEDED` — evidence is incomplete or confidence is insufficient;
- `TARGETED_REVALIDATION` — one or more critical competencies need another independent performance;
- `NOT_YET_READY` — integrated performance does not yet meet the level/stage standard.

The system should return the learner/teacher to targeted learning rather than forcing complete repetition of the level.

## 8. Portfolio and evidence bundle

Each final capstone should persist:

- project brief/version;
- target level/stage;
- sampled competency IDs;
- raw/derived evidence references;
- first-attempt and revised artifacts;
- AI assistance provenance;
- teacher/mentor observations where applicable;
- rubric/decision dimensions;
- retrieval/transfer evidence;
- final readiness state;
- confidence/limitations;
- next development recommendations.

## 9. Release requirement

A learner level or Teach stage may not be represented as curriculum-complete unless:

1. its integrated capstone design exists;
2. required competency coverage is mapped;
3. the capstone can be executed through the product/runtime or governed assessment workflow;
4. representative pilot/calibration evidence exists;
5. no critical competency can be bypassed by weak evidence;
6. the final decision can route the person to targeted revalidation rather than all-or-nothing repetition.

## 10. Future-language rule

Every future target-language program must have an equivalent integrated level-exit capstone architecture appropriate to that language's competency model.

The universal requirement is integrated authentic performance. The exact tasks, linguistic systems, cultural contexts and evidence weights are language-specific.
