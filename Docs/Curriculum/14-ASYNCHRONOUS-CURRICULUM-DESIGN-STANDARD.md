# Lurexa Asynchronous Curriculum Design Standard

Status: Authoritative design standard  
Applies to: all self-paced Lurexa Learn curriculum and teacher-development learning objects

## 1. Purpose

This document defines what makes a Lurexa lesson genuinely suitable for asynchronous, self-timed learning. Uploading classroom materials to an LMS is not asynchronous curriculum design.

Every lesson must be able to teach, check, support, and move the learner forward without assuming a teacher is present in real time.

## 2. Design test

A learning object is not production-ready for asynchronous delivery unless a learner can answer:

- What am I trying to learn or do?
- What should I do first?
- Why was my response weak or successful?
- What should I try next?
- When am I finished?
- What happens if I am stuck?
- What should I review later?

## 3. Required asynchronous lesson qualities

### Clear mission

Every lesson begins with a learner-readable performance goal.

### Short interaction cycles

Prefer:

**Input → Action → Feedback → Retry/Production**

Avoid long passive explanation chains.

### Progressive scaffolding

Support must decrease as the learner demonstrates competence.

### Meaningful feedback

Do not rely on answer keys alone. Feedback should explain the highest-value issue at the learner's level.

### Recovery from difficulty

Every critical task needs a defined help path: retry, hint, explanation, example, additional practice, or teacher escalation.

### Resumability

A learner should be able to stop and return without losing orientation.

### Retrieval

Previously taught material must reappear after delay.

### Productive evidence

Speaking, writing, conversation and pronunciation cannot be inferred solely from multiple-choice completion.

## 4. Recommended lesson architecture

A complete asynchronous lesson normally contains:

1. Hook and mission
2. Prior-knowledge activation
3. Vocabulary/input preparation
4. Contextual input
5. Comprehension check
6. Language noticing
7. Focused explanation
8. Guided practice
9. AI-assisted or simulated application
10. Independent production
11. Create & Apply or transfer
12. Retrieval review
13. Formative assessment
14. Reflection/next step
15. Evidence update

Not every lesson requires identical screens or timing.

## 5. Session-size design

Learners may study in fragmented time. Design content in meaningful segments, normally around 5-15 minutes of coherent activity, while allowing longer integrated tasks where the task itself requires sustained attention.

Avoid making a 45-90 minute uninterrupted session the hidden requirement for normal progress.

## 6. Entry and return behavior

When a learner returns after a short gap:

- resume at the last meaningful checkpoint;
- briefly reactivate the target if needed.

After a longer gap:

- use a short retrieval check before continuing;
- surface forgotten prerequisites efficiently;
- avoid forcing full lesson repetition unless evidence supports it.

## 7. Feedback standard

Feedback should normally contain one or more of:

- confirmation of successful meaning/task completion;
- concise explanation of the issue;
- contrast with the learner response;
- level-appropriate rule or pattern;
- next attempt instruction;
- focused micro-practice.

Avoid excessive explanation after every correct response.

## 8. AI dependency rule

AI may substantially improve asynchronous learning, but the curriculum must define what the AI is supposed to accomplish.

Each AI-enabled activity should declare:

- pedagogical role;
- target competencies;
- acceptable support level;
- correction timing;
- evidence expected;
- conditions for escalation;
- prohibited shortcuts.

Do not place a generic chatbot beside a lesson and call the lesson AI-assisted.

## 9. Conversation in asynchronous learning

Every level should provide meaningful speaking/interaction even when no human partner is available.

Possible mechanisms:

- AI roleplay;
- recorded voice response;
- branching dialogue;
- listen-and-respond task;
- simulated service/problem interaction;
- asynchronous peer exchange where available.

Teacher-supported plans add human conversation; they do not rescue a curriculum that otherwise contains no speaking.

## 10. Create & Apply in asynchronous learning

Independent learners need authentic production.

A Create & Apply task should define:

- purpose;
- audience;
- artifact/performance;
- minimum success criteria;
- allowed AI assistance;
- feedback source;
- revision opportunity.

Where human review is not available, use transparent AI/self-review criteria and avoid pretending that automated feedback has the same authority as expert human assessment.

## 11. Assessment integrity

Asynchronous assessment must distinguish:

- first attempt;
- success after hint;
- success after explanation;
- independent transfer;
- delayed retention.

Do not convert repeated clicking until correct into mastery.

## 12. Accessibility and device resilience

Curriculum should be usable on realistic learner devices and connections.

Design for:

- mobile-first interaction where possible;
- readable text and controls;
- captions/transcripts where pedagogically appropriate;
- audio replay control;
- alternatives for interaction barriers;
- low-bandwidth degradation where product capability allows;
- progress preservation across interruptions.

## 13. Teacher-supported extension

Every unit should identify optional/high-value teacher extensions such as:

- live conversation;
- pronunciation coaching;
- writing conference;
- portfolio review;
- collaborative task;
- targeted remediation;
- level-exit validation.

The teacher extension should build on completed async evidence rather than repeat it.

## 14. Asynchronous QA checklist

A lesson cannot move to publish until review confirms:

- performance goal is clear;
- no teacher presence is required to understand basic instructions;
- passive content is broken by meaningful interaction;
- feedback and retry behavior are defined;
- productive competencies generate productive evidence;
- AI role is constrained and purposeful;
- return/resume behavior is coherent;
- important prerequisites are handled;
- retrieval is planned;
- transfer or application exists where appropriate;
- accessibility issues have been reviewed;
- teacher extension is additive rather than duplicative.

## 15. Definition of success

A high-quality Lurexa asynchronous lesson should feel like a responsive learning experience, not a digital worksheet or recorded lecture.

The learner should be able to move from supported exposure toward increasingly independent performance even when studying alone.
