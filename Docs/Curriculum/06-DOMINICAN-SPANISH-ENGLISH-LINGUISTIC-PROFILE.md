# Dominican Spanish → English Linguistic Profile

Status: MVP adaptation profile  
Purpose: guide English instruction for Spanish-speaking learners, optimized first for Dominican learners

## 1. Principle

This profile provides likely transfer patterns and culturally relevant design guidance. It must never be treated as a deterministic description of an individual learner. Actual learner evidence overrides group-level assumptions.

## 2. Goals

Use this profile to improve:

- phonetics instruction;
- listening support;
- grammar explanations;
- vocabulary/cognate guidance;
- AI feedback;
- Lurexa Coach targets;
- culturally relevant examples;
- teacher recommendations.

## 3. Phonetic priority areas

Common high-value areas to monitor include:

- English vowel contrasts not mirrored cleanly in Spanish, e.g. /ɪ/ vs /iː/;
- /θ/ and /ð/ perception/production;
- word-final consonant clarity;
- consonant clusters;
- aspiration and voicing contrasts where intelligibility is affected;
- word stress;
- sentence stress and reduced unstressed syllables;
- English rhythm and connected speech;
- linking and reductions at higher levels;
- intonation for questions, emphasis, politeness and stance.

Do not teach every possible contrast to every learner. Prioritize by level, communicative impact and observed evidence.

## 4. Dominican-specific listening/production consideration

Dominican Spanish may feature substantial reduction or weakening of certain consonants in natural speech. Lurexa should be sensitive to possible transfer into English final consonants and clusters, while avoiding simplistic claims that all Dominican learners produce the same patterns.

## 5. Grammar transfer areas to monitor

Potential areas include:

- explicit subject pronouns vs Spanish subject omission;
- auxiliary `do/does/did` in English questions/negatives;
- adjective position;
- article use;
- countability;
- prepositions;
- tense/aspect contrasts;
- third-person singular `-s`;
- plural/final morphology;
- question word order;
- `be` vs lexical verb structures;
- present perfect vs past-time choices at later levels.

## 6. Vocabulary transfer

Use cognates strategically but teach false friends and register differences where useful.

Avoid vocabulary instruction that depends on direct word-for-word translation. Encourage chunks, collocations and context.

## 7. Pragmatic adaptation

Teach communicative differences explicitly where they matter, including:

- polite requests;
- disagreement;
- turn-taking;
- clarification;
- small talk;
- professional directness/indirectness;
- email/message register;
- academic argumentation.

Do not frame Dominican conversational style as deficient. Teach context-sensitive alternatives.

## 8. Cultural context progression

Use culturally recognizable situations such as:

- family/community life;
- Santo Domingo and regional travel;
- Dominican food and cafés;
- public/private transportation;
- tourism and hospitality;
- music and popular culture;
- education and work;
- baseball/sports where appropriate;
- Caribbean identity;
- migration and international communication.

Progress outward toward Caribbean, Latin American, international and global contexts.

## 9. Topic rule

Culture is a context, not the competency itself. A lesson on dembow may teach argumentation, identity language, rhetorical stance, listening to natural speech, rhythm/stress, or cultural analysis.

## 10. AI feedback rule

Feedback can use L1-aware explanations when useful, for example:

> Spanish speakers may be tempted to omit the subject here, but English normally needs it: “She works,” not “Works.”

Keep explanations concise and learner-specific. Avoid repeatedly labeling the learner by nationality or language background when it adds no value.

## 11. Coach integration

Coach should be able to use relevant authorized evidence from Learn and vice versa. A pronunciation target identified in Learn can become a Coach practice target; confirmed improvement in Coach can reduce unnecessary remediation in Learn.

## 12. Level adaptation

A1-A2: prioritize intelligibility, survival phrases, sound perception, final consonants, high-frequency grammar transfer and basic pragmatic repair.  
B1-B2: prioritize connected speech, fluency, discourse, spontaneous grammar control, pragmatic interaction and professional/social register.  
C1-C2: prioritize nuance, rhetorical delivery, register, idiomatic precision, advanced listening variation and culturally sophisticated discourse.

## 13. Extensibility

This file is the first L1 adaptation profile, not a permanent platform constraint. Future profiles may include other Spanish varieties and non-Spanish L1s without redesigning the core methodology.
