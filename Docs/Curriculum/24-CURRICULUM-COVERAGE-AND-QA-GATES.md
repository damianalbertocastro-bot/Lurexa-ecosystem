# Curriculum Coverage and QA Gates

Status: Authoritative curriculum quality-control standard
Applies to: Lurexa Learn and Lurexa Teach curriculum releases

## 1. Purpose

A level, module, unit, or professional-development stage is not complete because all planned files exist. It is complete only when coverage, evidence quality, async completeness, AI behavior, accessibility, human-support design and integrated capstone evidence meet explicit release standards.

## 2. QA workflow

Every substantial curriculum object progresses through:

**Draft → Pedagogical Review → Linguistic/Content Review → Assessment Review → Async/AI Review → Accessibility/UX Review → Pilot → Evidence Review → Publish → Monitor**

A later stage may send work backward.

## 3. Required coverage dimensions

### Competency coverage

For each required competency record:

- where first encountered;
- where practiced;
- where independently demonstrated;
- where retrieved later;
- where transfer is expected;
- where it is sampled in the integrated capstone when appropriate;
- evidence types available;
- whether human validation is useful/required.

### Skill balance

English levels must audit listening, speaking, reading, writing, vocabulary, grammar, phonetics, conversation, Create & Apply, mediation, online interaction and plurilingual/pluricultural competence.

Balance does not mean equal minutes. It means each required capability receives enough valid opportunities for its role at that CEFR level.

### Evidence quality

Audit whether evidence includes appropriate mixtures of recognition, retrieval, guided production, independent production, interaction, transfer, delayed retrieval and human observation where useful.

No productive competency can rely exclusively on selected-response evidence.

### Integrated capstone quality

Every learner level A1-C2 and teacher-development stage T1-T5 must have a final integrated capstone governed by `42-INTEGRATED-LEVEL-AND-STAGE-CAPSTONE-ASSESSMENT-STANDARD.md`.

Check that the capstone:

- samples competencies from across the level/stage rather than the final module only;
- requires coherent productive performance;
- includes transfer to at least one varied/unfamiliar context;
- includes delayed retrieval from earlier learning;
- preserves first-attempt vs supported/revised evidence;
- cannot be passed while a critical productive competence lacks valid evidence;
- records AI/human assistance provenance;
- supports targeted revalidation instead of forcing complete program repetition;
- has representative pilot/calibration evidence before level/stage release.

### Async completeness

Check that required learning can proceed without waiting for a live teacher, instructions are sufficient, help exists inside the experience, activities resume after interruption, and teacher support extends rather than repairs an incomplete digital curriculum.

### AI quality

Check task/level constraints, evidence-aware scaffolding, productive struggle, no premature answer reveal, selective correction, learner agency, safe escalation, uncertainty/provenance and no fabricated standards.

AI must not complete final learner/teacher capstone evidence on the person's behalf.

### Teacher support quality

Check that teacher briefs exist where useful, suggested live work is evidence-driven, teachers can override recommendations, observations return to the learner/professional path and routine administration is minimized.

### Expert educator/mentor quality

Check that escalation criteria are clear, expert support is reserved for higher-value cases, recurring resolutions become reusable institutional knowledge and expert support increases independence rather than dependency.

### Retrieval and retention

Every major competency must have planned reappearance after initial instruction when retention matters. Major level/stage competencies should also be sampled through the final capstone or a documented equivalent exit-evidence route.

### Cultural quality

Audit local relevance, Dominican/Spanish-speaker specialization where useful, progression toward broader international/global contexts, avoidance of stereotypes, exposure to variation and learner-specific evidence overriding assumptions.

### Accessibility/UX

Audit alternative modalities where required, captions/transcripts, keyboard interaction, readable instruction load, no pedagogically unnecessary time pressure, safe resume state and accessibility adaptation without silently lowering the target.

Capstone accessibility must preserve the competency being assessed; accommodation changes access conditions, not the required competence.

## 4. Module release gate

A Learn module can move from pilot to publish when:

1. every unit has a performance goal;
2. required competencies have mapped evidence opportunities;
3. major productive targets include productive evidence;
4. async path is complete;
5. AI behavior is specified;
6. feedback/retry policy is specified;
7. at least one retrieval mechanism exists;
8. Create & Apply/transfer is present;
9. teacher extension is defined where useful;
10. accessibility/resume requirements are met;
11. pilot results reveal no unresolved critical defect.

## 5. Level release gate

A CEFR level can be represented as curriculum-complete when:

- all modules pass module gate;
- level competency matrix is complete;
- coverage matrix contains no major unexplained gaps;
- phonetics progression is coherent;
- conversation progression is coherent;
- mediation/online/pluricultural integration is present;
- delayed retrieval exists across modules;
- an integrated level capstone is implemented and calibrated;
- the capstone samples the major level competencies across multiple evidence modes;
- critical productive competencies cannot be bypassed;
- targeted revalidation works for incomplete exit evidence;
- teacher-supported and Flex routes preserve the same standards;
- representative pilot/calibration evidence supports both the curriculum and the level-exit decision.

## 6. Lurexa Teach release gate

A teacher-development module can publish when:

- target `TCH.*` competencies are explicit;
- knowledge and performance evidence are distinguished;
- async path can stand alone;
- AI simulation/rehearsal has bounded behavior;
- authentic/simulated professional performance is required where competency demands it;
- mentor/expert escalation is defined where appropriate;
- advancement is not awarded from completion alone.

A Teach **stage** T1-T5 can be represented as complete only when its integrated stage capstone is implemented/calibrated, professional evidence covers the major stage competencies, any required mentor/expert review is available, and critical professional capability gaps cannot be hidden by a composite score.

## 7. Severity model

### Critical

Invalidates release:

- false mastery inference;
- learner can bypass required productive performance;
- AI routinely performs learner/teacher task;
- required async path depends on hidden teacher instruction;
- evidence/provenance cannot be trusted;
- serious accessibility blocker;
- level/stage can be awarded without valid integrated capstone evidence;
- capstone can pass while a critical competency has no valid evidence.

### Major

Release should normally wait:

- competency coverage gap;
- poor retrieval architecture;
- repeated AI level-control problem;
- teacher support duplicates async work;
- assessment underrepresents target performance;
- capstone over-samples one skill/domain while materially underrepresenting another required area;
- capstone decision rules are not calibrated.

### Minor

Can publish with tracked follow-up if risk is limited:

- wording inconsistency;
- noncritical example imbalance;
- small UX friction;
- optional enrichment gap.

## 8. Monitoring after publication

Monitor first-attempt vs scaffolded success, dropout/failure hotspots, AI hint/reveal rates, competency evidence gaps, retrieval decay, teacher override patterns, expert-escalation recurrence, accessibility feedback, recurring learner misconceptions, recurring teacher confusion, capstone readiness patterns, capstone revalidation rates and disagreement between capstone decisions and later performance.

Repeated patterns should trigger curriculum review rather than endless learner-level remediation.

## 9. Definition of quality

High-quality Lurexa curriculum produces valid, traceable evidence of improved independent performance while allowing learners and teachers to progress asynchronously, benefit from AI, receive meaningful human support when available and demonstrate integrated competence in a final level/stage capstone without making any single assessment or expert interaction an opaque bottleneck.
