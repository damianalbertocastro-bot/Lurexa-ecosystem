# Lurexa Teach Professional Development Program

Status: Authoritative teacher-development macro-curriculum  
Owner: Lurexa Learning Technologies  
Primary audience: English-language teachers and aspiring teachers  
Initial context: Spanish-speaking teachers and teachers serving Spanish-speaking learners, with first-market relevance to the Dominican Republic

## 1. Purpose

Lurexa Teach should not be only a teacher dashboard. It should become a professional learning system that helps teachers diagnose their current capabilities, improve their own English where needed, strengthen pedagogical knowledge, improve classroom performance, learn to use evidence and AI responsibly, and progress toward higher levels of professional autonomy and leadership.

Teacher development must remain separate from learner English proficiency.

A teacher can have strong English and weak pedagogy. Another teacher can be pedagogically strong while needing language development. Lurexa must represent both accurately.

## 2. Governing principle

**One teacher. One evolving professional profile. Every development experience builds on demonstrated capability.**

Lurexa Teach should remember professional evidence such as:

- diagnostic results;
- language proficiency evidence;
- lesson plans;
- microteaching;
- classroom observations;
- feedback quality;
- assessment design;
- learner-outcome patterns;
- reflective practice;
- completed professional projects;
- peer/mentor validation;
- leadership/training evidence.

## 3. Two-axis teacher profile

Every teacher profile should distinguish at least:

### Axis A — English language ability

Represented using CEFR-aligned evidence where appropriate:

- listening;
- speaking;
- reading;
- writing;
- vocabulary/grammar control;
- phonological/intelligibility control;
- professional classroom language.

### Axis B — Teaching capability

Represented through Lurexa Teach professional competencies.

Do not calculate one teacher score by simply averaging English and pedagogy.

## 4. Professional competency domains

Lurexa Teach uses nine core professional domains.

### TCH.LEARNER — Learning and the learner

Teacher understands language acquisition, learner differences, motivation, affect, developmental errors, prior knowledge, transfer, scaffolding and learner autonomy.

### TCH.DESIGN — Curriculum and learning design

Teacher can translate standards/competencies into coherent courses, modules, units, lessons, tasks and retrieval cycles.

### TCH.INSTRUCT — Teaching language systems and skills

Teacher can teach listening, speaking, reading, writing, vocabulary, grammar and phonetics through communicative and evidence-producing learning experiences.

### TCH.INTERACT — Classroom interaction and facilitation

Teacher can manage participation, questioning, wait time, grouping, discussion, feedback, learner talk time, differentiation and classroom climate.

### TCH.ASSESS — Assessment and feedback

Teacher can design valid formative/summative assessment, interpret evidence, use rubrics, diagnose gaps and provide actionable feedback.

### TCH.LANGUAGE — Language knowledge and classroom language

Teacher understands language form, meaning, use, discourse, pronunciation, pragmatics and the language demands of the levels being taught.

### TCH.INCLUSIVE — Inclusive, intercultural and learner-responsive practice

Teacher can respond to learner diversity, accessibility, culture, linguistic background, identity and different learning needs without stereotyping.

### TCH.DIGITAL_AI — Digital pedagogy and responsible AI

Teacher can use LMS tools, asynchronous learning, multimedia, online interaction, AI tutoring, AI-assisted planning and evidence dashboards responsibly while preserving teacher judgment and learner agency.

### TCH.PRO — Reflection, ethics and professional growth

Teacher can reflect critically, use observation evidence, maintain professional standards, set development goals, collaborate, mentor and contribute to professional communities.

## 5. Development stages

Lurexa uses five professional stages. These are Lurexa stages, not claims of equivalence to external certifications.

### T1 — Emerging Educator

Profile: aspiring teacher, new teacher, or teacher with fragmented foundational knowledge.

Can:

- identify basic learner needs;
- write simple learner-centered objectives;
- plan a coherent lesson with support;
- use basic teaching routines;
- explain simple language targets;
- check basic learning;
- give supportive feedback;
- reflect on a lesson using prompts.

### T2 — Developing Practitioner

Profile: teacher who can run routine lessons but needs stronger consistency and pedagogical reasoning.

Can:

- adapt lessons to learner needs;
- teach core skills/systems with appropriate sequences;
- manage common classroom challenges;
- use formative evidence;
- support pronunciation and speaking development;
- distinguish productive from receptive mastery;
- integrate asynchronous/digital practice;
- make reasoned post-lesson adjustments.

### T3 — Proficient Practitioner

Profile: autonomous teacher with reliable instructional judgment across common contexts.

Can:

- design units around competencies;
- differentiate learning;
- facilitate sustained interaction;
- diagnose recurring learner patterns;
- design valid integrated assessment;
- use learner evidence to plan intervention;
- evaluate AI-generated materials critically;
- mentor less experienced teachers informally;
- justify instructional choices using evidence and principles.

### T4 — Advanced Practitioner

Profile: highly capable teacher able to handle complex learner, curriculum and institutional needs.

Can:

- design multi-level or program-level curriculum;
- lead assessment calibration;
- coach teachers;
- interpret learning analytics responsibly;
- design inclusive interventions;
- lead action research;
- integrate human and AI support strategically;
- improve programs based on evidence.

### T5 — Teacher Leader / Educator

Profile: teacher trainer, curriculum leader, academic coordinator or expert practitioner.

Can:

- design teacher-development systems;
- observe and coach teachers reliably;
- facilitate professional learning communities;
- lead curriculum/assessment quality assurance;
- design and evaluate pilots;
- mentor trainers;
- make evidence-based program decisions;
- contribute original professional knowledge or validated practice.

## 6. Placement and diagnostic model

Teacher onboarding should create a **professional profile**, not simply assign a course level.

Recommended evidence:

1. teacher background and goals;
2. English-language diagnostic if needed;
3. professional knowledge scenarios;
4. lesson-planning task;
5. language-awareness task;
6. assessment/feedback task;
7. recorded microteaching or classroom sample where available;
8. self-evaluation;
9. mentor/observer evidence where available.

Output example:

```text
Professional stage estimate: T2
English: B2 overall
Strongest domains: classroom interaction, learner rapport
Priority domains: assessment, phonetics teaching, digital learning design
Recommended path: T2 Core → Assessment Essentials → Teaching Pronunciation → Asynchronous Design
Evidence confidence: medium
```

## 7. Program architecture

The teacher curriculum has a common professional core plus adaptive pathways.

### Pathway A — Core Teaching Practice

For T1-T3 development.

### Pathway B — English for English Teachers

For teachers whose own English limits classroom performance or career progression.

### Pathway C — Language Awareness and Pronunciation

Deepens grammar, vocabulary, discourse, pragmatics, phonetics and language explanation.

### Pathway D — Assessment and Learning Evidence

Builds assessment literacy, rubrics, diagnosis, feedback, mastery decisions and evidence interpretation.

### Pathway E — Digital, Asynchronous and AI-Enhanced Teaching

Builds LMS design, online interaction, multimedia instruction, adaptive practice and responsible AI use.

### Pathway F — Curriculum and Instructional Design

For teachers building courses, modules, lessons, materials and integrated performance tasks.

### Pathway G — Teacher Leadership and Training

For mentors, coordinators, trainers and curriculum leaders.

## 8. Core program — T1 Emerging Educator

### Module T1.1 — How Learning Happens

Core: prior knowledge, attention, practice, retrieval, motivation, affect, cognitive load, developmental error, transfer.

Performance task: analyze a learner case and propose appropriate support.

### Module T1.2 — Planning Lessons With a Purpose

Core: communicative outcomes, SMART/observable objectives, sequencing, pacing, scaffolding and evidence.

Performance task: create and teach a short lesson segment.

### Module T1.3 — Teaching Vocabulary and Grammar for Use

Core: meaning/form/use, context, noticing, controlled-to-communicative practice, retrieval.

Performance task: redesign a worksheet-style lesson into an interactive sequence.

### Module T1.4 — Teaching Listening and Reading

Core: pre/during/post processing, global vs detailed understanding, strategies, text difficulty.

Performance task: create a staged receptive-skills lesson.

### Module T1.5 — Teaching Speaking and Writing

Core: controlled → guided → independent production, task design, feedback and revision.

Performance task: facilitate a speaking task and assess task achievement.

### Module T1.6 — Classroom Interaction and Feedback

Core: teacher talk, questioning, participation, checking understanding, correction choices, supportive climate.

Performance task: microteaching + reflection.

### T1 capstone — The First Coherent Lesson

Teacher designs, teaches, assesses and reflects on one complete learner-centered lesson.

## 9. Core program — T2 Developing Practitioner

### Module T2.1 — Learner Differences and Differentiation

Needs, proficiency variation, confidence, pace, scaffolding, accessibility and grouping.

### Module T2.2 — Teaching Pronunciation for Intelligibility

Sound/spelling, segmentals, stress, rhythm, connected speech, correction priorities, L1 transfer and accent-respectful pedagogy.

### Module T2.3 — Assessment for Learning

Formative assessment, evidence validity, first-attempt performance, rubrics, feedback and reteaching.

### Module T2.4 — Conversation and Communicative Tasks

Interaction design, information gaps, scenarios, roleplay, follow-up, fluency/accuracy balance.

### Module T2.5 — Asynchronous and Blended Learning

Designing learning that works without constant teacher presence; interaction cycles; teacher-session extension rather than repetition.

### Module T2.6 — Reflective Teaching

Evidence-based reflection, lesson postmortems, learner work analysis and professional goal setting.

### T2 capstone — Improve a Learning Cycle

Teacher identifies a real instructional weakness, redesigns the sequence, implements it and analyzes evidence.

## 10. Core program — T3 Proficient Practitioner

### Module T3.1 — Competency-Based Unit Design

Map competencies, prerequisites, evidence and transfer across a coherent unit.

### Module T3.2 — Advanced Interaction and Facilitation

Sustained discussion, learner autonomy, strategic teacher intervention and inclusive participation.

### Module T3.3 — Diagnostic Teaching

Analyze error patterns, learner evidence, language transfer and misconceptions to target intervention.

### Module T3.4 — Integrated Assessment Design

Build performance tasks, analytic rubrics, moderation and level-appropriate mastery evidence.

### Module T3.5 — AI as a Teaching Partner

Prompting is not the objective. Teachers learn to evaluate AI outputs, constrain AI by curriculum, protect learner agency, use evidence responsibly and decide when human judgment is required.

### Module T3.6 — Curriculum Adaptation and Materials Evaluation

Evaluate materials for objective alignment, cognitive demand, representation, cultural quality, accessibility and evidence value.

### T3 capstone — Evidence-Driven Unit

Teacher designs and implements a multi-lesson unit, collects evidence, evaluates outcomes and revises the design.

## 11. Advanced program — T4 Advanced Practitioner

### Module T4.1 — Curriculum Architecture

Program coherence, progression, standards, spiraling, coverage and vertical alignment.

### Module T4.2 — Assessment Systems and Calibration

Reliability, validity, moderation, performance standards, calibration and responsible automated scoring.

### Module T4.3 — Coaching and Observation

Evidence-based observation, developmental feedback, difficult coaching conversations and follow-through.

### Module T4.4 — Learning Analytics and Intervention

Interpret trends without reducing learners to scores; detect patterns; design targeted support; evaluate intervention impact.

### Module T4.5 — Practitioner Inquiry and Action Research

Question → baseline → intervention → evidence → interpretation → revision → communication.

### Module T4.6 — Program Improvement With AI and Human Judgment

Design AI-supported systems while preserving curriculum authority, transparency and human accountability.

### T4 capstone — Program Improvement Project

Lead an evidence-based improvement initiative involving multiple learners or teachers.

## 12. Leadership program — T5 Teacher Leader / Educator

### Module T5.1 — Teacher Learning and Adult Development

How teachers learn, resistance, identity, motivation, transfer into practice and developmental support.

### Module T5.2 — Designing Professional Development

Needs analysis, competency frameworks, workshops, coaching cycles, communities of practice and impact evaluation.

### Module T5.3 — Training and Facilitation

Facilitation, modeling, rehearsal, feedback, differentiation and professional dialogue.

### Module T5.4 — Curriculum and Quality Leadership

Curriculum governance, review cycles, evidence standards, consistency and innovation control.

### Module T5.5 — Mentoring Teachers and Trainers

Mentor structures, observation calibration, feedback literacy and developing autonomy in others.

### Module T5.6 — Research, Innovation and Professional Contribution

Pilot design, evidence synthesis, responsible innovation and communicating professional knowledge.

### T5 capstone — Professional Learning System

Teacher leader designs, runs and evaluates a professional-development initiative for a defined group of educators.

## 13. English for English Teachers pathway

This pathway is adaptive and CEFR-aligned but profession-specific.

Priority capabilities include:

- accurate classroom instructions;
- explaining meaning and language patterns;
- modeling pronunciation intelligibly;
- asking productive questions;
- spontaneous classroom interaction;
- professional writing;
- parent/student communication where relevant;
- teacher collaboration;
- reading professional literature;
- presenting and facilitating.

A teacher who needs language development may use Lurexa Learn/Coach content plus profession-specific Teach activities. Evidence should remain connected through the authorized Learner/Professional Model without conflating student and teacher competency IDs.

## 14. Dominican-Spanish specialization

For English teachers working with Dominican Spanish speakers, specialized modules should include:

- Spanish-to-English sound/spelling transfer;
- vowel-system differences;
- final consonants and clusters;
- `-ed` and third-person/plural endings;
- question formation transfer;
- adjective/noun-order transfer;
- auxiliary omission;
- cognate and false-friend risks;
- rhythm/stress differences;
- confidence-preserving correction;
- distinguishing accent features from intelligibility problems.

Teachers should learn to use L1 knowledge diagnostically, not to assume every learner has the same errors.

## 15. Assessment model

Teacher mastery requires performance evidence.

Possible evidence:

- scenario decisions;
- lesson plans;
- annotated materials;
- assessment items;
- rubrics;
- feedback samples;
- language explanations;
- pronunciation demonstrations;
- microteaching;
- classroom video/audio;
- learner work analysis;
- reflection;
- action research;
- mentoring/training artifacts.

A multiple-choice quiz can support knowledge evidence but cannot establish teaching mastery by itself.

## 16. Professional competency states

Use the same educational logic as learner mastery where useful:

`NOT_SEEN → ENCOUNTERED → PRACTICED → DEMONSTRATED → MASTERED → RETAINED / NEEDS_REVALIDATION`

For professional competencies, `MASTERED` should normally require authentic or simulated teaching performance.

## 17. AI behavior in Lurexa Teach

AI may:

- analyze lesson plans;
- generate practice cases;
- roleplay learners;
- simulate classroom decisions;
- help teachers rehearse explanations;
- critique assessment items;
- identify possible evidence patterns;
- suggest resources and next steps;
- support reflection.

AI must not:

- declare a teacher competent from weak evidence;
- replace observation where performance matters;
- silently invent professional standards;
- encourage surveillance-heavy evaluation;
- overrule authorized human reviewers without transparent provenance.

## 18. Credentialing principle

Lurexa development stages are internal professional-development stages unless and until an external accreditation process validates a credential.

The product may issue completion badges or evidence-based Lurexa certificates, but marketing must distinguish internal recognition from CELTA, DELTA, state licensure, university qualifications or other external credentials.

## 19. External framework benchmarking

The Lurexa system is proprietary, but its coverage should be benchmarked against mature frameworks. The Cambridge English Teaching Framework is particularly useful because it distinguishes:

- learning and the learner;
- teaching, learning and assessment;
- language ability;
- language knowledge and awareness;
- professional development and values.

Lurexa extends this comparison with explicit curriculum design, inclusive/intercultural practice, digital/asynchronous pedagogy, responsible AI and evidence-driven professional growth.

## 20. Definition of success

A successful Lurexa Teach program does not produce teachers who merely complete professional-development modules.

It produces teachers who can demonstrate better planning, instruction, interaction, assessment, language awareness, learner responsiveness, reflection and professional judgment—and whose improved practice creates stronger learner performance.
