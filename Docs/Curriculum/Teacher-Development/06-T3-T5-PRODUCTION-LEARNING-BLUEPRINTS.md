# Lurexa Teach T3–T5 Production Learning Blueprints

Status: Implementation-ready professional-learning architecture  
Depends on: Teacher Development `00`–`05` and `19-EXPERT-EDUCATOR-MENTORSHIP-AND-ESCALATION.md`

## 1. Purpose

This document closes the production-blueprint gap above T2.

T3-T5 must develop progressively stronger professional judgment, curriculum design, assessment, coaching, leadership and evidence-based improvement. Advancement requires authentic or high-fidelity simulated performance.

## 2. Shared advanced professional-learning cycle

**Case/Problem → Evidence Review → Professional Reasoning → Design/Decision → AI Rehearsal/Simulation → Authentic Application → Observation/Feedback → Revision → Transfer → Revalidation → Institutional Learning**

The higher the stage, the less AI should supply the professional answer and the more it should challenge reasoning, simulate stakeholders and expose trade-offs.

# T3 — Proficient Practitioner

## T3 outcome

Teacher independently designs coherent competency-based units, facilitates sustained interaction, diagnoses learning evidence, designs integrated assessment, uses AI critically and adapts curriculum/materials responsibly.

## Module T3.1 — Competency-Based Unit Design

Performance outcome: design a multi-lesson unit whose objectives, competencies, prerequisites, activities, evidence, retrieval and transfer align.

Core competencies:

- `TCH.DESIGN.MAP_UNIT_COMPETENCIES`
- `TCH.DESIGN.ALIGN_EVIDENCE_ACROSS_UNIT`
- `TCH.DESIGN.PLAN_RETRIEVAL_TRANSFER`
- `TCH.DESIGN.SEQUENCE_SUPPORT_REDUCTION`

Learning experiences:

- audit a topic-driven unit;
- map performance goals;
- identify prerequisite knowledge;
- sequence encounters/practice/retrieval;
- map productive evidence;
- design Create & Apply;
- review accessibility and async completeness.

AI role:

- challenge missing alignment;
- simulate different learner evidence profiles;
- identify hidden teacher dependence;
- never write final unit as teacher evidence.

Evidence:

- competency map;
- unit sequence;
- evidence map;
- retrieval map;
- rationale;
- revision after feedback.

## Module T3.2 — Advanced Interaction and Facilitation

Performance outcome: facilitate sustained learner-centered interaction while adapting support and participation.

Focus:

- discussion structure;
- wait time;
- follow-up questioning;
- participation equity;
- repair;
- strategic intervention;
- learner autonomy;
- psychologically safe challenge.

Simulation cases:

- dominant participant;
- silent learner;
- mixed proficiency group;
- disagreement;
- task drift;
- repeated misunderstanding;
- learner overdependence on teacher.

Evidence:

- facilitation plan;
- simulated/live facilitation;
- interaction transcript/video analysis;
- revision.

## Module T3.3 — Diagnostic Teaching

Performance outcome: use evidence to distinguish isolated error, recurring pattern, prerequisite gap, transfer effect, task-design problem and confidence/strategy issue.

Core evidence activities:

- analyze first attempt vs scaffolded success;
- compare receptive/productive evidence;
- inspect recurring error sequences;
- identify what evidence is still missing;
- plan minimal targeted intervention;
- schedule re-check.

AI role:

Present plausible competing interpretations and require teacher to justify one using evidence.

Evidence:

- diagnostic case report;
- intervention hypothesis;
- intervention plan;
- follow-up evidence interpretation.

## Module T3.4 — Integrated Assessment Design

Performance outcome: create a valid integrated performance assessment and analytic scoring approach.

Focus:

- construct alignment;
- receptive/productive/interactive evidence;
- rubrics;
- task authenticity;
- reliability/calibration basics;
- reasonable accommodations;
- first attempt vs support;
- avoiding false precision.

Evidence:

- assessment blueprint;
- task;
- rubric;
- sample scoring;
- moderation comparison;
- revision.

## Module T3.5 — AI as a Teaching Partner

Performance outcome: use AI in ways that strengthen learner thinking and teacher judgment without outsourcing either.

Teacher must demonstrate ability to:

- constrain AI by objective/competency;
- evaluate generated content;
- detect hallucinated standards;
- preserve productive struggle;
- distinguish AI observation from authoritative evidence;
- protect privacy;
- decide when teacher judgment is required.

Evidence:

- AI-assisted workflow;
- output critique;
- revised prompt/constraint;
- human decision log;
- learner-agency analysis.

## Module T3.6 — Curriculum Adaptation and Materials Evaluation

Performance outcome: adapt materials while preserving competency/evidence intent.

Audit dimensions:

- objective alignment;
- cognitive demand;
- cultural representation;
- accessibility;
- language load;
- practice variety;
- evidence quality;
- transfer;
- AI suitability.

Evidence:

- materials audit;
- redesigned sequence;
- explanation of preserved vs changed elements;
- pilot evidence.

## T3 Capstone — Evidence-Driven Unit

Teacher designs, implements and revises a multi-lesson unit.

Required bundle:

1. learner/context analysis;
2. competency map;
3. lesson sequence;
4. async/digital layer where appropriate;
5. evidence map;
6. integrated assessment;
7. learner evidence sample;
8. intervention/adaptation decision;
9. revised unit;
10. professional reflection.

T3 advancement requires evidence of autonomous professional reasoning, not just technically correct templates.

# T4 — Advanced Practitioner

## T4 outcome

Teacher can improve programs, calibrate assessment, coach colleagues, interpret learning analytics responsibly, conduct practitioner inquiry and design human+AI support systems.

## Module T4.1 — Curriculum Architecture

Performance outcome: design or audit progression across modules/levels/programs.

Evidence:

- vertical alignment map;
- competency coverage map;
- redundancy/gap analysis;
- retrieval/spiral analysis;
- release recommendation;
- change-impact analysis.

AI role: surface possible gaps/patterns, not make final curriculum-governance decisions.

## Module T4.2 — Assessment Systems and Calibration

Performance outcome: improve consistency and validity across multiple assessors/tasks.

Focus:

- performance standards;
- benchmark samples;
- moderation;
- inter-rater disagreement;
- automated scoring limitations;
- validity/reliability trade-offs;
- revalidation.

Evidence:

- calibration set;
- scoring rationale;
- disagreement resolution;
- revised rubric/protocol.

## Module T4.3 — Coaching and Observation

Performance outcome: observe teaching, identify high-value development targets and conduct developmental coaching.

Cycle:

pre-conference → evidence target → observation → teacher self-analysis → evidence discussion → one/few priorities → rehearsal/modeling → action plan → follow-up evidence.

Evidence:

- observation notes separated from inference;
- coaching conversation;
- teacher action plan;
- follow-up evidence;
- coach reflection.

## Module T4.4 — Learning Analytics and Intervention

Performance outcome: interpret cohort/program patterns without reducing learners to scores.

Cases:

- strong completion/weak production;
- repeated retrieval decay;
- disproportionate intervention rates;
- activity hotspot;
- high hint dependence;
- teacher override patterns;
- accessibility dropout signal.

Evidence:

- analytic interpretation;
- competing explanations;
- intervention design;
- monitoring plan;
- ethical/risk analysis.

## Module T4.5 — Practitioner Inquiry and Action Research

Performance outcome: conduct disciplined small-scale inquiry.

Cycle:

question → baseline → intervention → evidence → interpretation → limitation → revision → communication.

Evidence:

- inquiry proposal;
- ethical/data plan;
- baseline;
- intervention artifact;
- analysis;
- limitations;
- practical conclusion.

## Module T4.6 — Program Improvement With AI and Human Judgment

Performance outcome: design a bounded AI-supported learning/teacher workflow with explicit human accountability.

Evidence:

- workflow map;
- trust boundary;
- AI role definition;
- escalation criteria;
- evidence/provenance plan;
- failure-mode analysis;
- pilot result.

## T4 Capstone — Program Improvement Project

Teacher leads an improvement initiative involving multiple learners/teachers or a program-level process.

Required evidence:

1. problem definition;
2. baseline evidence;
3. stakeholder analysis;
4. intervention design;
5. implementation evidence;
6. result analysis;
7. unintended-effect review;
8. revision/scale decision;
9. reusable institutional artifact.

Mentor/expert review is normally required for T4 capstone calibration.

# T5 — Teacher Leader / Educator

## T5 outcome

Teacher leader can design professional-learning systems, train/facilitate adults, mentor teachers/trainers, lead curriculum/quality governance and produce reusable professional knowledge.

## Module T5.1 — Teacher Learning and Adult Development

Performance outcome: design development support responsive to teacher stage, identity, motivation, resistance and transfer-to-practice needs.

Evidence:

- teacher-learning case analysis;
- development diagnosis;
- differentiated support plan;
- follow-up/transfer measure.

## Module T5.2 — Designing Professional Development

Performance outcome: design a coherent professional-learning intervention around demonstrated teacher needs.

Evidence:

- needs analysis;
- `TCH.*` competency targets;
- learning sequence;
- rehearsal/application design;
- impact measures;
- follow-up/coaching plan.

## Module T5.3 — Training and Facilitation

Performance outcome: facilitate professional learning that requires participant reasoning, rehearsal and application.

Evidence:

- facilitator plan;
- live/simulated facilitation;
- participant-response analysis;
- adaptation during facilitation;
- post-session transfer task.

## Module T5.4 — Curriculum and Quality Leadership

Performance outcome: govern curriculum change without sacrificing coherence or evidence standards.

Cases:

- pressure to launch before validation;
- inconsistent competency IDs;
- attractive content with weak evidence;
- local adaptation vs standards;
- AI-generated curriculum quality;
- stakeholder disagreement.

Evidence:

- governance decision memo;
- QA protocol;
- change/version plan;
- stakeholder rationale.

## Module T5.5 — Mentoring Teachers and Trainers

Performance outcome: build independence in another professional.

Evidence:

- mentoring goal;
- evidence review;
- mentor dialogue;
- mentee action;
- follow-up evidence;
- autonomy/transfer analysis.

Success is not mentee dependence on the mentor.

## Module T5.6 — Research, Innovation and Professional Contribution

Performance outcome: design/evaluate innovation and communicate responsible professional knowledge.

Evidence:

- problem/research question;
- evidence review;
- pilot/innovation design;
- findings;
- limitations;
- professional artifact/publication/workshop;
- recommendation for adoption/non-adoption.

## T5 Capstone — Professional Learning System

Teacher leader designs, runs and evaluates a professional-development system for a defined group.

Required components:

1. population/needs analysis;
2. professional competency map;
3. diagnostic model;
4. learning/rehearsal sequence;
5. mentor/coaching layer;
6. AI role and constraints;
7. evidence/advancement policy;
8. quality/calibration process;
9. pilot data;
10. improvement cycle;
11. reusable institutional resources.

T5 is not conferred from content completion. It requires evidence of leadership impact, professional judgment and the ability to develop capability in others.

# 3. T3–T5 mentor/expert architecture

As stages rise, expert involvement changes:

### T3

Expert support is occasional and focused on ambiguous assessment/diagnostic/curriculum cases.

### T4

Expert/mentor calibration becomes a normal part of advanced coaching, assessment and program-improvement evidence.

### T5

Teacher leader increasingly participates as a peer contributor to institutional knowledge while still using calibration for high-stakes or novel work.

## 4. AI simulation architecture by stage

### T3

AI simulates learners, evidence cases and instructional constraints.

### T4

AI simulates teachers, stakeholders, cohort patterns, assessment disagreements and program trade-offs.

### T5

AI simulates organizational systems, trainer/mentee interactions, leadership dilemmas and implementation scenarios.

AI should become less directive as professional stage rises.

## 5. Professional revalidation

Advanced capability can decay or become outdated.

Revalidation may be triggered by:

- long inactivity;
- major role change;
- new curriculum/assessment responsibility;
- repeated observation concerns;
- substantial technology/policy change;
- teacher self-request.

Revalidation should target relevant competencies rather than force full-stage repetition.

## 6. Release gate for complete T1–T5 Teach program

Lurexa Teach may claim full internal T1–T5 curriculum coverage only when:

- every stage has competency-mapped production blueprints;
- advancement evidence is explicit;
- simulated vs authentic evidence is distinguished;
- mentor/expert requirements are defined;
- capstones are calibrated;
- teacher-language proficiency remains a separate axis;
- representative pilots exist across at least T1, T2/T3 and advanced leadership stages;
- internal stage claims are clearly distinguished from external qualifications/licensure.
