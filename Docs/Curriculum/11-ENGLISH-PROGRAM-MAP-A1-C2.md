# Lurexa Learn English Program Map — A1 to C2

Status: Authoritative macro-curriculum map  
Framework: CEFR A1-C2 with CEFR Companion Volume integration  
Audience: global English learners; first deep linguistic profile for Dominican Spanish speakers

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the complete macro-program for Lurexa Learn English from A1 to C2. It does not replace level competency matrices or lesson blueprints. It establishes the destination each detailed curriculum layer must implement.

The program develops seven explicit skills:

- Listening
- Speaking
- Reading
- Writing
- Vocabulary
- Grammar
- Phonetics

Integrated performance modes:

- Conversation/interaction
- Create & Apply
- Mediation
- Online interaction
- Plurilingual/pluricultural competence

The program follows the Lurexa cycle:

**Encounter → Understand → Practice → Retrieve → Apply → Create → Receive Feedback → Improve → Transfer → Retain**

# A1 — Foundations for Real Communication

## Level mission

Build enough language, listening confidence, pronunciation control, and interaction strategies to manage basic personal and familiar everyday situations with substantial support.

## Module 1 — Hello, This Is Me

Performance focus: greet people, introduce oneself and others, exchange basic personal information, ask for repetition and clarification.

Core language: subject pronouns, `be`, basic questions, countries/nationalities, occupations, alphabet/spelling, core politeness.

Phonetics: sound-spelling awareness, alphabet names, high-value vowel/consonant contrasts, final consonant awareness, word stress.

Create & Apply: personal introduction recording and short introduction exchange.

## Module 2 — My World in Numbers

Performance focus: exchange phone numbers, dates, ages, prices, times, schedules and simple availability.

Core language: numbers, dates, clock time, calendar language, basic prepositions of time, `be` questions, possessives.

Phonetics: teen/ty contrasts, number clarity, stress in dates/time expressions.

Create & Apply: personal weekly schedule and appointment exchange.

## Module 3 — The People Around Me

Performance focus: identify, describe and talk simply about family, friends and familiar people.

Core language: family vocabulary, possessive adjectives, basic descriptive adjectives, `have`, simple adjective placement.

Phonetics: plural endings, final consonants, basic clusters, stress in descriptive phrases.

Create & Apply: family/person profile.

## Module 4 — My Everyday Life

Performance focus: describe routines, ask about daily activities and understand basic schedules.

Core language: present simple, do/does questions, frequency expressions, sequencing, common daily verbs.

Phonetics: third-person `-s`, sentence stress, basic rhythm, common reductions in high-frequency phrases.

Create & Apply: daily routine recording and schedule comparison.

## Module 5 — Food, Flavor and Everyday Choices

Performance focus: identify foods, express basic wants/preferences, order food, ask about prices and quantities.

Core language: count/non-count foundations, articles, some/any, `would like`, basic quantity language.

Phonetics: food-vocabulary contrasts relevant to learner profile, polite intonation, phrase stress.

Create & Apply: café/restaurant roleplay.

## Module 6 — My Neighborhood

Performance focus: locate places, ask/give simple directions, describe where things are and discuss basic transportation.

Core language: there is/are, prepositions of place, imperatives, place vocabulary, transport chunks.

Phonetics: direction phrases, consonant clusters, sentence stress for key information.

Create & Apply: neighborhood map explanation.

## Module 7 — What I Like

Performance focus: express simple preferences, hobbies and interests; make and respond to basic invitations.

Core language: like/love/don't like, can/can't for basic ability, simple invitation chunks, leisure vocabulary.

Phonetics: can/can't perception and production, contrastive stress, basic intonation for invitations.

Create & Apply: social-plan exchange.

## Module 8 — Life in Action

Performance focus: integrate A1 language in practical scenarios with decreasing support.

Scenarios: meeting someone, arranging a time, introducing a person, describing a routine, ordering, navigating a neighborhood, making a simple plan.

Capstone: **My Life, My English** portfolio.

### A1 exit evidence

Learner can produce and understand highly familiar language, maintain short supported exchanges, recover from simple communication breakdowns, and complete practical personal tasks with predictable language.

# A2 — Everyday Independence

## Level mission

Move from formulaic survival language toward routine independent communication across everyday social, service, travel, study and work-adjacent situations.

## Module 1 — Stories From Everyday Life

Performance focus: describe recent events and simple past experiences.

Language: past simple, regular/irregular verbs, time markers, sequencing.

Phonetics: regular past `-ed`, high-frequency irregular forms, connected storytelling chunks.

Create & Apply: short personal story.

## Module 2 — Around Town and On the Move

Performance focus: navigate transportation, ask for travel information, solve routine movement problems.

Language: transport vocabulary, comparative distance/time, directions review, future arrangements with present continuous.

Create & Apply: local travel plan.

## Module 3 — Home, Community and Services

Performance focus: describe home/community, handle simple service interactions and explain basic problems.

Language: comparatives, quantifiers, there was/were, requests, household/community vocabulary.

Create & Apply: community recommendation guide.

## Module 4 — Health and Well-Being

Performance focus: describe symptoms, routines and simple health needs; understand common advice.

Language: should/shouldn't, have to, body/health vocabulary, frequency and habits.

Create & Apply: well-being plan or pharmacy/clinic simulation.

## Module 5 — Work, Study and Responsibilities

Performance focus: describe duties, abilities, routines, simple goals and workplace/study needs.

Language: can/could, have to, need to, present/past review, basic future plans.

Online interaction: simple professional messages, scheduling and clarification.

Create & Apply: short profile plus task-planning exchange.

## Module 6 — Travel and Experiences

Performance focus: discuss trips, preferences, experiences and simple comparisons.

Language: present perfect introduction for experiences, past simple contrast, travel vocabulary, comparatives/superlatives.

Mediation: relay simple travel information from a notice, message or itinerary.

Create & Apply: weekend/travel itinerary.

## Module 7 — Entertainment, Culture and Social Life

Performance focus: describe and recommend media, music, events and leisure experiences.

Language: opinion chunks, adjectives, simple reasons, relative clause introduction where useful.

Pluricultural focus: compare familiar cultural practices without stereotyping.

Create & Apply: recommendation video/audio.

## Module 8 — Everyday Independence Challenge

Performance focus: integrate A2 across service, travel, social, work/study and digital situations.

Capstone: **Living More Independently in English**.

### A2 exit evidence

Learner can manage routine interactions, describe past and present experiences, make basic plans, explain simple needs/problems, exchange practical information online and mediate straightforward information with support.

# B1 — Independent Communication

## Level mission

Develop connected discourse, sustained interaction, narration, opinion, functional independence and the ability to manage familiar unexpected situations.

## Module 1 — Experiences That Shape Us

Performance focus: narrate experiences, sequence events, describe reactions and explain significance.

Language: past simple/continuous, present perfect development, narrative linkers.

Phonetics: rhythm in connected narration, weak forms, past endings in spontaneous speech.

Create & Apply: personal narrative podcast.

## Module 2 — Relationships and Communication

Performance focus: discuss relationships, misunderstandings, expectations and communication choices.

Language: modals for advice/possibility, reported speech introduction, functional language for disagreement/repair.

Conversation: clarification, follow-up questions, turn management.

Create & Apply: advice discussion or scenario analysis.

## Module 3 — Work, Goals and Professional Growth

Performance focus: discuss experience, goals, responsibilities, strengths and workplace situations.

Language: present perfect/past contrasts, future forms, obligation, conditionals introduction.

Online interaction: email/chat tone, scheduling, updates, requests.

Create & Apply: interview + professional action plan.

## Module 4 — Media, Information and Everyday Claims

Performance focus: summarize accessible information, distinguish opinion/evidence and respond to claims.

Language: reporting verbs, opinion/hedging basics, discourse markers.

Mediation: summarize or relay key ideas from a short text/audio.

Create & Apply: short fact-check/explainer.

## Module 5 — Travel, Culture and Intercultural Encounters

Performance focus: describe cultural experiences, explain norms, compare perspectives and manage misunderstandings.

Language: comparatives, modals, narrative/reaction language, relative clauses.

Pluricultural focus: perspective-taking and respectful comparison.

Create & Apply: intercultural guide or travel reflection.

## Module 6 — Problems, Choices and Solutions

Performance focus: explain a problem, compare options, negotiate simple solutions and justify choices.

Language: first/second conditionals, modals, cause/effect, recommendation language.

Create & Apply: problem-solving meeting.

## Module 7 — Ideas, Opinions and Participation

Performance focus: express and support opinions, respond to others and sustain structured discussion.

Language: stance, reasons/examples, contrast/concession, discourse markers.

Phonetics: contrastive stress, chunking and fluency.

Create & Apply: moderated discussion or opinion presentation.

## Module 8 — Independent Communication Project

Performance focus: integrate narration, explanation, interaction, mediation and digital communication.

Capstone: **My Voice in English** — portfolio, conversation, writing and applied project.

### B1 exit evidence

Learner can sustain familiar conversations, produce connected speech/writing, explain experiences and opinions, solve common problems, summarize accessible information and participate independently in familiar digital and intercultural contexts.

# B2 — Flexible Communication

## Level mission

Build spontaneous, flexible communication across personal, academic, professional and public contexts with stronger argumentation, authentic input tolerance and discourse control.

## Module 1 — Identity, Society and Perspective

Performance focus: discuss identity, social expectations and multiple perspectives.

Language: stance, hedging, complex comparison, discourse organization.

Pluricultural competence: perspective interpretation and avoiding simplistic cultural generalization.

## Module 2 — Communication, Influence and Media

Performance focus: analyze messaging, persuasion, bias and audience.

Language: reporting structures, passive voice, evaluative vocabulary, rhetorical devices.

Mediation: synthesize key points across two accessible sources.

Create & Apply: media analysis or campaign critique.

## Module 3 — Professional Communication and Collaboration

Performance focus: negotiate, propose, clarify, disagree constructively and present recommendations.

Language: formal/informal register, conditionals, modals, diplomatic language.

Online interaction: collaborative documents, asynchronous discussion, concise professional updates.

Create & Apply: proposal + negotiation.

## Module 4 — Science, Technology and Change

Performance focus: explain complex-but-accessible concepts, discuss implications and evaluate options.

Language: cause/effect, qualification, relative/participle clauses, technical vocabulary strategies.

Mediation: explain specialist information to a non-specialist audience.

## Module 5 — Culture, Creativity and Interpretation

Performance focus: interpret artistic/cultural products and defend interpretations.

Language: evaluative language, metaphor awareness, nuanced comparison.

Create & Apply: cultural review/panel.

## Module 6 — Global Challenges and Local Responses

Performance focus: analyze issues, weigh evidence, compare solutions and discuss trade-offs.

Language: argumentation, concession, probability, complex conditionals.

Create & Apply: structured debate or policy proposal.

## Module 7 — Academic and Information Literacy

Performance focus: understand extended texts, synthesize, paraphrase, cite ideas appropriately and build structured arguments.

Language: academic discourse markers, paraphrase strategies, nominalization awareness.

Mediation: synthesize multiple sources for a defined audience.

## Module 8 — Flexible Communication Capstone

Capstone: **From Opinion to Impact** — extended presentation, interactive defense, written artifact and mediation task.

### B2 exit evidence

Learner communicates spontaneously with good control, understands extended authentic/semi-authentic input, participates in professional/academic interaction, builds supported arguments, adapts register and mediates complex-but-accessible information.

# C1 — Precision, Identity and Global Perspective

## Level mission

Develop nuanced, precise and strategically flexible communication across complex professional, academic, cultural and public contexts.

## Module 1 — Caribbean Heritage, Language and Identity

Performance focus: analyze relationships among language, culture, history and identity.

Language: nuance, evaluation, rhetorical organization, metaphor and stance.

Pluricultural competence: multiple identities, varieties and perspectives.

## Module 2 — Power, Language and Public Discourse

Performance focus: analyze framing, rhetoric, implication, politeness and power.

Language: pragmatic nuance, hedging/boosting, rhetorical patterns, register.

Create & Apply: discourse analysis or public commentary.

## Module 3 — Leadership, Collaboration and Difficult Conversations

Performance focus: facilitate discussion, negotiate complexity, reframe disagreement and build shared understanding.

Mediation: mediate concepts and communication among participants with different perspectives.

Phonetics: pragmatic intonation, pacing, emphasis and professional delivery.

## Module 4 — Knowledge, Research and Evidence

Performance focus: synthesize complex sources, evaluate claims, communicate uncertainty and present disciplined analysis.

Language: synthesis, citation discourse, qualification, complex clause control.

Create & Apply: research brief.

## Module 5 — Innovation, Ethics and Future Systems

Performance focus: discuss uncertain futures, evaluate ethical dilemmas and defend strategic choices.

Language: hypotheticality, nuanced modality, argument counterargument, abstraction.

## Module 6 — Culture, Creativity and Interpretation

Performance focus: interpret sophisticated cultural texts and construct layered analyses.

Language: figurative language, evaluative precision, intertextual discussion.

## Module 7 — Global Professional Communication

Performance focus: adapt spoken/written communication across audiences, domains and registers.

Online interaction: complex asynchronous collaboration, facilitation and audience-aware digital communication.

## Module 8 — Precision and Perspective Capstone

Capstone: **Language, Identity and Global Perspective** — research-informed artifact, presentation, discussion facilitation and mediation performance.

### C1 exit evidence

Learner communicates complex ideas fluently and precisely, adapts register strategically, synthesizes sources, mediates complex ideas and perspectives, and manages demanding professional/academic interaction with nuanced pragmatic control.

# C2 — Adaptive Mastery

## Level mission

Develop highly flexible, domain-sensitive language use characterized by synthesis, interpretive depth, register agility, strategic mediation and control across unfamiliar or high-complexity contexts.

## Module 1 — Language, Meaning and Subtlety

Performance focus: interpret implied meaning, humor, irony, ambiguity, connotation and stylistic effect.

## Module 2 — Advanced Rhetoric and Argument

Performance focus: construct, challenge and reshape sophisticated arguments for different audiences.

Create & Apply: keynote, editorial, advanced debate or rhetorical transformation.

## Module 3 — Expert Mediation and Facilitation

Performance focus: facilitate understanding among people, texts, perspectives or fields; compress, expand, reframe and translate complexity for audience need.

## Module 4 — Domains, Registers and Professional Agility

Performance focus: shift effectively among technical, academic, public, interpersonal and creative registers.

Online interaction: lead complex digital collaboration and respond strategically to multimodal communication.

## Module 5 — Culture, Literature and Interpretive Depth

Performance focus: engage critically with sophisticated literary/cultural discourse and competing interpretations.

## Module 6 — Systems, Complexity and Uncertainty

Performance focus: reason publicly about complex systems, incomplete evidence and competing values.

## Module 7 — Independent Mastery Project

Learner selects a domain, defines an authentic audience/purpose, researches, creates, presents, mediates and defends a substantial artifact.

## Module 8 — Adaptive Mastery Portfolio

Capstone: **English Without a Script** — multi-context performance demonstrating flexible comprehension, production, interaction, mediation, register control and self-directed improvement.

### C2 exit evidence

Learner can understand and produce sophisticated language across domains and varieties, infer subtle meaning, adapt register and rhetorical strategy, synthesize complex material, mediate expert-level ideas for different audiences and perform effectively in unfamiliar communicative conditions.

# Cross-level recurrence architecture

Every level must recycle earlier competencies at greater independence and in new contexts.

A target is not finished when its teaching unit ends. Competencies should reappear through:

1. near retrieval within the module;
2. delayed retrieval in later modules;
3. integrated conversation;
4. Create & Apply tasks;
5. placement/challenge evidence;
6. teacher observations;
7. Coach practice where relevant;
8. level-exit performance.

# Cultural architecture

Contexts should expand through:

**Personal → Local → Dominican → Caribbean → Latin American → International → Global/Abstract**

Dominican specificity is a first-market strength, not a ceiling. Learners should encounter multiple English varieties, cultures and communicative norms as proficiency increases.

# Commercial completion rule

A level is not complete because all module titles exist. Before release as a complete CEFR level, each module and unit must be backed by:

- explicit competencies;
- structured learning objects;
- valid evidence opportunities;
- assessment coverage;
- conversation;
- phonetics;
- Create & Apply;
- retrieval;
- mediation/online/pluricultural integration appropriate to level;
- accessibility review;
- pilot evidence and revision.
