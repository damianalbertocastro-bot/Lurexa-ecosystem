# Lurexa Teach — Canonical Product Definition

Status: **Canonical product definition**
Decision date: 2026-08-24
Owner: Lurexa Learning Technologies

## Canonical definition

**Lurexa Teach is Lurexa's professional learning and teacher-formation platform for practicing educators and teachers-to-be. It develops academic and subject knowledge, pedagogical methodology, professional competencies, and teaching practice through structured learning, AI-guided development, human support, evidence-based feedback, community, and progressive professional pathways.**

Lurexa Teach is not limited to continuing professional development for people who are already teachers. It intentionally serves two major educator populations:

1. **Practicing educators** who want to improve their academic, linguistic, pedagogical, technological, and professional capabilities.
2. **Teachers-to-be / aspiring educators** who need structured preparation in teaching methodology, subject knowledge, classroom practice, assessment, reflection, and professional competencies while progressing toward effective teaching practice.

## Product promise

Primary product line:

> **Learn to teach. Grow as an educator.**

Supporting positioning:

> **Professional learning and teacher formation, powered by intelligent and human support.**

The product should support the educator from preparation through continued professional growth rather than treating teacher education and teacher development as disconnected experiences.

## Primary pathways

### 1. Teacher Formation Pathway

For aspiring teachers and teachers-to-be.

May include:

- foundations of education and learning;
- teaching methodology and major pedagogical approaches;
- subject and academic knowledge development;
- lesson and unit planning;
- curriculum literacy;
- classroom management;
- assessment literacy and feedback;
- inclusive and accessible teaching;
- educational technology and responsible AI use;
- observation and analysis of teaching;
- simulated teaching and microteaching;
- practicum preparation and supported practice;
- reflective practice;
- professional ethics and responsibilities;
- evidence portfolios and competency development.

### 2. Practicing Educator Growth Pathway

For active teachers and educators.

May include:

- subject-matter and academic knowledge development;
- English/CEFR proficiency development where relevant;
- advanced methodology and pedagogy;
- assessment and feedback practice;
- classroom and learner-support strategies;
- curriculum and instructional design;
- inclusive education;
- educational technology and AI literacy;
- research-informed teaching practice;
- leadership, mentoring, and specialization pathways;
- professional evidence and reflection;
- credentials and continuing development.

The two pathways may share courses, competencies, evidence models, community, mentors, and AI capabilities while preserving pathway-appropriate expectations.

## Supported learning model

Teach must not feel like a passive self-study course catalog. Its differentiation is a supported professional-development and formation system combining:

- structured learning pathways;
- adaptive recommendations through Lurexa Mind;
- AI tutoring, coaching, rehearsal, and feedback where pedagogically appropriate;
- human instructors, mentors, reviewers, or coaches where the program requires them;
- peer learning and professional community;
- guided practice and simulations;
- feedback on lesson plans, teaching artifacts, reflections, demonstrations, and evidence;
- competency-based evidence;
- formative and summative assessment;
- reflective practice;
- professional credentials;
- persistent growth history and next-step recommendations.

AI complements rather than replaces human mentorship, expert review, supervised practice, or high-stakes professional judgment.

## Educator development context

Teach requires a persistent educator-development representation that may progressively include authorized information such as:

- educator stage: aspiring, pre-service, early-career, practicing, mentor, or other approved stage;
- academic/subject knowledge;
- language proficiency where relevant;
- methodology knowledge;
- demonstrated teaching competencies;
- strengths and development needs;
- professional goals;
- completed professional learning;
- evidence and review history;
- observation, rehearsal, or practicum evidence where governed;
- credentials;
- mentor/instructor feedback;
- recommended next steps.

Trusted educator records and authorization remain governed by **Lurexa Core**. **Lurexa Mind** may interpret authorized educator evidence and context to personalize learning and recommend development actions, but Mind does not independently grant credentials, verify high-stakes evidence, or own authoritative persistence.

## Product boundary with Lurexa Learn

The distinction is mandatory:

> **Lurexa Learn is where teachers operate and support student learning. Lurexa Teach is where practicing and future teachers learn, practice, develop, and grow as educators.**

Lurexa Learn owns classroom/student-learning operations such as classes, assignments, learner progress, instructional delivery, and teacher actions directly tied to operating a student learning experience.

Lurexa Teach owns educator formation and professional growth such as methodology learning, academic development, professional courses, practice, evidence, reflection, credentials, mentoring, and educator community.

A person may use both products under one Lurexa identity. The products may exchange authorized context through Core/Mind contracts, but they must not collapse into one teacher portal.

## Relationship with Lurexa Campus

An institution may provide Teach through **Lurexa Campus** as an entitled educator-development experience for faculty, trainees, or pre-service teachers.

Campus provides the institutional context and access; Teach remains responsible for the professional-learning and teacher-formation experience.

Examples include:

- a language academy developing its teaching staff;
- a university preparing teachers-to-be;
- a school system running professional-development pathways;
- a training provider delivering teacher certification or specialization programs.

## Human support model

Programs may combine different levels of human support:

- instructor-led cohorts;
- mentor assignment;
- office hours or coaching;
- evidence review;
- practicum supervision;
- peer feedback;
- expert assessment;
- human escalation after AI-supported practice.

The availability and required level of human support is a program/configuration concern and should be explicit to the learner.

## AI support model

Subject to Core authorization and Mind governance, AI capabilities may include:

- personalized learning recommendations;
- methodology tutoring;
- academic-concept explanation;
- lesson-plan critique;
- scenario and classroom simulation;
- microteaching rehearsal;
- reflective questioning;
- assessment-design feedback;
- language/proficiency coaching;
- evidence preparation support;
- practice feedback;
- professional-growth planning.

AI output must remain distinguishable from trusted human verification and must not fabricate professional competency or credential attainment.

## Professional evidence and credentials

Teach should progressively support evidence such as:

- lesson plans and unit plans;
- teaching demonstrations or simulations;
- reflection artifacts;
- assessment designs;
- course/project work;
- peer collaboration;
- mentor/reviewer observations;
- practicum evidence where institutionally authorized;
- subject/proficiency assessment evidence;
- professional portfolios.

Credential rules must distinguish participation, completion, demonstrated competency, verified evidence, and externally meaningful certification.

## Community

Teach Community is the educator-only professional collaboration space inside Lurexa Teach. It may support peer learning, professional circles, questions, resource exchange, feedback, study groups, and mentoring relationships.

Community engagement does not automatically become trusted professional evidence. Any evidence-bearing use requires an explicit governed evidence flow.

## MVP implications

The current Teach MVP foundation remains useful, but product development should progressively recognize both educator pathways.

Near-term additions should include:

1. educator-stage/pathway selection;
2. Teacher Formation and Practicing Educator pathway metadata;
3. methodology/academic competency taxonomy;
4. AI + human support model in course/program definitions;
5. teaching-practice/simulation evidence types;
6. mentor/reviewer roles and workflows where needed;
7. pathway-aware recommendations;
8. clear separation between completion and verified competency.

## Non-goals

Lurexa Teach must not become:

- the Lurexa Learn teacher operations dashboard;
- a generic course marketplace with no educator-development model;
- an AI-only replacement for teacher educators or mentors;
- an ungoverned credential generator;
- a separate identity or trust system outside Lurexa Core;
- a second Lurexa Mind implementation.

## Canonical shorthand

When a shorter definition is required, use:

> **Lurexa Teach is the professional learning and teacher-formation platform for practicing educators and teachers-to-be, combining academic and pedagogical development, teaching practice, AI-guided learning, human support, professional evidence, community, and progressive credentials.**

## Governing sentence

> **Learn to teach. Grow as an educator.**
